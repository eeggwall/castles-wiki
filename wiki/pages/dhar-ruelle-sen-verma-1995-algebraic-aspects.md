# Algebraic Aspects of Abelian Sandpile Models (Dhar, Ruelle, Sen, Verma, 1995)

**Source:** `raw/dhar-ruelle-sen-verma-1995-algebraic-aspects.pdf` (arXiv:cond-mat/9408022v1, 5 August 1994, 40 pages; the footnotes give the preprint's page numbers)
**Publication:** D. Dhar, P. Ruelle, S. Sen and D.-N. Verma, "Algebraic aspects of abelian sandpile models", Journal of Physics A 28 (1995) 805-831 (bibliographic data as cited in [[rossin-2000-group-of-a-sandpile](pages/rossin-2000-group-of-a-sandpile.md)], reference [9]).
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-27
**Type:** paper (preprint version read)

## Summary

The paper studies the finite abelian group `G` of an abelian sandpile model, generated by the operators `a_k` that add a grain at site `k` and let the pile relax, first for a general integer toppling matrix `Δ` and then for rectangles of the square lattice. It recalls the results of Dhar 1990 (its reference [6]): the `a_k` commute, only the recurrent configurations occur in the steady state, they occur with equal probability, and there are `det Δ` of them, so `|G| = |R| = det Δ`.[^1] It then builds **toppling invariants**, `Q_i = Σ_j (Δ⁻¹)_ij z_j mod 1`, which take the same value on configurations related by topplings and so label the recurrent configurations, though not independently. Writing `Δ = A D B` in Smith normal form, with `D = diag(d_1, …, d_N)` and each `d_i` a multiple of `d_{i+1}`, gives a minimal complete set `I_i = Σ_j (A⁻¹)_ij z_j mod d_i` and the decomposition `G = Z/d_1 × … × Z/d_g`, with `g` the number of `d_i > 1`. The invariants add under the group law (add sitewise, then relax), and the identity is the recurrent configuration with every invariant zero.[^2]

For an `L_1 × L_2` rectangle of the square lattice with the discrete Laplacian as toppling matrix (4 on the diagonal, `−1` between nearest neighbors), every boundary toppling loses sand, which is open boundary conditions on all four sides. Every addition operator is a product of powers of the `L_2` operators of one column, so the rank `g` of `G` satisfies `g ≤ L_2` (eq. 4.3). On the `L × L` square the rank is exactly `L` (eq. 4.7). The paper gives the idea of the proof: `Δ` has `L` linearly independent integer eigenvectors of eigenvalue 4, which give `L` independent operators `U_k` with `U_k⁴ = I` (eq. 4.8), so `g ≥ L`. The groups for `L = 2` to 5 are listed, starting with `Z/24 × Z/8`.[^3] The order `|G|` is the product of the eigenvalues `λ_{m,n} = 4 − 2cos(2πm/N) − 2cos(2πn/N)`, `1 ≤ m, n ≤ L`, `N = 2(L + 1)` (eqs. 5.1-5.3), and grouping them into orbits of the Galois group of `Q(cos 2π/N)` factors `|G|` maximally into integers; the paper counts the orbits and builds algebraic toppling invariants from eigenvectors, which it shows are neither independent nor complete.[^4] Appendix A solves the `L × 2` strip exactly.[^5]

Section 7 takes up two questions. Wiesenfeld et al. studied a deterministic sandpile that always adds its grain at the center of a `(2L + 1) × (2L + 1)` square; once past the transients it cycles with a period `T_L` independent of the starting pile, and they found `T_L = 4, 16, 104, 544, 146 248, 7 889 840` for `L = 0` to 5. The paper shows that `T_L` is the order of the central addition operator on the recurrent configurations, `T_L = (det Δ)/M` with `M` the gcd of the central column of `E = (det Δ)Δ⁻¹` (eqs. 7.1-7.3), and that `T_L` divides the largest invariant factor `d_1^{sym}` of the group of recurrent configurations with the symmetry of the square; equality is observed for `L = 1` to 4 and not proved.[^6] The second question is the identity configuration of the `L × L` square, which shows fractal structure. On `2L × 2L` lattices a central square of maximal height has side about `4L/5`, and the identity on `(2L + 1) × (2L + 1)` lattices is obtained from the one on `2L × 2L` by pulling its four quarters apart to leave a cross. The authors write that these structures "are not well understood yet".[^7]

## Key Takeaways

- **The algebra the sandpile pages use.** `|G| = det Δ` (the number of spanning trees, for a reduced Laplacian), the Smith normal form as the structure of `G`, and toppling invariants built from `Δ⁻¹` ([[sandpile-group](pages/sandpile-group.md)]).[^1][^2]
- **The grid is a castle's dual (the wiki's reading).** The `L × L` open grid, 4 on the diagonal and one sink edge per missing neighbor, is the sink-model block matrix of the `(L+1) × (L+1)` castle ([[sandpile-group](pages/sandpile-group.md)]). So `G(L × L)` is that castle's sink group: `Z/24 × Z/8` for `(3, 3, 3)`, `Z/224 × Z/112 × Z/4` for `(4, 4, 4, 4)`, `Z/6600 × Z/1320 × Z/8 × Z/8` for the `5 × 5` castle and `Z/102960 × Z/102960 × Z/48 × Z/16 × Z/4` for the `6 × 6` castle. Each agrees with the Smith normal form of the castle's reduced Laplacian with a corner sink cell, and the number of factors is `L` in each case.[^3][^8]
- **The height-3 castles.** The `L × 2` strip is the dual of the castle `(3, …, 3)` of width `L + 1`. Its group is `Z/d_1 × Z/d_2` with `d_2 = gcd(m_{L+1}, n_{L+1})` and `d_1 = m_{L+1} n_{L+1} / d_2`, where `m_0 = 0`, `m_1 = 1`, `m_{x+1} = 3m_x − m_{x−1}` (the Fibonacci numbers of even index, `m_x = F_{2x}`) and `n_0 = 0`, `n_1 = 1`, `n_{x+1} = 5n_x − n_{x−1}`. The paper calls `d_2` irregular, and its one general result is that the group is non-cyclic whenever 3 divides `L + 1`. Both agree with the castle sink groups for `L ≤ 12`; `d_2 = 1, 8, 1, 1, 48, 13, 1, 8, 5, 1, 2208, 1` for `L = 1` to 12, so the group is also non-cyclic at `L = 6` and `L = 9`.[^5][^8]
- **Clocks.** The period of the fed-at-the-center pile as the order of one addition operator, computed from a column of `Δ⁻¹`, is the period formula of [[sandcastle-clock](pages/sandcastle-clock.md)]: `(det Δ)/M` equals the least common denominator of the column of `Δ⁻¹`.[^6][^8]
- **The identity is the grid's.** The fractal identity and the `2L` to `2L + 1` construction concern the sandpile on the grid, which for a castle is the dual, one site per `2 × 2` block. They are not statements about the castle's identity on cells, drawn on [[sandpile-identity](pages/sandpile-identity.md)]; the two groups are isomorphic, but their configurations are different objects.[^7]

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

Through planar duality the paper's groups are the sink groups of the square and height-3 castles. It treats only rectangles with open boundaries on every side, so the wiki's tide model does not appear. Still, the even-index Fibonacci number `m_x` of the `L × 2` strip is the tide group order of the castle `(2, …, 2)` of width `x`, so the sink order `m_{L+1} n_{L+1}` of the height-3 castle of width `L + 1` is a tide order times `n_{L+1}` ([[sandpile-census](pages/sandpile-census.md)]).[^8] The Galois-orbit factorization of `|G|` and the fractal identities have no castle counterpart on the wiki.

Ruelle and Sen, two of its authors, wrote the 1992 paper in ref. [4] of [[chau-1993-abelian-sandpile-model](pages/chau-1993-abelian-sandpile-model.md)]. The paper works in Dhar's toppling-matrix formalism ([[dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile](pages/dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile.md)]), which [[chau-cheng-1991-deterministic-soc-sandpile](pages/chau-cheng-1991-deterministic-soc-sandpile.md)] also uses, on the model of [[bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md)].
