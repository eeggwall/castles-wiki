# Algebraic Aspects of Abelian Sandpile Models (Dhar, Ruelle, Sen, Verma, 1995)

**Source:** `raw/dhar-ruelle-sen-verma-1995-algebraic-aspects.pdf` (arXiv:cond-mat/9408022v1, 5 August 1994, 40 pages)
**Publication:** D. Dhar, P. Ruelle, S. Sen and D.-N. Verma, "Algebraic aspects of abelian sandpile models", Journal of Physics A 28 (1995) 805-831 (bibliographic data as cited in [[rossin-2000-group-of-a-sandpile](pages/rossin-2000-group-of-a-sandpile.md)], reference [9]).
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-27
**Type:** paper (preprint version read)

## Summary

The paper studies the finite abelian group of an abelian sandpile model, generated by the operators `a_k` that add a grain at site `k` and let the pile relax, for a general integer toppling matrix `Δ` and then for rectangles of the square lattice. It first recalls Dhar's results: the `a_k` commute, only the recurrent configurations occur in the steady state, they occur with equal probability, and there are `det Δ` of them, so `|G| = |R| = det Δ`.[^1] It then builds **toppling invariants**, `Q_i = Σ_j (Δ⁻¹)_ij z_j mod 1`, which take the same value on configurations related by topplings and therefore label the recurrent configurations, though not independently. Writing `Δ = A D B` in Smith normal form, with `D = diag(d_1, …, d_N)` and `d_i` a multiple of `d_{i+1}`, gives a minimal complete set `I_i = Σ_j (A⁻¹)_ij z_j mod d_i`, and with it the canonical decomposition `G = Z/d_1 × … × Z/d_g`, `g` the number of `d_i > 1`. The invariants add under the group law (add sitewise, then relax), and the identity is the unique recurrent configuration with every invariant zero.[^2]

For an `L_1 × L_2` rectangle of the square lattice with the discrete Laplacian as toppling matrix (4 on the diagonal, open boundary conditions on all four sides), the addition operators of one column generate `G`, so the rank is at most `L_2`. On the `L × L` square the `L`-fold eigenvalue 4 gives `L` independent operators of order dividing 4, so the rank is exactly `L`; the groups for `L = 2` to 5 are listed, starting with `Z/24 × Z/8`.[^3] The order `|G|` is the product of the eigenvalues `4 − 2cos(2πm/N) − 2cos(2πn/N)`, `N = 2(L+1)`, and grouping them into orbits of the Galois group of `Q(cos 2π/N)` factors `|G|` maximally into integers; the paper counts the orbits and builds algebraic toppling invariants from eigenvectors, which it shows are neither independent nor complete in general.[^4] Appendix A solves the `L × 2` strip exactly.[^5]

Section 7 takes up two questions. A deterministic sandpile that always adds its grain at the centre of a square lattice cycles with a period independent of the starting pile, and that period is the order of the central addition operator on the recurrent configurations, computed from the column of `Δ⁻¹` at the centre; it divides the largest cyclic factor of the relevant group.[^6] And the identity configuration of the `L × L` square shows fractal structure, including a central square of maximal height of side about `4L/5` on `2L × 2L` lattices and a simple relation between the identities on `2L × 2L` and `(2L+1) × (2L+1)` lattices, which the authors describe as not well understood.[^7]

## Key Takeaways

- **The algebra the sandpile pages use, from a read source.** `|G| = det Δ` (the spanning-tree count for a Laplacian), the Smith normal form as the structure of `G`, and toppling invariants built from `Δ⁻¹`.[^1][^2]
- **Dhar's steady-state facts.** Commuting addition operators, and recurrent configurations occurring with equal probability in the steady state, as summarized in §2 - the fact behind averaging avalanches over the recurrent piles.[^1]
- **The grid is a castle's dual.** The `L × L` open-boundary grid, 4 on the diagonal and one sink edge per missing neighbour, is exactly the sink-model block matrix of the `(L+1) × (L+1)` castle. So `G(L × L)` is the castle's sink group: `Z/24 × Z/8` for `(3,3,3)`, `Z/224 × Z/112 × Z/4` for `(4,4,4,4)`, `Z/6600 × Z/1320 × Z/8 × Z/8` and `Z/102960 × Z/102960 × Z/48 × Z/16 × Z/4` for the `5 × 5` and `6 × 6` castles, all matching the wiki's computation.[^3]
- **Rank of the square castle.** The paper's proof that `g = L` on the `L × L` grid says the `(L+1) × (L+1)` castle's sink group has exactly `L` cyclic factors.[^3]
- **The height-3 castles.** The `L × 2` strip is the dual of the castle `(3, …, 3)` of width `L + 1`. Its group is `Z/d_1 × Z/d_2` with `d_2 = gcd(m_{L+1}, n_{L+1})` and `d_1 = m_{L+1} n_{L+1} / d_2`, where `m_x` are the even Fibonacci numbers (`m_{x+1} = 3m_x − m_{x−1}`) and `n_{x+1} = 5n_x − n_{x−1}`; it is non-cyclic whenever `3` divides `L + 1`, and otherwise `d_2` behaves irregularly.[^5]
- **Clocks.** The deterministic period as the order of one addition operator, computed from a column of `Δ⁻¹` and dividing the largest cyclic factor, is the principle of the wiki's sandcastle clock.[^6]
- **The identity lives on the grid here.** The fractal identity and its `2L → 2L + 1` self-similarity are properties of the sandpile on the grid, which for a castle is the dual (one site per `2 × 2` block). They are not statements about the castle's cell-level identity drawn on [[sandpile-identity](pages/sandpile-identity.md)]; the groups are isomorphic, the configurations are not the same objects.[^7]

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

This is the second ingested source for the Sandcastles pages and the first primary paper. It confirms the sink-model algebra the pages use and supplies, through planar duality, independent computations of the sink groups of square and height-3 castles, which agree with the wiki's census in every case checked. It treats only the sink setting on rectangles with open boundaries on every side; the wiki's tide model does not appear, though the paper's even-Fibonacci mode `m_x` of the `L × 2` strip is the tide group order of the 2-high castle ladder, so the sink order of the height-3 castle factors as a tide order times `n_x` (checked on [[sandpile-census](pages/sandpile-census.md)]). The paper's Galois-orbit factorization of `|G|` and its fractal identities are threads the wiki has not followed.
