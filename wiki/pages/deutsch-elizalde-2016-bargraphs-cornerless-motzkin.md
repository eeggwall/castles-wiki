# Statistics on bargraphs viewed as cornerless Motzkin paths (Deutsch-Elizalde, 2016)

**Source:** `raw/deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin.pdf` (text layer at `raw/deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin.txt`, extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; the PDF is git-ignored, the text is tracked). arXiv:1609.00088v1 [math.CO], 1 September 2016.[^1]
**Authors:** Emeric Deutsch (NYU Tandon), Sergi Elizalde (Dartmouth).[^1]
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-26
**Type:** paper (PDF, 19 pp.)

## Summary

A **bargraph** is a lattice path with unit steps `U` (up), `H` (right) and `D` (down) that starts at the origin, ends on the `x`-axis, stays strictly above it in between, and has no `UD` or `DU`. Its **semiperimeter** is `#U + #H`.[^2] Under the column-height reading every castle is a bargraph and conversely ([[castle-polyomino](pages/castle-polyomino.md)]): `#H` is the width `w` and `#U` is the total rise, which is the block count. So the paper's two grading variables, `x` for `H` and `y` for `U`, are exactly the castle's width and blocks, and its semiperimeter is the castle's `w + #blocks` ([[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]).

The paper's observation is that bargraphs are the same thing as **cornerless Motzkin paths**, meaning Motzkin paths with no peak `UD` and no valley `DU`. Given such a path, add an up step at the front and a down step at the back, then read up steps as vertical `U`, down steps as vertical `D`, and flats as `H`. This map `Δ` sends paths with `#U + #H = n − 1` to bargraphs of semiperimeter `n`.[^3] The authors remark that it "surprisingly seems not to have been noticed before".[^4] Cornerless paths have a first-return decomposition simpler than the five-case wasp-waist decomposition of Bousquet-Mélou and Rechnitzer: a nonempty path is `HA`, `UA′D`, or `UA′DHA` with `A′` nonempty. That gives the factorization `M = (1 + y(M − 1))(1 + xM)`, and with `B = y(M − 1)` the quadratic `xB² − (1 − x − y − xy)B + xy = 0`.[^5] Setting `x = y = z` gives bargraphs by semiperimeter, A082582.[^6]

The rest of the paper is a toolkit. Whenever a statistic behaves well under `Δ`, its bargraph GF is `y(M_st − 1)`,[^7] and the authors use this for about twenty statistics. These include the first-column height, double rises and falls, valleys and peaks of fixed width, corners, horizontal segments and unit segments, the first descent, columns of height `h`, the least column height, the leftmost segment width, `UHU` factors, the initial staircase, odd- and even-height columns, and area. Each GF is quadratic. The sequences are listed in a table and most were added to OEIS because of this work (A273342-A276068 and others).[^8] Several coincidences get bijective proofs: no-double-rise bargraphs versus RNA secondary structures,[^9] least column height versus leftmost segment width,[^10] and strictly alternating bargraphs versus Motzkin paths avoiding `UD`, `UU` and `DD`.[^11] Section 4 counts symmetric bargraphs and weakly or strictly alternating bargraphs.[^12]

## Key Takeaways

- **The bijection is the castle's tower word.** `Δ⁻¹` removes the outer `U…D`, which is [[project-euler-502-representations](pages/project-euler-502-representations.md)]'s "castle = `U (tower) D`". The decomposition `HA | UA′D | UA′DHA` is the tower grammar `E_k → ε | R·E_k | U V D (ε | R·E_k)` of [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)] without the height bound: `R` is `H`, the nonempty `V` is `A′`, and the optional `R·E_k` tail is `HA`.[^5] The wiki found the same grammar independently and named its words peakless-valleyless Motzkin paths ([[tower-word-continued-fraction](pages/tower-word-continued-fraction.md)]). This paper is where that grammar lives in the bargraph literature.
- **The castle GF by width and blocks.** `xB² − (1 − x − y − xy)B + xy = 0`, with `x` marking width and `y` marking blocks. Checked against brute-force castle counts for every `(w, b)` with `w + b ≤ 11`. PE 502's sign `(−1)^blocks` is the substitution `y → −y`, so every statistic GF in the paper has a signed castle version for free.[^5]
- **One-row deletion and prime castles.** Deleting the initial `U^h` and final `D^h` gives a bijection from bargraphs of semiperimeter `n` with least column height `> h` to all bargraphs of semiperimeter `n − h`.[^10] At `h = 1` the left side is the castles with no height-1 column, the primes of [[prime-castles](pages/prime-castles.md)], and the map is the wiki's row-raising map read backwards ([[castle-row-raising-equation](pages/castle-row-raising-equation.md)]). Raising adds one block and keeps the width, so prime castles by semi-perimeter are all castles shifted by one: `0, 1, 2, 5, 13, 35, 97, 275, 794, 2327` for `s = 2..11` (checked by brute force, and equal to the paper's columns-of-height-1 GF at `t = 0`).[^13]
- **RNA secondary structures.** Bargraphs with no double rise, of semiperimeter `n + 1`, correspond bijectively to secondary structures on `n` vertices. The semiperimeter GF has exponents shifted by one from Stanley's RNA GF.[^9] A double rise in a castle is a rise of two or more at one column step. The mirror statistic, no double fall, is the "drops of at most 1, last column at height 1" family on [[motzkin-castles](pages/motzkin-castles.md)], whose semi-perimeter count A004148 is the RNA sequence (both families: `1, 1, 2, 4, 8, 17, 37, 82, 185, 423` for `s = 2..11`, by brute force).
- **Named castle types get GFs.** Nondecreasing bargraphs, meaning `c_1 ≤ … ≤ c_w` (the reverse Ferrers type of [[castle-classification-shape](pages/castle-classification-shape.md)]), have GF `xy/(1 − x − y)`.[^14] Symmetric bargraphs, meaning palindromic castles `c_i = c_{w+1−i}` (a type that classification page lists as open), have an explicit algebraic GF in `(x, y)` built from Motzkin prefixes.[^12] Both were checked against brute-force castle counts by `(w, b)`. Palindromic castles by semi-perimeter are A273905: `1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 15, 27, 46, 83`.[^15]
- **Area as a continued fraction.** Area enters through `B_area(t, x, y) = y(M_area(t, tx, y) − 1)`, since `Δ` lifts every `H` by one row. Iterating the factorization gives a continued fraction in the `t`-shifted width variable, and truncating it at `−tⁿx + 1` is exact up to `n` vertical steps.[^16] Prellberg-Brak also iterate their bar-graph functional equation into a continued fraction in the shifted width variable (eq. 3.12, [[prellberg-brak-1995-cluster-models](pages/prellberg-brak-1995-cluster-models.md)]); this paper reaches a continued fraction from the cornerless decomposition instead. Whether the two are term-for-term the same expansion has not been checked.

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

This paper confirms the wiki's tower-word machinery rather than contradicting anything. The semiperimeter convention, the A082582 series, and the quadratic GF all agree with [[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]. That page derives the same `B(t)` from the tower-Narayana identity; this paper derives it from the cornerless decomposition. Its variables `(x, y)` are the wiki's `(width, blocks)`, so the whole statistic table can be read as castle statistics and split by PE 502 parity with `y → −y`. Most of those splits are not done yet and remain open. The follow-up is [[prodinger-2025-cornerless-motzkin-bargraphs](pages/prodinger-2025-cornerless-motzkin-bargraphs.md)]: the same bijection read from the prefix side, with `UD` and `DU` weighted instead of forbidden, solved by the kernel method, and extended to skew Motzkin paths.
