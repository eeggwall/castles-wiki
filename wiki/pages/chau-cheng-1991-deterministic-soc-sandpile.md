# Completely deterministic self-organized critical state of sandpile automaton models (Chau-Cheng, 1991)

**Source:** `raw/chau-cheng-1991-deterministic-soc-sandpile.pdf` (text layer at `raw/chau-cheng-1991-deterministic-soc-sandpile.txt`, extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; the PDF is git-ignored, the text is tracked). The two-column text layer loses the displayed matrices of eq. 13, which are read from the page image.
**Publication:** H. F. Chau and K. S. Cheng, *Physics Letters A* **157** (1991) 103-106; received 18 March 1991. University of Hong Kong.[^1]
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-27
**Type:** paper (PDF, 4 pp.)

## Summary

The paper works in Dhar's abelian sandpile model (ASM) ([[dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile](pages/dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile.md)]). Heights are integers, a site topples when its height exceeds a critical value, and toppling subtracts a row of an integer matrix `Δ` with positive diagonal, non-positive off-diagonal and non-negative row sums.[^2] Chau and Cheng shift the critical heights to 0, so stable heights are `≤ 0`, where Dhar uses `1..Δ_ii` and the wiki `0..deg − 1`.[^2] Following Dhar, the grain operators `a_i` commute, so the set `R` of steady-state (self-organized critical, SOC) configurations can be treated as a finite abelian group.[^3]

Instead of the usual exponents and correlations, the authors look for sandpiles whose steady state is **completely deterministic**: once the system is in `R`, dropping a grain on any site gives the same result, `a_i|_R = a_j|_R` for all `i, j` (eq. 5). The future then depends only on the total number of grains added, and the probability distribution of where grains land no longer matters.[^4] Their first result is that, up to relabelling, `R` is then `{(0, 0, …, 0), (−1, 0, …, 0), …, (−n, 0, …, 0)}` (eq. 6). At most one site can vary, because two distinct sites that accept a grain without toppling would give two different results. So `R ≅ Z_{n+1}`: the system is "one-dimensional", labelled by a single height.[^5] The argument does not use commutativity, so it carries over to gradient-triggered toppling as well.[^5]

To classify these systems they introduce **equivalent toppling rules**. Two matrices `Δ, Δ′` are equivalent if they have the same `R` and the same grain operators on it (eq. 9). By Dhar's criterion that two configurations are equivalent when they differ by an integer combination of rows of `Δ` (eq. 10), this holds iff one matrix becomes the other by elementary row additions and subtractions.[^6] Row operations can always reach a **fundamental toppling rule**, one with `−Δ_ij < Δ_jj` for `i ≠ j` (eq. 11); `−Δ_ij ≥ Δ_jj` is exactly the condition that site `j` always topples whenever `i` does (eq. 12).[^6]

The main theorem is that an SOC system is completely deterministic iff, after relabelling, its fundamental toppling rule is either the identity matrix or the matrix with `Δ_11 = n` (`n > 1`), `Δ_ii = 1` and `Δ_i1 = −1` for `i ≠ 1`, and zeros elsewhere (eq. 13). In the second form every other site passes its single grain to site 1, and only site 1 loses grains.[^7] The one-dimensional BTW sandpile is the trivial case: its SOC state is a single configuration with every local gradient just below critical, every added grain slides off, and `R` is the trivial group.[^8] The paper closes with an analogy. A family of circle maps `f(x) = (x + θ) mod 2π` evolves deterministically when all the `θ`s are equal, and the orbit of a point then forms `Z_n` for rational `θ/2π`, like the cyclic `R` here.[^9]

## Key Takeaways

- **Deterministic castles are exactly the trivial ones.** This is own reasoning from eqs. 5 and 10, not in the paper. In a castle sandpile, in the sink model (one sink cell) or the tide model (the bottom row as the sink) ([[sandpile-group](pages/sandpile-group.md)]), every cell next to the sink has exactly **one** edge into it. If the steady state is deterministic, every grain is the same element `g` of `K = Z^n / L̃ Z^n`. Toppling a cell `v` changes nothing in `K`, so row `v` of `L̃` gives `s(v)·g = 0`, where `s(v) = deg(v) − (live neighbours of v)` is the number of sink edges at `v`. A sink neighbour has `s(v) = 1`, so `g = 0` and `K` is trivial. Conversely a trivial group is deterministic. The census confirms it: among castles with at most 12 cells, none with a nontrivial group is deterministic, in either model (769 trivial groups in the sink model, 758 in the tide model among castles of height at least 2; exact rational check, 2026-09-27).
- **Nontrivial examples need multiple sink edges.** The general version of the same argument gives `s(v)·g = 0` for every site, so a deterministic group has order dividing every nonzero dissipation `s(v)`. That is Chau-Cheng's form (13), where the only dissipating site loses `n` grains and the group is cyclic of order `det Δ = n` (this `n` is eq. 13's; eq. 6 uses `n` for the largest height offset, so there `|R| = n + 1`). A castle can only reach it if the sink is joined to a cell by several edges, for instance a tide that pulls `n` grains per toppling.
- **Deterministic is stronger than cyclic.** A deterministic sandpile has a cyclic group, but the census on [[sandpile-census](pages/sandpile-census.md)] has many cyclic castle groups, and by the argument above none of the nontrivial ones is deterministic. So "cyclic sandcastles" and "deterministic sandcastles" are different questions, and the second is settled.
- **Row operations as the natural equivalence.** Chau-Cheng's equivalence (same `R`, same grain operators) is invariance of the row lattice `Z^n Δ` under unimodular row operations. That is a finer relation than having the same group, which is all that the 2×2-block presentation of the census preserves (it has a different size).
- **Trees wash away.** Arc 14's slogan that tree castles "wash away" is the castle version of the paper's 1D BTW remark: every added grain leaves, and the steady state is a single configuration. BTW's one-dimensional pile is itself a skyline, and its single steady state is a staircase with every drop equal to the threshold ([[bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md)]).

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

The paper adds a classification (deterministic steady states) and an equivalence relation (row operations) to the sandpile theory the wiki sources to Dhar. Its height convention (stable heights `≤ 0`) differs from Dhar's and the wiki's by a shift, recorded on [[castle-notation](pages/castle-notation.md)]. Nothing on the wiki conflicts with it. The castle consequence, that only trivial castle groups are deterministic, is new and is recorded on [[sandpile-group](pages/sandpile-group.md)].
