# Tower word language

## Tower words

Removing the bottom row of a castle leaves towers, and the towers on a base of length `L` are written as a word in `U` (up one row), `R` (one column) and `D` (down one row), with one `R` per column at the column's height ([[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)]). A **tower word** has exactly `L` letters `R`, starts and ends at height 0, never goes below 0, and contains neither `UD`, a block of width 0, nor `DU`, two touching blocks.[^1] Its height is the largest height it reaches. The castle `(2, 1, 2)` stands on the towers `(1, 0, 1)`, whose tower word is `URDRURD`.

With `U = +1`, `R = 0` and `D = −1`, a tower word is a Motzkin path, a path of up, flat and down steps that never goes below 0 and ends at 0, with no peak `UD` and no valley `DU`. Such paths are the **cornerless** Motzkin paths of [[deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin](pages/deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin.md)].

**Lemma.** The tower words with `L` letters `R` and height at most `k` correspond one-to-one with the tuples `(c_1, …, c_L) ∈ {0, …, k}^L`, the heights at which the `R`s stand.

*Proof.* Between two consecutive `R`s the vertical steps are all `U` or all `D`, since a change of direction would create `UD` or `DU`; so they are determined by the heights of the two `R`s. Before the first `R` the word is `U^{c_1}`, and after the last it is `D^{c_L}`. So a tower word is determined by its tuple of heights, and every tuple gives a tower word.[^2]

## The grammar

The towers of height at most `k` are generated by[^1]

```
E_k  →  empty  |  R E_k  |  U V D ( empty | R E_k ),        V a nonempty word of E_{k−1}.
```

**Proposition.** The grammar generates exactly the tower words of height at most `k`, and each of them exactly once.

*Proof.* Each production gives a tower word: `U V D` raises a tower word of height at most `k − 1` by one row, `V` is nonempty so `UD` does not occur, and the tail `empty | R E_k` puts an `R` after the `D`, so `DU` does not occur. Conversely, take a nonempty tower word, and argue by induction on its length. It starts with `R` or `U`, since a `D` would go below 0. If it starts with `R`, the rest is a tower word, and only the production `R E_k` applies. If it starts with `U`, let `D` be the step of the first return to height 0. The letters between them form a word `V` that stays at height at least 1; lowered by one row, `V` is a tower word of height at most `k − 1`, and it is nonempty because `UD` is excluded. After that `D` the word ends or continues with `R`, since a `D` would go below 0 and a `U` would create `DU`. So the first letter and the first return fix the production and its parts, and the parse is unique.[^2]

This is the first-return decomposition of Dyck and Motzkin paths, with the extra letter `R` ([[dyck-words](pages/dyck-words.md)]).

## Rational at fixed height, algebraic without a bound

For fixed `k`, the tower words of height at most `k` form a regular language: an automaton whose states are the current height, at most `k`, and the last letter recognizes it, since the excluded factors `UD` and `DU` have two letters. Flajolet notes the same for Motzkin paths of bounded height, which form "a regular or rational set".[^3] Counted by width, with `x` marking each `R`, their generating function is

```
E_k(x)  =  1/(1 − (k+1)x),        so        T(k, L)  =  (k+1)^L
```

([[castle-counting-formula](pages/castle-counting-formula.md)], the section *Tower count*).[^4] Without a height bound there are infinitely many tower words with `L` letters `R`, but finitely many of each length. Counted by length, the tower words number `A004149(n+1)` for words of length `n`, and their generating function satisfies a quadratic equation with a square-root singularity at `√2 − 1`, so it is algebraic and not rational ([[tower-word-continued-fraction](pages/tower-word-continued-fraction.md)]).

## Dyck, Motzkin and tower words

| language | steps | condition | count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dyck words | `U`, `D` (`+1`, `−1`) | never below 0, end at 0 | Catalan `C_n` for length `2n` |
| Motzkin paths | `U`, `R`, `D` (`+1`, `0`, `−1`) | never below 0, end at 0 | Motzkin `M_n` for length `n` |
| tower words | `U`, `R`, `D` | as Motzkin, and no `UD`, no `DU` | `(k+1)^L` with `L` letters `R` and height at most `k`; `A004149(n+1)` for length `n` |

The Motzkin paths with no flat steps are the Dyck paths. Choosing the positions of the flat steps gives `M_n = Σ_j C(n, 2j) C_j` ([[motzkin-numbers](pages/motzkin-numbers.md)]).[^5]

A Dyck word is **steep** if it is nonempty and contains no factor `UDU`. Removing from the Dyck grammar the one rule that creates `UDU` leaves the grammar `S → UD | U S D | U S D S`, whose generating function `G` satisfies `G = x + xG + xG²` with `x` marking each pair `UD`; so the steep Dyck words of length `2n` number `M_{n−1}` ([[dyck-words](pages/dyck-words.md)]).[^6] Steepness excludes the factor `UDU`, while tower words exclude `UD` and `DU`, which are different conditions. The tower words need theirs to be counted by width: without it, factors `UD` would add length without adding an `R`, and each `L` would have infinitely many words.

## Boundary words: Boasson's lemma

Delest and Viennot show that a convex polyomino cannot be coded by a word of an algebraic language "by just following the border". By Boasson's lemma, no word of an algebraic language has overlapping iterative pairs, and the boundary words of convex polyominoes have them.[^7] They cut a convex polyomino by two vertical lines into a parallelogram polyomino and two stacks, and code each piece separately ([[algebraic-languages-and-polyominoes-enumeration](pages/algebraic-languages-and-polyominoes-enumeration.md)]).

The lower boundary of a castle is its full bottom row, so its step word `U (tower word) D` follows only the upper boundary ([[urd-step-strings](pages/urd-step-strings.md)]). The step words of castles of every height form an unambiguous context-free language: the grammar of the section *The grammar* with no height bound, which is Deutsch and Elizalde's first-return decomposition. The convex castles are the stack polyominoes, and Delest and Viennot's code of a stack, `a` for each vertical step and `xx` for each horizontal one, is the step word of the convex castle with `U` and `D` replaced by `a` and `R` by `xx` ([[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)]).

Delest and Viennot's method is to write an unambiguous grammar, translate it into an algebraic system of equations in noncommuting variables, and then commute the variables; in their paper, "the method is reversed: no formula is known", and the grammar produces the formula.[^8]
