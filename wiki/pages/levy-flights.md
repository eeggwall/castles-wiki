# Lévy flights on the castle graph

## The framing

A castle graph carries two natural random walks. The nearest-neighbor walk has generator `L = D − A` (the graph Laplacian of [[castle-graph](pages/castle-graph.md)]); its semigroup `exp(-t L)` is the ordinary heat kernel. On a comb (a spine with long teeth), a random walker gets trapped in the teeth and, over times short compared with the tooth length, its mean-square displacement along the spine grows like `t^{1/2}` instead of `t`, the classical subdiffusion regime.

The **opposite** anomalous-diffusion regime is Lévy flight: a walk whose single-step distribution has a power-law tail, so displacement grows faster than `t^{1/2}`. On a lattice this is generated by the space-fractional Laplacian `(-Δ)^α` for `α ∈ (0, 1)`. Riascos and Mateos (2014) transferred the construction to arbitrary graphs by spectral calculus,[^1] and this page uses their `L^α` on the castle graph. It settles two questions:

1. **Is the fractional heat trace a shape invariant that Laplacian-cospectral castles share?** Yes, trivially, by construction.
2. **Does the return probability at a named cell separate the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair, where the trace cannot?** Yes, at every `(α, t)` tested.

## The Riascos-Mateos fractional Laplacian

Let `L = U Λ U^T` be the spectral decomposition of the ordinary graph Laplacian, with eigenvalues `0 = λ_1 ≤ λ_2 ≤ … ≤ λ_n`. The **space-fractional graph Laplacian** of order `α > 0` is

```
L^α  :=  U Λ^α U^T,     where   Λ^α = diag(λ_i^α),   0^α := 0.
```

For `α = 1` this is `L` itself; for `α ∈ (0, 1)` it is a Lévy-flight generator: its off-diagonal entries are `≤ 0` (Bochner's formula writes `L^α` as an integral of `I − exp(-sL)`), so `exp(-t L^α)` is a Markov semigroup. For `α > 1` this fails: on the 11-cell pair below, `L^{1.5}` and `L^2` have positive off-diagonal entries and `exp(-t L^α)` has negative entries, so for `α > 1` the diagonal entries are spectral-calculus quantities, not return probabilities of a walk. The transition semigroup `exp(-t L^α)` acts as `Σ_i u_i u_i^T exp(-t λ_i^α)`, and the **return probability** at vertex `v` at time `t` is the diagonal entry

```
p_t(v, v)  =  [exp(-t L^α)]_{vv}  =  Σ_i u_i(v)^2 exp(-t λ_i^α).
```

Two structural facts follow from the definition. First, unlike the sparse `L`, which only connects graph neighbors, `L^α` is **dense** for non-integer `α` (every off-diagonal entry is nonzero on the 11-cell pair at `α = 0.5` and `1.5`), with entries decaying with graph distance at a power-law rate governed by `α`.[^1] Second, every spectral invariant of `L` (heat trace, characteristic polynomial, Kirchhoff index) has a fractional-order counterpart that is again a function of `Λ` alone. In particular `Tr exp(-t L^α) = Σ_i exp(-t λ_i^α)` **depends only on the Laplacian spectrum**.

## The 11-cell pair, and what fractional heat cannot separate

[[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)] identifies the smallest castle Laplacian-isospectral pair: two 11-cell trees.

```
class A: skyline (1,1,1,2,1,1,2,1,1)         class B: skyline (1,1,3,1,1,1,2,1)
...#..#..                                     ..#.....
#########                                     ..#...#.
                                              ########
```

Both are trees, 11 vertices, 10 edges, common Laplacian characteristic polynomial `x¹¹ − 20x¹⁰ + … + 11x`.[^2] Different local branching patterns: `A` has two degree-3 vertices each adjacent to a leaf; `B` has one degree-3 vertex adjacent to a two-cell path (the tower) and one adjacent to a leaf.

Because they share every Laplacian eigenvalue, they share every fractional heat trace:

```
Tr exp(-t L_A^α)  =  Σ_i exp(-t λ_i^α)  =  Tr exp(-t L_B^α),   for every α > 0, t > 0.
```

The check is one line: raise each eigenvalue to `α`, exponentiate, sum. Numerically for `α ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}` and `t = 1`:

| α | Tr exp(-t L^α), both A and B |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 4.2486… |
| 1.0 | 4.0081… |
| 2.0 | 4.0680… |

So the fractional heat trace is a **spectral** invariant, and any family of shape invariants read off from it is blind to the 11-cell pair by construction.

## Local return probability at a named cell — the separator

The diagonal entry `p_t(v, v) = Σ_i u_i(v)^2 exp(-t λ_i^α)` **is not** a function of the Laplacian spectrum alone: the eigenvector amplitude `u_i(v)^2` at a named vertex depends on the graph's local structure at `v`. Two Laplacian-cospectral graphs can — and, at cospectral non-isomorphic pairs, do — have different local return probabilities.

The **tallest cell** of each castle is the natural named cell to compare: in `A`, the two tied height-2 spike tips at columns 4 and 7; in `B`, the unique height-3 tower tip at column 3.

```
p_t(apex, apex), t = 1, at the tallest cell of each castle:

α      A: (3,1)   A: (6,1)   A avg      B: (2,2)   |B − A avg|
0.10   0.43314    0.43403    0.43359    0.43820    0.00462
0.25   0.44454    0.44646    0.44550    0.45662    0.01112
0.50   0.46229    0.46464    0.46347    0.48331    0.01984
0.75   0.47867    0.48010    0.47939    0.50479    0.02540
1.00   0.49402    0.49405    0.49403    0.52242    0.02839
1.50   0.52056    0.51941    0.51998    0.55123    0.03125
2.00   0.54009    0.54219    0.54114    0.57401    0.03287
```

Three readings.

**The 3-tower tip has the higher value at every α.** For every fractional order in the table, the diagonal entry at `B`'s height-3 apex exceeds those at `A`'s height-2 apices.

**The separation shrinks as α decreases.** The `|B − A avg|` column is **monotone increasing in α**, from 0.005 at α = 0.10 to 0.033 at α = 2, and it tends to 0 as `α → 0` (see the limit below).

**The argmax cell depends on α.** `A`'s largest diagonal entry is at the leftmost spine end `(0, 0)` for `α ≤ 1` and at the other spine end `(8, 0)` for `α = 1.5, 2`. `B`'s is at the tower tip `(2, 2)` for `α = 0.10, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5`, and at the spine end `(0, 0)` for `α = 0.75` and `2`.

## Sorted-diagonal multiset separation

A stronger test: the sorted multiset `{p_t(v, v) : v ∈ V}` of diagonal entries across all 11 vertices, compared as a vector. Agreement at every `t` would be a much stronger condition than cospectrality. They do not agree; the L² distance between sorted multisets is:

```
||sort(p_A) − sort(p_B)||_2

α      t = 0.5   t = 1.0    t = 2.0    t = 5.0
0.10   4.95e-3   6.59e-3    5.82e-3    1.16e-3
0.25   1.02e-2   1.53e-2    1.68e-2    5.70e-3
0.50   1.38e-2   2.45e-2    3.26e-2    1.69e-2
0.75   1.34e-2   2.88e-2    4.19e-2    3.19e-2
1.00   1.27e-2   3.53e-2    5.05e-2    4.45e-2
1.50   1.41e-2   5.33e-2    7.11e-2    6.29e-2
2.00   3.13e-2   7.09e-2    9.24e-2    6.16e-2
```

Two features. First, **every entry is nonzero**, so the multisets always disagree. Second, at `t = 1` and `t = 2` the distance grows monotonically with `α`, and at small `α` it becomes small. In the `α → 0` limit `L^α → I − P_ker`, whose exponential is `e^{-t}(I) + (1 − e^{-t})P_ker`, a rank-1 kernel projector; the sorted diagonal multiset collapses to a single value repeated `n` times, distance zero.

## A short reproducer

A NumPy reproducer for the two skylines above:

```python
import numpy as np

def castle_L(skyline):
    cells = [(i, j) for i, h in enumerate(skyline) for j in range(h)]
    idx = {c: k for k, c in enumerate(cells)}
    n = len(cells)
    A = np.zeros((n, n))
    for (i, j), k in idx.items():
        for di, dj in [(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)]:
            if (i+di, j+dj) in idx:
                A[k, idx[(i+di, j+dj)]] = 1
    return cells, np.diag(A.sum(1)) - A

def return_diag(L, alpha, t):
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(L)
    return (V**2) @ np.exp(-t * np.where(w > 1e-12, w**alpha, 0.0))

cellsA, LA = castle_L((1,1,1,2,1,1,2,1,1))
cellsB, LB = castle_L((1,1,3,1,1,1,2,1))
assert np.allclose(np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(LA)), np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(LB)))
# LA, LB are Laplacian-cospectral; the diagonals of exp(-t L^alpha) separate them
```

The assertion passes; the two diagonals do not match at any `(α, t)` above.

## Numerics and the Toeplitz handle

`L^α` is dense for non-integer `α`, so on a castle with `n` cells it takes `Θ(n²)` storage, the same regime as dense discretizations in the space-fractional PDE literature.[^3] Two features of the castle graph help.

**Path structure.** Restricted to a single column of a rectangular castle, the graph Laplacian is the Laplacian of the path `P_h`, tridiagonal and Toeplitz except at the two ends; its eigenvectors are the discrete cosine basis and its eigenvalues `4 sin²(kπ/(2h))`, `k = 0, …, h − 1`. Restricted to a single row, likewise. On a rectangular `w × h` castle the two directions decouple exactly, `L = L_w ⊗ I_h + I_w ⊗ L_h`, and `L^α` follows from the two small eigendecompositions in `Θ(w³ + h³)`. For non-rectangular skylines the decoupling fails, though each column is still a path.

**Practical size.** For the 11-cell pair the whole calculation is 121 doubles per operator and instantaneous, and the census of [[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)] stops at 16 cells. For much larger castles a Krylov (Lanczos) method for `exp(-t L^α) v` avoids forming `L^α`; the tensor-product route applies to rectangles.[^3]

## What this settles

- **Fractional heat traces `Tr exp(-t L^α)` are functions of the Laplacian spectrum**, so the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair shares them at every `α, t`. Any family of castle shape invariants extracted from `Tr exp(-t L^α)` alone — heat-content moments, spectral entropy, fractional isoperimetric quantities — is blind to the pair.
- **The diagonal entry `p_t(v, v)` at a named vertex is not spectral**. The tallest cell (height-3 tower tip in `B`, height-2 spike tip in `A`) separates the pair at every `α` in the table and every `t ∈ [0.5, 5]` tested, with the height-3 tower tip higher.
- **The separation shrinks in the Lévy regime.** The A-vs-B separation is *smaller* for `α < 1` than at `α = 1`, larger for `α > 1` (where the entries are no longer probabilities), and tends to 0 as `α → 0`.
- The sorted multiset of diagonal entries separates the 11-cell pair at every `(α, t)` in the table.
- On rectangles `L^α` factors through the two path Laplacians.

## What this opens

- Does the sorted-multiset separation vanish at some `α > 0`? It tends to 0 as `α → 0`; at `α = 0.10, t = 5` it is `1.2e-3`, far above double-precision noise.
- The **fractional Kirchhoff index** is a function of the Laplacian spectrum, so also spectral. What is its local counterpart (a "fractional effective resistance" between named vertices)? The ordinary version is already on the wiki: the inverse reduced Laplacian of [[castle-avalanches](pages/castle-avalanches.md)], whose entries are Dhar's expected topplings, is the effective-resistance matrix grounded at the drain.
- **Coupling the two regimes.** A castle with teeth (comb-subdiffusive) walked with a Lévy generator: does the exponent `1/2` from tooth-trapping compete or cooperate with the Lévy jump-length exponent `α`?  This is the two-parameter anomalous-diffusion phase diagram on castle graphs.
- Whether `L^α` has a low-rank plus sparse decomposition on tree castles specifically.
