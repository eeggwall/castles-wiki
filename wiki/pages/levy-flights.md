# Lévy flights on the castle graph

Riascos and Mateos define a fractional power of the Laplacian matrix of any graph, from its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and use it for random walks with long-range jumps, Lévy flights; on a ring the resulting transition and return probabilities decay as a power law in the distance.[^1] This page applies their fractional Laplacian to the castle graph ([[castle-graph](pages/castle-graph.md)]: cells as vertices, edge-sharing cells joined) and asks whether it separates the smallest pair of Laplacian-cospectral castles ([[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)]). The fractional heat trace does not; the diagonal of the fractional heat kernel does.

## The fractional Laplacian

Let `L = D − A` be the Laplacian of the castle graph, with spectral decomposition `L = U Λ U^T` and eigenvalues `0 = λ_1 ≤ λ_2 ≤ … ≤ λ_n`. For `α > 0`,

```
L^α  =  U Λ^α U^T,        Λ^α = diag(λ_i^α),   0^α = 0.                     (1)
```

At `α = 1` this is `L`. For `0 < α < 1`, the formula `λ^α = (α / Γ(1 − α)) ∫_0^∞ (1 − e^{−sλ}) s^{−1−α} ds` gives

```
L^α  =  (α / Γ(1 − α)) ∫_0^∞ (I − exp(−sL)) s^{−1−α} ds,
```

and `exp(−sL)` has nonnegative entries and row sums 1, so the off-diagonal entries of `L^α` are at most 0 and its row sums are 0. Then `exp(−t L^α)` is a Markov semigroup, and its diagonal entry

```
p_t(v, v)  =  [exp(−t L^α)]_{vv}  =  Σ_i u_i(v)² exp(−t λ_i^α)                   (2)
```

is the probability that the continuous-time walk generated by `L^α` started at `v` is at `v` at time `t`. For `α > 1` this fails: on the castle `A` below, `L^{1.5}` has positive off-diagonal entries (largest 0.321), and `exp(−L^{1.5})` and `exp(−L^2)` have negative entries (smallest −0.024 and −0.053), so for `α > 1` the entries of Equation (2) are not probabilities. For non-integer `α`, `L^α` has no zero entry on `A` at `α = 0.5` and `α = 1.5`; for integer `α` it is a polynomial in `L` and vanishes between cells more than `α` apart in the graph.

The heat trace

```
Tr exp(−t L^α)  =  Σ_i exp(−t λ_i^α)                                             (3)
```

depends only on the eigenvalues of `L`, while the diagonal entries (2) depend on the eigenvectors too.

## The 11-cell pair

The smallest pair of castles with the same Laplacian spectrum and non-isomorphic graphs is a pair of tree castles with 11 cells ([[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)]):

```
A: skyline (1,1,1,2,1,1,2,1,1)        B: skyline (1,1,3,1,1,1,2,1)
...#..#..                              ..#.....
#########                              ..#...#.
                                       ########
```

Both are trees with 11 vertices and 10 edges and Laplacian characteristic polynomial `x¹¹ − 20x¹⁰ + 169x⁹ − ⋯ + 11x`.[^2] Write cells as `(column, row)` from `(0, 0)`. In `A`, each of the two cells of degree 3, `(3, 0)` and `(6, 0)`, has one leaf neighbor. In `B`, the cell `(6, 0)` of degree 3 has two leaf neighbors, `(7, 0)` and `(6, 1)`, and the cell `(2, 0)` has none; its branches toward `(2, 2)` and toward `(0, 0)` are paths of two cells, exchanged by an automorphism of `B`.

**The heat trace.** By Equation (3), `A` and `B` have the same heat trace at every `α > 0` and `t > 0`. At `t = 1`:

| `α` | `Tr exp(−L^α)`, for `A` and for `B` |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 4.24861 |
| 1.0 | 4.00807 |
| 2.0 | 4.06801 |

**The diagonal at the tallest cells.** At `t = 1`, Equation (2) at the spike tips `(3, 1)` and `(6, 1)` of `A` and at the tower tip `(2, 2)` of `B`:

```
α      A: (3,1)   A: (6,1)   A mean     B: (2,2)   B − A mean
0.10   0.43314    0.43403    0.43359    0.43820    0.00462
0.25   0.44454    0.44646    0.44550    0.45662    0.01112
0.50   0.46229    0.46464    0.46347    0.48331    0.01984
0.75   0.47867    0.48010    0.47939    0.50479    0.02540
1.00   0.49402    0.49405    0.49403    0.52242    0.02839
1.50   0.52056    0.51941    0.51998    0.55123    0.03125
2.00   0.54009    0.54219    0.54114    0.57401    0.03287
```

The entry at `B`'s tower tip exceeds both of `A`'s spike tips at every `α` in the table, and the difference from `A`'s mean increases with `α`. The largest diagonal entry of `B` is at `(2, 2)` and, by the automorphism, equally at `(0, 0)`, at every `α` in the table; that of `A` is at `(0, 0)` for `α ≤ 1` and at `(8, 0)` for `α = 1.5, 2`.

**The sorted diagonals.** The Euclidean distance between the sorted lists of the 11 diagonal entries of `A` and of `B`:

```
α      t = 0.5    t = 1      t = 2      t = 5
0.10   4.95e-3    6.59e-3    5.82e-3    1.16e-3
0.25   1.02e-2    1.53e-2    1.68e-2    5.70e-3
0.50   1.38e-2    2.45e-2    3.26e-2    1.69e-2
0.75   1.34e-2    2.88e-2    4.19e-2    3.19e-2
1.00   1.27e-2    3.53e-2    5.05e-2    4.45e-2
1.50   1.41e-2    5.33e-2    7.11e-2    6.29e-2
2.00   3.13e-2    7.09e-2    9.24e-2    6.16e-2
```

Every entry is nonzero, and at `t = 1` and `t = 2` the distance increases with `α`. As `α → 0`, `L^α → I − P`, where `P = (1/n)𝟙𝟙ᵀ` projects onto the kernel of `L` (the castle graph is connected), so `exp(−t L^α) → e^{−t} I + (1 − e^{−t}) P`, whose diagonal entries all equal `e^{−t} + (1 − e^{−t})/n`. Both the difference at the tallest cells and the distance between the sorted diagonals tend to 0.

## A short reproducer

```python
import numpy as np

def castle_L(skyline):
    cells = [(i, j) for i, h in enumerate(skyline) for j in range(h)]
    idx = {c: k for k, c in enumerate(cells)}
    n = len(cells)
    A = np.zeros((n, n))
    for (i, j), k in idx.items():
        for di, dj in [(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)]:
            if (i+di, j+dj) in idx:
                A[k, idx[(i+di, j+dj)]] = 1
    return cells, np.diag(A.sum(1)) - A

def return_diag(L, alpha, t):
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(L)
    return (V**2) @ np.exp(-t * np.where(w > 1e-12, np.abs(w)**alpha, 0.0))

cellsA, LA = castle_L((1,1,1,2,1,1,2,1,1))
cellsB, LB = castle_L((1,1,3,1,1,1,2,1))
assert np.allclose(np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(LA)), np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(LB)))
dA, dB = return_diag(LA, 1.0, 1.0), return_diag(LB, 1.0, 1.0)
tipsA = [cellsA.index((3, 1)), cellsA.index((6, 1))]
print(round(dB[cellsB.index((2, 2))] - dA[tipsA].mean(), 5))   # 0.02839
```

## Rectangles

`L^α` is dense for non-integer `α`, so it has `n²` entries on a castle of `n` cells. On a rectangular castle of width `w` and height `h` the castle graph is the product of two paths, and `L = L_w ⊗ I_h + I_w ⊗ L_h`, with `L_m` the Laplacian of the path on `m` vertices. `L_m` has eigenvalues `4 sin²(kπ/(2m))`, `k = 0, …, m − 1`, and the discrete cosine basis as eigenvectors, so the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of `L` are the sums and products of those of `L_w` and `L_h`, found in `O(w³ + h³)` operations. On a castle that is not a rectangle the two directions do not separate.
