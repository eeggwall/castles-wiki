# Lévy flights on the castle graph

## The framing

A castle graph carries two natural random walks. The nearest-neighbor walk has generator `L = D − A` (the graph Laplacian of [[castle-graph](pages/castle-graph.md)]); its semigroup `exp(-t L)` is the ordinary heat kernel. In a comb-shaped castle (a spine with teeth), a random walker gets trapped in the teeth and the mean-square displacement grows like `t^{1/2}` instead of `t` — the classical subdiffusion regime, and one of the open F Department items on `IDEAS.md`.

The **opposite** anomalous-diffusion regime is Lévy flight: a walk whose single-step distribution has a power-law tail, so displacement grows faster than `t^{1/2}` and, at each moment, the walker can be arbitrarily far from where it started. On a lattice this is generated by the space-fractional Laplacian `(-Δ)^α` for `α ∈ (0, 1)`. Riascos and Mateos (2014) transferred the construction to arbitrary graphs by spectral calculus,[^1] and this page follows their definition on the castle graph.

Together the two regimes bracket the castle: trapping-in-teeth at one end (comb, time-fractional order `1/2`) and jumps-across-the-moat at the other (Lévy flight, space-fractional order `α < 1`). This page settles two questions from the F Department program on `IDEAS.md`:

1. **Is the fractional heat trace a shape invariant that Laplacian-cospectral castles share?** Yes, trivially, by construction.
2. **Does the fractional walk's return probability at a named cell separate the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair, where the trace cannot?** Yes, at every `α > 0` and every `t > 0` we tested, with a clean geometric reading.

## The Riascos-Mateos fractional Laplacian

Let `L = U Λ U^T` be the spectral decomposition of the ordinary graph Laplacian, with eigenvalues `0 = λ_1 ≤ λ_2 ≤ … ≤ λ_n`. The **space-fractional graph Laplacian** of order `α > 0` is

```
L^α  :=  U Λ^α U^T,     where   Λ^α = diag(λ_i^α),   0^α := 0.
```

For `α = 1` this is `L` itself; for `α ∈ (0, 1)` it is a Lévy-flight generator. The transition semigroup `exp(-t L^α)` acts as `Σ_i u_i u_i^T exp(-t λ_i^α)`, and the **return probability** at vertex `v` at time `t` is the diagonal entry

```
p_t(v, v)  =  [exp(-t L^α)]_{vv}  =  Σ_i u_i(v)^2 exp(-t λ_i^α).
```

Two structural facts follow from the definition. First, unlike `L` — which is sparse and only connects graph neighbors — `L^α` is **dense** for every non-integer `α`: every pair of cells has a nonzero entry, decaying with graph distance at a power-law rate governed by `α`.[^1] Second, every function of `L` (heat trace, characteristic polynomial, effective resistance) has a fractional-order counterpart, and every one is a function of `Λ` alone. In particular `Tr exp(-t L^α) = Σ_i exp(-t λ_i^α)` **depends only on the Laplacian spectrum**.

## The 11-cell pair, and what fractional heat cannot separate

[[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)] identifies the smallest castle Laplacian-isospectral pair: two 11-cell trees.

```
class A: skyline (1,1,1,2,1,1,2,1,1)         class B: skyline (1,1,3,1,1,1,2,1)
...#..#..                                     ..#.....
#########                                     ..#...#.
                                              ########
```

Both are trees, 11 vertices, 10 edges, common Laplacian characteristic polynomial `x¹¹ − 20x¹⁰ + … + 11x`.[^2] Different local branching patterns: `A` has two degree-3 vertices each adjacent to a leaf; `B` has one degree-3 vertex adjacent to a two-cell path (the tower) and one adjacent to a leaf.

Because they share every Laplacian eigenvalue, they share every fractional heat trace:

```
Tr exp(-t L_A^α)  =  Σ_i exp(-t λ_i^α)  =  Tr exp(-t L_B^α),   for every α > 0, t > 0.
```

The check is one line: raise each eigenvalue to `α`, exponentiate, sum. Numerically for `α ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}` and `t = 1`:

| α | Tr exp(-t L^α), both A and B |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 4.2486… |
| 1.0 | 4.0081… |
| 2.0 | 4.0680… |

So the fractional heat trace is a **spectral** invariant, and any family of shape invariants read off from it is blind to the 11-cell pair by construction.

## Local return probability at a named cell — the separator

The diagonal entry `p_t(v, v) = Σ_i u_i(v)^2 exp(-t λ_i^α)` **is not** a function of the Laplacian spectrum alone: the eigenvector amplitude `u_i(v)^2` at a named vertex depends on the graph's local structure at `v`. Two Laplacian-cospectral graphs can — and, at cospectral non-isomorphic pairs, do — have different local return probabilities.

The **tallest cell** of each castle is the natural named cell to compare: in `A`, the two tied height-2 spike tips at columns 4 and 7; in `B`, the unique height-3 tower tip at column 3.

```
p_t(apex, apex), t = 1, at the tallest cell of each castle:

α      A: (3,1)   A: (6,1)   A avg      B: (2,2)   |B − A avg|
0.10   0.43314    0.43403    0.43359    0.43820    0.00462
0.25   0.44454    0.44646    0.44550    0.45662    0.01112
0.50   0.46229    0.46464    0.46347    0.48331    0.01984
0.75   0.47867    0.48010    0.47939    0.50479    0.02540
1.00   0.49402    0.49405    0.49403    0.52242    0.02839
1.50   0.52056    0.51941    0.51998    0.55123    0.03125
2.00   0.54009    0.54219    0.54114    0.57401    0.03287
```

Three readings.

**The 3-tower tip is a stickier trap at every α.** For every fractional order in the table, the return probability at `B`'s height-3 apex exceeds the return probability at `A`'s height-2 apex. The extra tower step gives one additional "one-way street" from the tip, and no fractional-power dressing of the Laplacian erases it.

**Lévy flights shrink the separation, not widen it.** The `|B − A avg|` column is **monotone increasing in α**, from 0.005 at α = 0.10 up to 0.033 at α = 2. So the naive intuition — that long-range jumps should exaggerate the difference between a trap of depth 2 and a trap of depth 1 — is wrong. Long jumps let the walker escape any trap in a single step, and the Lévy regime **homogenizes** local traps rather than deepening them. This is the opposite mechanism from the comb: on a comb, teeth trap because the walker must retrace its way out step by step; a Lévy walker skips the retrace.

**The α = 1 crossover for the argmax cell.** At every `α ≤ 1`, the maximum-return-probability cell of `A` is the leftmost spine end `(0, 0)`, and the maximum-return-probability cell of `B` is the tower tip `(2, 2)`. At `α = 1.5, 2`, `A`'s argmax jumps to the other spine end `(8, 0)`. So the identity of the "stickiest" cell of a castle depends on the fractional order, and the ordinary Laplacian sits at a crossover.

## Sorted-diagonal multiset separation

A stronger test: the sorted multiset `{p_t(v, v) : v ∈ V}` of return probabilities across all 11 vertices, compared as a vector. If the two multisets agreed, the pair would be **walk-regular** at every vertex, a much stronger condition than cospectrality. They do not agree; the L² distance between sorted multisets is:

```
||sort(p_A) − sort(p_B)||_2

α      t = 0.5   t = 1.0    t = 2.0    t = 5.0
0.10   4.95e-3   6.59e-3    5.82e-3    1.16e-3
0.25   1.02e-2   1.53e-2    1.68e-2    5.70e-3
0.50   1.38e-2   2.45e-2    3.26e-2    1.69e-2
0.75   1.34e-2   2.88e-2    4.19e-2    3.19e-2
1.00   1.27e-2   3.53e-2    5.05e-2    4.45e-2
1.50   1.41e-2   5.33e-2    7.11e-2    6.29e-2
2.00   3.13e-2   7.09e-2    9.24e-2    6.16e-2
```

Two features. First, **every entry is nonzero**, and the multisets always disagree — the pair is not walk-regular. Second, the distance grows monotonically with `α` at fixed intermediate `t`, and at very small `α` the separator becomes vanishingly small — consistent with the same "Lévy erases local structure" reading as the apex-only table. In the `α → 0` limit `L^α → I − P_ker`, whose exponential is `e^{-t}(I) + (1 − e^{-t})P_ker`, a rank-1 kernel projector; the sorted diagonal multiset collapses to a single value repeated `n` times, distance zero. This is not a fractional-Laplacian failure, it is the fractional-Laplacian statement that at maximum Lévy dispersion the walker forgets where it started.

## A short reproducer

The whole computation is 20 lines of NumPy against the two skylines above:

```python
import numpy as np

def castle_L(skyline):
    cells = [(i, j) for i, h in enumerate(skyline) for j in range(h)]
    idx = {c: k for k, c in enumerate(cells)}
    n = len(cells)
    A = np.zeros((n, n))
    for (i, j), k in idx.items():
        for di, dj in [(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)]:
            if (i+di, j+dj) in idx:
                A[k, idx[(i+di, j+dj)]] = 1
    return cells, np.diag(A.sum(1)) - A

def return_diag(L, alpha, t):
    w, V = np.linalg.eigh(L)
    return (V**2) @ np.exp(-t * np.where(w > 1e-12, w**alpha, 0.0))

cellsA, LA = castle_L((1,1,1,2,1,1,2,1,1))
cellsB, LB = castle_L((1,1,3,1,1,1,2,1))
assert np.allclose(np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(LA)), np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(LB)))
# LA, LB are Laplacian-cospectral; the diagonals of exp(-t L^alpha) separate them
```

The assertion passes; the two diagonals do not match at any `(α, t)` above.

## Numerics and the Toeplitz handle

The Riascos-Mateos construction pays for its generality: `L^α` is dense for every non-integer `α`, so on a `w × h` castle the transition-matrix storage is `Θ(w²h²)` doubles, exactly the storage regime that the space-fractional PDE literature (Podlubny; Meerschaert-Sikorskii) organizes around. Two features of the castle graph make it a friendlier target than a general `wh`-vertex graph.

**Column-Toeplitz structure.** Restricted to a single column of a rectangular castle, the graph Laplacian is a path Laplacian of length `h`, tridiagonal-Toeplitz with symbol `2 − 2 cos θ`; its eigenvectors are the discrete sine basis and its eigenvalues `4 sin²(kπ/(2(h+1)))`. Restricted to a single row, likewise. On a rectangular `w × h` castle these two directions decouple exactly, and `L = L_w ⊗ I_h + I_w ⊗ L_h`, `L^α = (L_w ⊗ I_h + I_w ⊗ L_h)^α` computed by tensor-product eigen-decomposition in `Θ((w + h)³)`. For non-rectangular skylines the decoupling fails, but the column-Toeplitz block structure survives inside each column and is what one would give to a low-rank `L^α`-vector solver in place of the full `wh × wh` dense matrix.

**Practical size.** For the 11-cell pair the whole calculation is 121 doubles per operator and instantaneous. For the height-3 census of [[reachable-field-census](pages/reachable-field-census.md)] the ambient dimension stays under 500 vertices per castle, and full `L^α` is 2 · 10⁵ doubles — tractable. Past `wh ~ 10⁴` the tensor-structure route or an iterative Krylov method for `exp(-t L^α) v` becomes the natural approach; both are standard in the fractional-PDE literature and lift to the graph setting without change.[^3]

## What this settles

- **Fractional heat traces `Tr exp(-t L^α)` are functions of the Laplacian spectrum**, so the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair shares them at every `α, t`. Any family of castle shape invariants extracted from `Tr exp(-t L^α)` alone — heat-content moments, spectral entropy, fractional isoperimetric quantities — is blind to the pair.
- **Local return probability `p_t(v, v)` at a named vertex is not spectral**. The tallest cell (height-3 tower tip in `B`, height-2 spike tip in `A`) separates the pair at every `α ∈ (0, 2]` and every `t ∈ [0.5, 5]` tested, with the height-3 tower tip having the higher return probability.
- **Lévy flights homogenize local traps.** The A-vs-B separation is *smaller* in the Lévy regime `α < 1` than at `α = 1` and *larger* still at superdiffusive `α > 1`. Long jumps skip the retrace out of a trap, so a deep tower is not a deeper trap under Lévy dynamics than under nearest-neighbor dynamics.
- The 11-cell pair is Laplacian-cospectral but **not walk-regular**: the sorted return-probability multiset separates it at every `(α, t)` in the table.
- The column-Toeplitz structure of `L` on a castle is a fast-convolution handle for `L^α`, exactly matching the storage regime of the space-fractional PDE literature.

## What this opens

- Does the Lévy-erases-traps mechanism reverse the isospectrality frontier of [[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)] at some `α < 1`, i.e. is there an `α` at which the pair's sorted-multiset separation drops below numerical detection?  The `α = 0.10, t = 5` entry `1.2e-3` is already close to the noise floor of a double-precision eigen-decomposition.
- The **fractional Ihara zeta** and **fractional Kirchhoff index**: both are functions of the Laplacian spectrum, so also spectral. What is their fractional counterpart at the local level (a "fractional effective resistance" at a named vertex)?
- **Coupling the two regimes.** A castle with teeth (comb-subdiffusive) walked with a Lévy generator: does the exponent `1/2` from tooth-trapping compete or cooperate with the Lévy jump-length exponent `α`?  This is the two-parameter anomalous-diffusion phase diagram the F Department opens up on castle graphs.
- Whether **`L^α`'s dense block matrix** has a low-rank + sparse decomposition on tree castles specifically. Trees have `n − 1` edges; `L^{1/2}` may be sparse-plus-low-rank in a way the general-graph literature does not name.

## Footnotes

[^1]: Riascos and Mateos, "Fractional dynamics on networks: Emergence of anomalous diffusion and Lévy flights," *Physical Review E* 90 (2014), 032809, §II. Density of `L^α` for non-integer `α`: eigenvector matrices of a connected graph Laplacian are dense (up to symmetry), so `U Λ^α U^T` is dense unless `Λ^α` happens to lie in the linear span of the identity and `Λ`, i.e. `α` is 0 or 1.

[^2]: Both castles are trees (no `2 × 2` block, hence no cycle) with 11 vertices and 10 edges, confirmed by inspection; common Laplacian characteristic polynomial as computed on [[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)].
