# Algebraic languages and polyominoes enumeration (Delest-Viennot 1984)

**Source:** `raw/delest-viennot-1984.pdf` (M.-P. Delest and G. Viennot, "Algebraic languages and polyominoes enumeration", *Theoretical Computer Science* 34 (1984) 169-206, doi:10.1016/0304-3975(84)90116-6; local copy, not tracked in git; locators are journal page numbers)
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-22
**Type:** paper

## Summary

The paper runs the Schützenberger program in reverse. Usually a combinatorial class already has a formula, and a bijection with the words of an unambiguous context-free ("algebraic") grammar explains why its generating function is algebraic. Here no formula was known: Delest and Viennot construct the grammar first and use it to find the formula for the number of convex polyominoes by perimeter.[^1] The result is `p_4 = 1`, `p_6 = 2`, and `p_{2n+8} = (2n+11)4^n - 4(2n+1)C(2n,n)` for `n ≥ 0`, with generating function `t^4(1 - 6t^2 + 11t^4 - 4t^6)/(1 - 4t^2)^2 - 4t^8(1 - 4t^2)^{-3/2}` (OEIS A005436).[^2] Section 2 is a short course on algebraic languages for combinatorialists. It uses the Dyck grammar `D → xDx̄D | ε` as the running example alongside Motzkin, 2-colored Motzkin, and even-length "Fibonacci" words, and translates each grammar into its commutative generating function.[^3]

The encoding cannot simply trace the boundary. By Boasson's lemma, no word of an algebraic language has overlapping iterative pairs, and boundary words of convex polyominoes have them. So no boundary-following code, whether in four direction letters or three turn letters, gives an algebraic language.[^4] Instead each convex polyomino is split by the vertical lines through its two extreme points `S` and `N` (the Klarner-Rivest trisection of [[klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)], made with vertical lines instead of rows; Bender's version is [[bender-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/bender-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)]). This gives a parallelogram polyomino in the middle and a stack polyomino (rotated 90°) on each side. There are three cases, depending on whether `N` lies right of, on, or left of the line through `S` (types I, II, III).[^5] Each type gets its own language:
- **Type I:** pigmented Dyck words, i.e. Dyck words with an even number of `a`'s inserted into each leg.
- **Type II:** a linear language of word pairs, recognized by a two-head finite automaton with 16 states, so its generating function is rational.
- **Type III:** a mixture of the two.

The systems have 20 to 40 equations each and were solved with MACSYMA.[^6]

The parts of the trisection each carry a classical bijection, and these are the pieces the castle wiki uses. Stack polyominoes of perimeter `2n+4` are coded by Fibonacci words, and there are `F_{2n+1} = A001519(n+1)` of them in the usual indexing `F_1 = F_2 = 1`; the paper writes `F_{2n}`, with its own `F_0 = F_1 = 1`.[^7] Parallelogram polyominoes of perimeter `2n+2` are counted by the Catalan number `C_n`. The paper gives two bijections with Dyck words: a classical one γ through 2-colored Motzkin words, and a new one β built from peak and trough heights. Under β, **the area of the polyomino is the sum of the peak heights** of the Dyck word, and its width is the number of peaks.[^8] The conclusion names the q-analog as the major open problem: put area into the perimeter count.[^9]

## Key Takeaways

- **Convex polyominoes by perimeter:** `p_{2n+8} = (2n+11)4^n - 4(2n+1)C(2n,n)`, the terms `7, 28, 120, 528, …` of A005436. The paper calls its grammar proof "the only proof we know".[^2][^10]
- **Algebraic-language method:** code each object as a word of an unambiguous grammar, translate the grammar into an algebraic system in noncommuting variables, then commute the variables. "The algebraicity has been 'concentrated' in the Dyck language": both `p_I(t)` and `p_III(t)` are rational in `t` and the Catalan GF `c(t)`.[^3][^11]
- **Boasson's lemma:** the full boundary word of a convex polyomino is never algebraic, so the coding has to decompose the shape.[^4]
- **Stacks = Fibonacci words:** there are `F_{2n+1}` stack polyominoes of perimeter `2n+4`, with GF `(1-t^2)/((1-t-t^2)(1+t-t^2)) = (1 - t^2)/(1 - 3t^2 + t^4)`, whose coefficient of `t^{2n}` counts perimeter `2n + 4`: `1, 2, 5, 13, 34, …`. The word is read along the upper boundary: each vertical step writes `a`, and each East step writes `xx`.[^7]
- **Parallelograms = Dyck words (β):** area = Σ peak heights, width = #peaks. So parallelogram polyominoes of perimeter `2n+2` and width `k` number `(1/n)C(n,k)C(n,k-1)`, the Narayana number, equal to the count of Dyck words with `k` peaks (Kreweras).[^8][^12]
- **Open problem:** a q-analog by area. At the time it was done only for stacks (Wright) and parallelograms (Pólya, Gessel, Hofbauer-Fürlinger).[^9]

## Castle tie-ins

- **A convex castle is a stack polyomino.** A [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] has a full-width base and a unimodal skyline, which is exactly the paper's stack condition (`S = W'` and `S' = E`). A stack in a `w × h` bounding box has perimeter `2(w+h)`. So Lemma 3.2 counts convex castles along the anti-diagonals of the `(w,h)` grid: `Σ_{w+h=n+2} C(2h+w-3, w-1) = F_{2n+1}`.[^13] At `n = 2` the three terms `C(4, 0) + C(3, 1) + C(2, 2) = 1 + 3 + 1` are the convex castles in the boxes `1 × 3`, `2 × 2` and `3 × 1`. This is a diagonal reading of [[convex-castle-binomial-identity](pages/convex-castle-binomial-identity.md)]. The Fibonacci GF is the perimeter-graded partner of the area-graded A001523 on [[stack-polyomino-gf](pages/stack-polyomino-gf.md)].
- **The Fibonacci word Φ is the convex-castle U/R/D word, relabeled.** Φ writes `a` for each vertical step and `xx` for each East step. The word starts with `a`, ends with `a`, and has an `xx` between the rising `a`'s and the falling `a`'s. That is the convex castle's front/middle/back structure (opens with `U`, at least one `R` at the top, closes with `D`), with `U, D → a` and `R → xx`.[^7] The word of a stack in a `w × h` box has `2h` letters `a` and `2w` letters `x`, so its length is the perimeter `2(w + h)`; the first `h` letters `a` are the steps `U`, so the relabeling is one-to-one on convex castles.
- **β is an area-preserving bijection.** It carries the parallelogram area onto a Dyck-word statistic, the sum of peak heights. That statistic is the combinatorial content of the Pólya/Gessel family on [[q-catalan-numbers](pages/q-catalan-numbers.md)]. The concept is written up on [[parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection](pages/parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection.md)].
- **Width = peaks = Narayana.** By Remark 4.4, the parallelogram polyominoes of perimeter `2n + 2` and width `k` correspond to the Dyck words of length `2n` with `k` peaks, and number the Narayana number `N(n, k) = (1/n)C(n, k)C(n, k − 1)`.[^12] The same numbers count towers: the towers on a base of length `w` with `b` blocks and `k − 1` descents (columns `i` with `c_i > c_{i+1}`) number `N(w, k)·C(b + w − k, w − 1)`, so the towers with `b` blocks number `Σ_k N(w, k) C(b + w − k, w − 1)` ([[tower-narayana-polynomial](pages/tower-narayana-polynomial.md)], [[narayana-numbers](pages/narayana-numbers.md)]).[^15]
- **The castle word follows one boundary.** Boasson's lemma rules out codes that follow the whole boundary of a convex polyomino.[^4] A castle's U/R/D word follows only its upper boundary, since the lower boundary is the straight base, and the tower grammar generates each tower word exactly once ([[tower-word-language](pages/tower-word-language.md)]).
- **Blocks are the perimeter.** For any castle, semi-perimeter = width + blocks, so Delest-Viennot's grading carries PE 502's block statistic and its parity clause. The follow-up analysis is [[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]: castles by semi-perimeter (A082582, growth `τ²`), the signed and even-block splits, and the period-6 signed convex count.

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

This is the wiki's source on convex polyominoes by **perimeter**. The other polyomino pages grade by area ([[stack-polyomino-gf](pages/stack-polyomino-gf.md)], [[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]), or by perimeter and area together through the add-a-column method ([[column-convex-polygon-enumeration](pages/column-convex-polygon-enumeration.md)]). The paper's method, coding a class of polyominoes as the words of an unambiguous grammar, is the method of the castle's [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)]; [[steep-polyominoes-q-motzkin-bessel](pages/steep-polyominoes-q-motzkin-bessel.md)] uses object grammars and q-grammars. Its closing question, the count by area, is the one [[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)] answers for castles.

**The count by area.** For convex polyominoes, the question of §12(8) is answered by [[bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes](pages/bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes.md)]. They start from Bousquet-Mélou's algebraic-language encoding (Schützenberger's methodology, this paper's program) and mark width, height and area together. The height-width formula of Lin and Chang they report refines this paper's perimeter count and gives A005436 at `x = y`. For parallelograms, β links the area statistic to Klarner and Rivest's transfer kernel `min{m, n}` ([[parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection](pages/parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection.md)], [[klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)]).

**The area asymptotic.** §1 quotes the area growth constant `γ = 2.30914...` of convex polyominoes, citing Klarner-Rivest and Bender;[^14] the prefactor is `2.9195985097` (OEIS A067675, [[convex-castle-cap-factor](pages/convex-castle-cap-factor.md)]).
