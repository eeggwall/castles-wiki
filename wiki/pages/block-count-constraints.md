# Block-count constraints

## The block-count generating function

For the towers of height at most `k` on a base of length `L`, let

```
G_{k,L}(z)  =  Σ_{towers of height ≤ k on a base of length L}  z^{blocks}.
```

Then `G_{k,L}(1) = T(k, L)` and `G_{k,L}(−1) = P(k, L)`, the tower count and the signed tower count ([[castle-notation](pages/castle-notation.md)]).[^1] A castle of width `w` and height `h` has one block more than its towers, and its towers reach height `h − 1`, so the castles of width `w` and height `h` have block-count generating function `z·(G_{h−1,w}(z) − G_{h−2,w}(z))`.[^2]

At height at most 1 the towers are binary strings and their blocks are the runs of 1s. A string of length `L` with `r` runs of 1s is fixed by the `2r` positions, among the `L + 1` gaps between and around its letters, where a run starts or ends, so

```
G_{1,L}(z)  =  Σ_{r≥0} C(L+1, 2r) z^r.                                       (1)
```

*Example.* `G_{1,3}(z) = 1 + 6z + z²`. The castles of width 3 and height 2 therefore have block-count generating function `z·(G_{1,3}(z) − G_{0,3}(z)) = 6z² + z³`: six castles with two blocks and the skyline `(2, 1, 2)` with three.

Counting the towers whose block count lies in a set `S` means extracting the coefficients of `G` at the exponents in `S`. Any `S` can be extracted coefficient by coefficient; two shapes of `S` have shortcuts:

| shape of `S` | method | examples |
|---|---|---|
| a residue class, or a union of residue classes, modulo `m` | average of `G` over the `m`-th roots of unity | even; divisible by 7; `≡ 3 (mod 4)` |
| any set, including sets with no period | dot product with the lacunary series of `S` | powers of 2; squares |
| a set closed under addition, generated by denominations `D` | indicator series of the semigroup `⟨D⟩` | `⟨5, 7⟩`; `⟨5, 10, 25⟩` |

## Residue classes: roots of unity

With `ω = e^{2πi/m}`, the indicator of `b ≡ r (mod m)` is `(1/m) Σ_{j=0}^{m−1} ω^{j(b−r)}`, which is 1 when `b ≡ r` and 0 otherwise. Multiplying the coefficient of `z^b` in `G` by it and summing over `b` gives

```
#{ blocks ≡ r (mod m) }  =  (1/m) Σ_{j=0}^{m−1} ω^{−jr} G(ω^j).                (2)
```

[[parity-via-roots-of-unity](pages/parity-via-roots-of-unity.md)] develops Equation (2).

*`m = 2`.* With `ω = −1`, Equation (2) gives `#{even} = (G(1) + G(−1))/2 = (T + P)/2` and `#{odd} = (T − P)/2`, the split of [[castle-sign](pages/castle-sign.md)].

*`m = 4`.* With `ω = i`, and `G(−i)` the complex conjugate of `G(i)` since `G` has real coefficients,

```
#{ blocks not divisible by 4 }  =  T − (G(1) + G(i) + G(−1) + G(−i))/4  =  (3T − P − 2·Re G(i))/4,
```

which needs one value, `Re G(i)`, besides `T` and `P`. At `k = 1`, `L = 6` both sides are 63.[^3]

## Any set: lacunary series

For any set `S` of block counts, let `ℓ_S(z) = Σ_{b ∈ S} z^b`. Its coefficients are 1 on `S` and 0 elsewhere, so for a polynomial `G`

```
#{ blocks ∈ S }  =  Σ_{b ∈ S} [z^b] G(z)  =  [z^0] G(z)·ℓ_S(1/z).                (3)
```

When `S` is not periodic, as for the powers of 2 or the squares, no fixed modulus `m` serves every `L`, and Equation (3) is the extraction. For example, the towers of height at most 1 on a base of length 4 have 0, 1 or 2 blocks, in `C(5, 0) = 1`, `C(5, 2) = 10` and `C(5, 4) = 5` ways by Equation (1), so 15 of them have a power of 2 as block count. With `S = {1}`, Equation (3) gives the towers with exactly one block, `C(L+1, 2) = L(L+1)/2` of them at height at most 1.

## Sets closed under addition: semigroups

The nonnegative integer combinations of a set `D` of denominations form the additive monoid `⟨D⟩`, a numerical semigroup when `gcd(D) = 1`. The coin-change series `C_D(z) = Π_{d ∈ D} 1/(1 − z^d)` counts the representations of each `b`, and its support is `⟨D⟩`. The indicator series keeps the support and drops the multiplicities:

```
I_D(z)  =  Σ_{b≥0} min(1, [z^b] C_D(z)) z^b,          [z^b] I_D(z)  =  [b ∈ ⟨D⟩],
#{ blocks ∈ ⟨D⟩ }  =  [z^0] G(z)·I_D(1/z).                                    (4)
```

Equation (4) is Equation (3) with `S = ⟨D⟩`, and `I_D` is the lacunary series of `⟨D⟩`. Using `C_D` in place of `I_D` would count a block count once per representation.

*One denomination.* For `D = {5}`, `C_D = I_D = 1/(1 − z^5)`, and `⟨D⟩` is the residue class of 0 modulo 5, so Equation (2) applies. The same holds whenever `gcd(D)` is itself a denomination, as for `{5, 10, 25, 50, 100}`, since then `⟨D⟩` is the set of nonnegative multiples of `gcd(D)`.

*Two denominations.* For `D = {5, 7}`, `[z^35] C_D = 2` (seven 5s or five 7s) while `[z^35] I_D = 1`. The largest integer outside `⟨5, 7⟩` is `5·7 − 5 − 7 = 23`, and `⟨5, 7⟩` misses `(5 − 1)(7 − 1)/2 = 12` positive integers. Towers of height at most 1 on a base of length 10 have at most 5 blocks, and of `0, …, 5` only 0 and 5 lie in `⟨5, 7⟩`, so `C(11, 0) + C(11, 10) = 12` of them have their block count in `⟨5, 7⟩`.[^3]

*Ordered representations.* The coin-change series `Π_d 1/(1 − z^d)` is the `MSET` construction of [[symbolic-method](pages/symbolic-method.md)] and counts unordered representations; `SEQ` gives `1/(1 − Σ_{d ∈ D} z^d)`, which counts compositions with parts in `D`. The two series have the same support `⟨D⟩`, so either gives `I_D`. The compositions with parts in `{1, 3, 4, 5}` are counted by `A000570`, which also counts the unique tournaments ([[unique-tournament](pages/unique-tournament.md)]) and the tree castles of height at most 4 by area ([[tree-castle-by-area](pages/tree-castle-by-area.md)]).

## Height at most 1

By Equation (1), `G_{1,L}(−1) = Σ_r (−1)^r C(L+1, 2r) = Re((1+i)^{L+1})`, which is `P(1, L) = A146559(L+1)` ([[signed-tower-count](pages/signed-tower-count.md)]). The two classes of Equation (2) at `m = 2` are

```
#{ blocks even }  =  Σ_{s≥0} C(L+1, 4s)      =  A038503(L+1)
#{ blocks odd  }  =  Σ_{s≥0} C(L+1, 4s+2)    =  A038505(L+1)  =  A^e(L, 2)
```

since `r` is even exactly when `2r ≡ 0 (mod 4)`. The odd-block towers stand under the even-block castles of height 2, whose count `A^e(L, 2)` is the `F(L, 2)` of the problem statement ([[hyperbolic-sequence-family](pages/hyperbolic-sequence-family.md)], [[oeis-height2-hyperbolic-castles](pages/oeis-height2-hyperbolic-castles.md)]). In general, the towers of height at most 1 with `blocks ≡ r (mod m)` are counted by a sum of every `2m`-th binomial coefficient `C(L+1, j)`, `j ≡ 2r (mod 2m)`; the case `m = 4` is a sum of every eighth.

At every height, the even-block condition of Project Euler 502 is Equation (2) with `m = 2`, applied to `z·(G_{h−1,w} − G_{h−2,w})`; this gives `A^e(w, h)` on [[castle-counting-formula](pages/castle-counting-formula.md)].
