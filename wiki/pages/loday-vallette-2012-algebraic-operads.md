## Summary

**Quadratic algebras and their duals.** A quadratic data `(V, R)` is a graded space `V` and a subspace `R ⊆ V ⊗ V`, and the quadratic algebra `A(V, R) = T(V)/(R)` is the free associative algebra on `V` modulo the two-sided ideal generated by `R`. It is graded by weight, the number of generators in a product.[^2] Its Koszul dual coalgebra `A^¡` is built from `V` and `R` inside the cofree coalgebra, and when `V` is finite dimensional the graded dual of `A^¡` is the Koszul dual algebra, which Proposition 3.2.3 presents as `A^! = A(V*, R⊥)`: the dual generators with the orthogonal space of relations.[^3]

**Koszul algebras.** A quadratic algebra is Koszul when its Koszul complex `A^¡ ⊗_κ A` is acyclic (3.4.7); Theorem 3.4.6 lists equivalent conditions, among them that the cobar construction on `A^¡` is then a minimal resolution of `A`.[^4]

**Generating series.** For finite dimensional `V` the generating series, or Hilbert-Poincaré series, is `f^A(x) = Σ_{n≥0} dim A^(n) x^n`. Theorem 3.5.1: if `(V, R)` is Koszul, then `f^A(x) f^{A^!}(−x) = 1`. The proof takes the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of the part of weight `n` of the Koszul complex, `Σ_{k=0}^{n} (−1)^k dim A^(k) dim A^¡(n−k)`, which is the coefficient of `x^n` in the product and vanishes for `n > 0` because the complex is acyclic.[^5] The examples are the tensor algebra `T(V)`, with `f(x) = 1/(1 − kx)` when `V` has dimension `k`, against `K ⊕ V`, with `1 + kx`; and the symmetric algebra `S(V)`, with `1/(1 − x)^k`, against the exterior algebra `Λ(V)`, with `(1 + x)^k`.[^5] The identity is a test: if `f^A(−x)^{−1}` has a negative coefficient, `A` is not Koszul. The converse fails: the book notes that some quadratic algebras satisfy the identity without being Koszul.[^6]

**Quadratic monomial algebras.** A quadratic monomial algebra `Å` has a basis `{v_i}_{i∈I}` of `V` such that `R` is spanned by products `v_i v_j` with `(i, j)` in a set `L̄(2) ⊆ I²`; `L(2)` is the complement. Proposition 4.3.4: the tuples whose neighboring pairs all lie in `L(2)` label a basis of `Å`. Proposition 4.3.5: the tuples whose neighboring pairs all lie in `L̄(2)` label a basis of the Koszul dual coalgebra, and when `V` is finite dimensional the Koszul dual algebra is again monomial, `Å^! ≅ A(V*, R⊥)` with `R⊥` spanned by the products `v*_i v*_j` with `(i, j) ∈ L(2)`.[^7]

**Every quadratic monomial algebra is Koszul** (Theorem 4.3.6). In the basis `s^k v_ī ⊗ v_j̄` of the Koszul complex, with `ī ∈ L̄(k)` and `j̄ ∈ L(l)`, the boundary sends `s^k v_ī ⊗ v_j̄` to `± s^{k−1} v_{i_1} ⋯ v_{i_{k−1}} ⊗ v_{i_k} v_{j_1} ⋯ v_{j_l}` when `(i_k, j_1) ∈ L(2)`, and to 0 when `(i_k, j_1) ∈ L̄(2)`; in the second case the element is itself the boundary of `s^{k+1} v_{i_1} ⋯ v_{i_k} v_{j_1} ⊗ v_{j_2} ⋯ v_{j_l}`, so the complex is acyclic.[^8]

**PBW bases.** Monomial algebras are the base case of the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt method: a quadratic algebra with a PBW basis has the same basis labels as an associated monomial algebra, and Theorem 4.3.8 deduces that it is Koszul. A Koszul algebra need not have a PBW basis; the book's example, from [PP05] and due to J. Backelin, has generators `x, y, z` and the relations `x² − yz` and `x² + 2zy`.[^9]

## The castle reading

A strip rule `M` on the heights `1, …, h` is a set of allowed pairs of neighboring heights ([[castle-strip](pages/castle-strip.md)]). Its **monomial algebra** is `Å` with one generator `v_a` for each height and `L(2)` the pairs that `M` allows, so `R` is spanned by the products `v_a v_b` of the forbidden pairs. Then:

- **Basis.** By Proposition 4.3.4 the words `v_{c_1} ⋯ v_{c_w}` with every neighboring pair allowed, the strips of `M`, are a basis, so `dim Å^(w)` is the number of strips of width `w` and `f^Å(x) = G_M(x)`, the strip series of [[hilbert-castles](pages/hilbert-castles.md)].
- **Dual.** By Proposition 4.3.5 the Koszul dual `Å^!` is the monomial algebra whose allowed pairs are `L̄(2)`, the pairs that `M` forbids: the monomial algebra of the dual rule `J − M`, with `f^{Å^!}(x) = G_{J−M}(x)`.
- **Hilbert duality.** By Theorems 4.3.6 and 3.5.1, `G_M(x) G_{J−M}(−x) = 1`, Equation (1) of [[hilbert-castles](pages/hilbert-castles.md)]. The tensor algebra and `K ⊕ V` are the rule `J` that allows every pair, whose strips are all skylines, and its dual `0`, whose strips are the single columns. The symmetric and exterior algebras are not monomial and have no strip rule.
- **The boundary is a pairing of cuts.** A basis element `s^k v_ī ⊗ v_j̄` is a skyline `ī j̄` cut after its `k`-th column into a strip of `J − M` and a strip of `M`, and the boundary moves the cut one column to the left. For each skyline of positive width the admissible cuts are none or two neighboring ones, which is the cancellation in the Euler characteristic of Theorem 3.5.1; [[gessel-2013-carlitz-scoville-vaughan](pages/gessel-2013-carlitz-scoville-vaughan.md)] gives the same cancellation as a proof of the Carlitz-Scoville-Vaughan theorem.
- **Not covered.** The book counts by weight only. The Project Euler 502 block count is a weight on neighboring pairs of columns, `max(0, c_{i+1} − c_i)` per pair, so the parity split of the castles is outside this duality.

The basis, the duality and the pairing were checked by execution on every rule of height at most 4 and on random rules up to height 7 ([[hilbert-castles](pages/hilbert-castles.md)], footnote 1 there).[^exec]

## Key Takeaways

- **The dual of a quadratic algebra.** `A^! = A(V*, R⊥)`; for a monomial algebra this swaps the allowed and the forbidden pairs.[^3][^7]
- **Koszul algebras have reciprocal Hilbert series at `−x`.** `f^A(x) f^{A^!}(−x) = 1`, from the Euler characteristic of the acyclic Koszul complex.[^5]
- **Monomial algebras are always Koszul,** with an explicit contraction of the Koszul complex.[^8]
