# Self-organized critical state of sandpile automaton models (Dhar, 1990)

**Source:** `raw/dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile.pdf` (text layer at `raw/dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile.txt`, extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; the PDF is a scan and git-ignored, the text is tracked). The two-column OCR scrambles the displayed equations, so equations are quoted from the page images and the line ranges point to the surrounding text.
**Publication:** Deepak Dhar, *Physical Review Letters* **64** (1990) 1613-1616; received 21 November 1989. Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Bombay.[^1]
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-27
**Type:** paper (PDF, 4 pp.)

## Summary

Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld (BTW) introduced a sandpile automaton whose evolution settles into a critical state with power-law correlations in space and time, the self-organized critical (SOC) state. Before this paper its exponents were known mainly from simulation and mean-field arguments.[^2] Dhar studies a general BTW-type model on an arbitrary finite set of `N` sites, where toppling depends on the local height and not on its gradient. He calls it the **Abelian model (AM)**.[^3] Grains are added at site `i` with probability `p_i`. Site `i` topples when its height exceeds a threshold, and toppling changes every height by `z_j → z_j − Δ_ij` for an integer matrix `Δ`. The matrix has `Δ_ii > 0`, `Δ_ij ≤ 0` for `i ≠ j`, and non-negative row sums, so no grains are created and some can leave the system. It need **not** be symmetric, and the thresholds can be taken as `Δ_ii`, so the stable heights are `1 ≤ z_i ≤ Δ_ii`.[^4]

The key observation is that toppling order does not matter. Two unstable sites can be toppled in either order with the same result, and toppling commutes with adding a grain. So the operators `a_i` ("add a grain at `i`, then relax") commute: `[a_i, a_j] = 0`.[^5] This fails for gradient-dependent rules, where condition `Δ_ij ≤ 0` is violated.[^5] From commutativity the steady state follows. Configurations split into recurrent ones, those returned to by repeated additions (`a_i^{m_i} C = C`), and transients, which have zero probability in the SOC state. On the recurrent set the operators are invertible, and the SOC state gives **all recurrent configurations equal probability**.[^6] Adding `Δ_ii` grains at `i` forces one toppling, which gives the relations `∏_j a_j^{Δ_ij} = 1` (eq. 9). Writing `a_j = exp(iφ_j)` turns them into `Σ_j Δ_ij φ_j = 2πn_i`, solved by `φ = 2πΔ⁻¹n` (eqs. 10-12). Counting the distinct solutions modulo `2π` gives the number of recurrent configurations,[^7]

```
N_R = det Δ          (13),        S = ln det Δ          (14),
```

with `N_R = ∏ Δ_ii` for directed models, where `Δ` is triangular (eq. 15). For the square lattice with free boundaries, the entropy per site is `s = (2π)⁻² ∫∫ ln(4 − 2cos θ − 2cos φ) dθ dφ` (eq. 16).[^8] A second derivation reads configurations as lattice points. Two configurations are equivalent exactly when they differ by an integer combination of the rows of `Δ` (eq. 17), so each recurrent configuration is one cell of the superlattice spanned by those rows, which has volume `det Δ`.[^9] The wiki writes this as the sandpile group `Z^N / Z^N Δ`.

The rest of the paper derives four more results:

- **Allowed configurations.** A *forbidden subconfiguration* (FSC) is a set `F` of sites with `z_j ≤ Σ_{i∈F, i≠j} (−Δ_ij)` for every `j ∈ F` (eq. 18). A configuration with no FSC is *allowed*. A recursive deletion procedure tests for an FSC; the wiki calls it the burning test.[^10] Dhar proves that the allowed configurations are closed under the dynamics and that every recurrent configuration is allowed. The converse "appears quite plausible, though a strict proof is lacking".[^11]
- **Two-point function.** Let `G_ij` be the expected number of topplings at `j` caused by a grain added at `i`. Balancing grain flux in the steady state gives `Σ_k G_ik Δ_kj = δ_ij`, so `G = Δ⁻¹` (eqs. 19-20). The flux argument does not need the abelian property.[^12]
- **Mean avalanche on the square.** For the `ℓ × ℓ` nearest-neighbour lattice, open on all four sides, the mean avalanche size (average topplings per added grain, Dhar's `⟨T⟩`) is an explicit double sum over odd `m, n` (eq. 21) and grows like `ℓ²`.[^13]
- **Relaxation.** The master equation has transition matrix `W = Σ p_i a_i` (eq. 23). Its eigenvalues follow from the one-dimensional representations `a_j = exp(iφ_j)`. The slowest relaxation time grows like `ℓ^d` (`d` the lattice dimension), much longer than an avalanche's duration, which is at most the mean avalanche size `∼ ℓ²`. Toppling only after every `N` additions speeds up simulation.[^14]

On the Bethe lattice (details deferred to later work), the probability that an avalanche covers more than a given number of sites falls like the inverse square root of that number, and the probability that it lasts longer than a given time falls like the inverse of that time.[^15] The introduction also records the competing two-dimensional size exponents `τ`, where an avalanche of a given size occurs with probability `∼ size^{−τ}`: Zhang's `τ = 2(1 − 1/d)`, and the numerical estimate `τ ≈ 1.22` at `d = 2`.[^16]

## Key Takeaways

- **A castle sandpile is an Abelian model (the wiki's reading).** On the castle graph with a sink, one cell in the sink model or the whole bottom row in the tide model, `Δ` is the reduced Laplacian `L̃`: the cell degree on the diagonal, `−1` for each neighbour outside the sink, and row sum equal to the number of sink neighbours. Dhar's stable heights `1..Δ_ii` are the wiki's `0..deg − 1` shifted by one. So the theorems here apply to castles ([[sandpile-group](pages/sandpile-group.md)]).
- **Results the sandpile pages use.** The abelian property (eq. 5), the uniform stationary state on recurrent configurations, the group `Z^N / Z^N Δ` with `det Δ` elements (eqs. 9, 13, 17), the expected-toppling matrix `G = L̃⁻¹` behind the exact mean avalanche ([[castle-avalanches](pages/castle-avalanches.md)]), and the phases `φ = 2πΔ⁻¹n`. The phases make the order of a single grain the least common denominator of its column of `L̃⁻¹`, the [[sandcastle-clock](pages/sandcastle-clock.md)] period formula (own reasoning from eq. 12).
- **Entropy is ln det L̃.** Dhar's entropy `S = ln det Δ` is, for a castle, `ln det L̃ = ln |K|` (written that way on the wiki, since `S(w, h)` is the parity term), the log of the sandpile group order that [[sandpile-census](pages/sandpile-census.md)] tabulates: tree castles have entropy 0. Eq. 16 evaluates to `1.16624`, numerically 4 × (Catalan's constant)/π. Large rectangular castles approach it per cell: `ln det L̃ / cells` is `1.050, 1.108, 1.137, 1.147` for `10 × 10, 20 × 20, 40 × 40, 60 × 60` cells above the ground in the tide model, and `0.984, 1.076, 1.121, 1.136` in the sink model with a corner sink cell (NumPy, 2026-09-27).
- **Eq. 21 checked.** Dhar's double sum equals the mean of `Δ⁻¹𝟙` on the four-side-open `ℓ × ℓ` square exactly for every `ℓ = 2..20` (`0.5, 0.8194, 1.6669, 3.4625, 6.8385, 16.9621` at `ℓ = 2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 20`). The castle rectangle under the tide, open only at the bottom, has the exact mean `h(2h − 1)/6`, quadratic in the height and independent of the width ([[castle-avalanches](pages/castle-avalanches.md)]).
- **What the paper does not contain.** The bijection between recurrent configurations and spanning trees, and the proof that "allowed" implies "recurrent", are not here. Dhar leaves the converse open, and the spanning-tree connection comes from later work with no source page on the wiki. The identity element of the group is not discussed either.

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

The model Dhar generalizes is Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld's ([[bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md)]). The sandpile pages cite this paper for the abelian property, the uniform stationary state, `det Δ`, `G = Δ⁻¹` and the burning *test* (forbidden subconfigurations), of which it proves only recurrent ⇒ allowed. The wiki's toppling at `≥ deg` with heights from 0 is Dhar's rule with heights shifted by one.
