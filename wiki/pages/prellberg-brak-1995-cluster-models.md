# Critical exponents for partially directed cluster models (Prellberg-Brak, 1995)

**Source:** `raw/prellberg-brak-1995-partially-directed-cluster-models.pdf` (scanned; its OCR text layer is `raw/prellberg-brak-1995-partially-directed-cluster-models.txt`, extracted with `pdftotext -layout`). The OCR garbles most formulas, so the formulas on this page are read from the page images; PDF page `n` is journal page `700 + n`.
**Publication:** T. Prellberg and R. Brak, "Critical exponents from nonlinear functional equations for partially directed cluster models," *Journal of Statistical Physics* **78** (1995) 701-730. Received May 23, 1994.[^1]
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-23
**Type:** paper (PDF, 30 pp.)

## Summary

**Models.** The paper treats six models (Fig. 1, §2). A column-convex polygon is generated by two partially directed self-avoiding walks with common ends, and restrictions on the two walks give the others:[^2]

- **staircase polygons**, both walks fully directed (north and east steps): the parallelogram polyominoes of the wiki;
- **directed column-convex polygons**, the upper walk partially directed and the lower walk fully directed;
- **column-convex polygons**, both walks partially directed;
- **bar-graph polygons**, the lower walk horizontal: the castles ([[castle-polyomino](pages/castle-polyomino.md)]);
- **alternating bar-graph polygons**, bar-graph polygons whose upper walk reverses direction after every horizontal step;
- **ZL walks**, partially directed walks that reverse direction after every horizontal step, counted by length and nearest-neighbor contacts.

For a polygon model, `G(x, y, q) = Σ c x^{n_x} y^{n_y} q^m`, where `c` counts the polygons with `2n_x` horizontal steps, `2n_y` vertical steps and area `m` (2.1).[^2] So `x` marks a column and `y` a pair of vertical steps.

**Functional equations (§3).** Each set of polygons is partitioned into "inflated" polygons, obtained from all polygons by adding one cell to the top of every column, which gives `G(qx, y, q) y` (3.1), and polygons built by concatenation.[^3] For **bar-graph polygons** (§3.1, Fig. 4), splitting at the leftmost column of height 1 gives five subsets and[^4]

```
B(x, y, q)  =  B(qx, y, q) y + {1 + B(qx, y, q)} qx {y + B(x, y, q)}                    (3.11)
B(x, y, q)  =  (y / qx) { 1/(1 − qx − qx B(qx, y, q)) − 1 − qx }                         (3.12)
```

Iterating (3.12) in `x → qx` gives a continued fraction for `B`. Forbidding two of the five concatenations gives the alternating bar-graph polygons, `R = R(qx) y + R(qx) qx R + qxy` (3.13). For **staircase polygons** (§3.2), splitting at the leftmost overlap of height 1 between neighboring columns gives `S(x, y, q) = {S(qx, y, q) + qx}{y + S(x, y, q)}` (3.14), which the paper notes is Eq. (6.1) of a Bousquet-Mélou-Fédou preprint derived by Schützenberger's methodology.[^5] For **directed column-convex polygons** (§3.3), a variable `μ` marks the height of the rightmost column; the functional-differential equation (3.24) becomes the pair (3.25) and then one equation (3.28) in `D(x)` that is cubic, containing the product `D(q²x)D(qx)D(x)`. The ZL-walk equation (3.29) differs from the bar-graph one only in the inflation step.[^6]

**Linearization (§4).** The bar-graph, alternating bar-graph, staircase and ZL-walk equations all have the form[^7]

```
G(x) G(qx) + a(x) G(x) + b(x) G(qx) + c(x)  =  0                                         (4.1)
```

and the substitution `G(x) = α H(qx)/H(x) − b(x)` (4.2) turns (4.1) into the linear equation `α² H(q²x) + α[a(x) − b(qx)] H(qx) + [c(x) − a(x)b(x)] H(x) = 0` (4.3). A linear equation `0 = x H(qx) + Σ_{k=0}^{N} α_k H(q^k x)` with `Σ_k α_k = 0` (4.4) has the solution `H(x) = Σ_n (−x)^n q^{C(n,2)} / Π_{m=1}^{n} A(q^m)`, `A(t) = Σ_k α_k t^k`, regular at `x = 0` (4.5). For staircase polygons, `S(x) = y(T(qx)/T(x) − 1)` (4.6) gives[^8]

```
T(x)  =  Σ_{n≥0} (−qx)^n q^{C(n,2)} / (q, qy; q)_n                                       (4.9)
```

with `(x_1, …, x_k; q)_n = Π_{m=0}^{n−1} (1 − x_1 q^m) ⋯ (1 − x_k q^m)`. Since `(−qx)^n q^{C(n,2)} = (−x)^n q^{C(n+1,2)}`, `T` is the series `J_0(x, y, q)` of [[bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes](pages/bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes.md)], so `S = y(J_0(qx)/J_0(x) − 1)`.[^9] The paper says that "this calculation easily generalizes for the other models with quadratic functional equations", without displaying the bar-graph solution.[^8] The cubic directed column-convex equation (3.28) yields to the same substitution, `D(x) = y(E(qx)/E(x) − 1)` (4.11), which gives the linear equation (4.12) and `E(x) = Σ_{n≥0} [(y − 1)qx]^n q^{C(n,2)} / (q, qy, y; q)_n` (4.13).[^10] The paper notes that for staircase polygons the form `G = y(H(qx)/H − 1)` (4.14) "can also be explained via a bijection with heaps", citing a private communication of Viennot.[^10]

**Continuum limit (§5).** With lattice spacing `a`, `y = e^{−τ}` and `q = e^{−ε}`, the staircase equation becomes, as `a → 0`, the Riccati equation[^11]

```
λ t ∂Ŝ/∂t  =  t − Ŝ + Ŝ²,        λ = ε/τ,   t = x/τ²                                     (5.10)
```

At `λ = 0` it gives the perimeter generating function `Ŝ = ½{1 − (1 − 4t)^{1/2}}` (5.12), with `γ_u = −1/2` and `t_c = 1/4`. For `λ ≠ 0`, `Ŝ = −λ t h'/h` (5.14) leads to a Bessel equation (5.15) and, with `J'` the derivative in the argument,

```
Ŝ(t)  =  1/2 − √t · J'_{1/λ}(2√t/λ) / J_{1/λ}(2√t/λ).
```

The paper prints this as (5.17) with a plus sign before `√t`; substituting (5.14) and (5.16) gives the minus sign, and only the minus sign satisfies (5.10) (checked numerically).[^11]

**Scaling function and exponents (§§1, 6-8).** The scaling function `f` of the area-perimeter generating function near the tricritical point satisfies `f' = c f² − b z` (1.10), with `b` and `c` constants of the model, and `f = −h'/(c h)` (1.11) turns this into the Airy equation `h'' − bczh = 0` (1.12). So `f(z) = −(b/c²)^{1/3} Ai'((cb)^{1/3} z)/Ai((cb)^{1/3} z)`, the logarithmic derivative of the Airy function; the paper prints (1.13) with a plus sign, and the minus sign follows from (1.11) (checked numerically). The exponents are[^12]

```
γ_u = −1/2,     γ_t = −1/3,     φ = 2/3,     ψ = 3/2                                     (1.14)
```

The paper derives them by formal perturbation of the functional equations around `q = 1` for staircase polygons, states that "applied to bar-graph polygons, this method gives identical results", and repeats the computation for directed column-convex polygons (§6); it finds the same Airy form by dominant balance for the semicontinuous staircase and directed column-convex models (§7). Its conclusion is that "all the models of Fig. 1 belong to the same universality class" (§8).[^13]

## Castles

- **Bar-graph polygons are castles.** A bar-graph polygon is a column-convex polygon with a horizontal lower boundary, a castle of [[castle-polyomino](pages/castle-polyomino.md)]. A castle of width `w` has `2w` horizontal steps, and its vertical perimeter is twice its total ascent, which is its block count ([[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]). So in (3.11), `x` marks width, `y` blocks and `q` area: `B(x, y, q) = Σ x^{width} y^{blocks} q^{area}` over the castles.[^9]
- **Small cases.** At area 3, `B` has the terms `x³y + 2x²y² + xy³`, from the castles `(1, 1, 1)`, `(1, 2)`, `(2, 1)` and `(3)`. At `x = y = 1` the coefficients are `2^{n−1}`, the castles of area `n` ([[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]). At `x = 1`, `y = −1` they are `−1, 0, 0, 2, 0, 2, −4, 2, −12` for `n = 1, …, 9`, and `E(q, z) = 1 + B(z, −1, q)` satisfies the signed equation `1 + qz(2 − E(z)) = 1/(1 − qz E(qz))` of [[castle-row-raising-equation](pages/castle-row-raising-equation.md)].[^9]
- **The closed form.** [[castle-q-bessel-closed-form](pages/castle-q-bessel-closed-form.md)] writes the castle generating function as a quotient `N/M` of q-series by a Möbius substitution of the same kind as (4.2), with `N = J_0` at width `(1 − x)u` and height `x` in that page's variables.
- **The tricritical limit and the signed count.** The exponents (1.14) describe the castle generating function near `q = 1` and the critical perimeter weight. The signed castle count by area is governed by the zero `q_0 = −0.61583…` of the denominator `M`, a different limit ([[castle-q-bessel-closed-form](pages/castle-q-bessel-closed-form.md)]).

## Key Takeaways

- **Six models, one universality class:** staircase, directed column-convex, column-convex, bar-graph and alternating bar-graph polygons and ZL walks, with Airy scaling function and `γ_u = −1/2`, `γ_t = −1/3`, `φ = 2/3`.[^13]
- **Bar-graph equation (3.11)**, quadratic in `B`, and its iterate (3.12).[^4]
- **Linearization (4.2)** of the equations of the form (4.1), with q-series solutions (4.5).[^7]
- **Staircase solution (4.9):** `T = J_0`, so `S = y(J_0(qx)/J_0(x) − 1)`.[^8]
- **Directed column-convex:** cubic (3.28), linearized by (4.11) with solution (4.13).[^10]
