# Cornerless, peakless, valleyless Motzkin paths (regular and skew) and applications to bargraphs (Prodinger, 2025)

**Source:** `raw/prodinger-2025-cornerless-motzkin-bargraphs.pdf` (text layer at `raw/prodinger-2025-cornerless-motzkin-bargraphs.txt`, extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; the PDF is git-ignored, the text is tracked). arXiv:2501.13645v1 [math.CO], 23 January 2025.[^1]
**Author:** Helmut Prodinger (Stellenbosch and NITheCS).[^1]
**Date ingested:** 2026-09-27
**Type:** paper (PDF, 12 pp.)

## Summary

The paper starts from Deutsch and Elizalde's bijection between bargraphs and cornerless Motzkin paths ([[deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin](pages/deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin.md)]). Blecher and Knopfmacher studied prefixes of bargraphs, walks along a bargraph that may stop anywhere; Prodinger studies prefixes of Motzkin paths (meanders) instead, with peaks `UD` and valleys `DU` allowed and each counted by its own variable.[^2] A Motzkin path has steps `U = (1, 1)`, `D = (1, −1)` and `H = (1, 0)` and never goes below the axis; the count is by `(n, j, k, ℓ)`, the length, the final height and the two corner counts, with variables `z`, `u` and two corner weights.[^3] Both weights 0 give the cornerless paths, one weight 0 gives the peakless or the valleyless paths, and both weights 1 give all Motzkin paths.[^4]

The method is the kernel method on an automaton. Its states come in three layers by the type of the last step (up, level, down) and are indexed by the height. Reading off the last step gives recursions for three families of height-indexed generating functions, and summing them against `u^j` gives a linear system in `F(u)`, `G(u)`, `H(u)` with common denominator `z(u − r_1)(u − r_2)`. Since `1/(u − r_2)` has no power series expansion at `u = z = 0`, the factor `u − r_2` must cancel, and setting `u = 0` then determines the boundary values `G(0)` and `H(0)`. The result is an explicit algebraic generating function in `z`, `u` and both corner weights, with a single square root `W`.[^5] Section 3 repeats this for skew Motzkin paths, which add a left step `L = (−1, −1)` with the factors `UL` and `LU` forbidden, so that the path does not overlap itself; this needs a fourth layer of states.[^6] The paper does not treat asymptotics and notes that they are driven by the square-root singularity of `W`.[^7]

**The corner variables.** The paper's text assigns `σ` to `UD` and `τ` to `DU` ("we allow UD but use a variable σ to count often it occured, likewise we use τ to count the occurrences of DU"; "If σ = 0 we are in the peakless domain"), and it names its special cases by that assignment.[^4][^8] Its recursions do the opposite: an up step taken after a down step, a `DU`, picks up `σ`, and a down step taken after an up step, a `UD`, picks up `τ`.[^9] The printed series follow the recursions. In the length-4 coefficient `4 + τ²σ + 4τ` of the returning series, the path `UDUD`, with two peaks and one valley, contributes `τ²σ`, and enumeration of the nine Motzkin paths of length 4 by their numbers of `UD` and `DU` gives exactly this polynomial.[^10][^14] So in every printed series `τ` marks `UD` and `σ` marks `DU`, and each of the paper's names "peakless" and "valleyless" in §2 (L289-L294, L301-L310, L321-L324) is exchanged with the series it labels.

The paper's special cases, with the factor each forbids in the convention of its series, and the offsets checked against the OEIS:[^11][^14]

Reversing a Motzkin path and exchanging `U` with `D` keeps a peak a peak, so peaks and valleys are not exchanged by any symmetry, and the peakless and the valleyless returning paths are `A004148` at offsets one apart. The name of `A091964` agrees with the table's reading of that row.

## Key Takeaways

- **The two corners are the two factors the tower grammar excludes.** A tower word has no `UD`, which would be a block of width 0, and no `DU`, which would be two touching blocks ([[tower-word-language](pages/tower-word-language.md)]). So the corner weights count the violations of these two rules. At both weights 0 the returning paths are the tower words, `A004149(n+1)` by length ([[tower-word-continued-fraction](pages/tower-word-continued-fraction.md)]); at both weights 1 they are all Motzkin paths.
- **The valleyless meanders are a castle count.** The castles whose columns never drop by more than 1, with any first and last column, number `1, 2, 5, 12, 29, 71, 175, 434, 1082, 2709, 6807, …` for semi-perimeter `s = w + #blocks = 2, 3, …`, the valleyless meanders of length `s − 2`, the row the paper reports as "not in [10]" (checked for `s ≤ 24`).[^14] [[motzkin-castles](pages/motzkin-castles.md)] §7 gives the bijection and the peak refinement it carries; [[castle-sequence-catalogue](pages/castle-sequence-catalogue.md)] records the OEIS status.
- **The skew paths are counted by the castles by semi-perimeter.** At both weights 1 the skew paths returning to the axis give `A082582`.[^12] The coefficient of `z^n` in the paper's series is `A082582(n+1)`, which for `n ≥ 1` is the number of castles of semi-perimeter `n + 1` ([[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]; checked for `n ≤ 10`).[^14] The OEIS comment instead reads `A082582(n)` as the number of skew Motzkin paths of length `n`, for a step set with an "anti-down" step `A = (−1, 1)`, so the two definitions of length differ by one.[^13] At both weights 0, the skew paths returning to the axis number `1, 1, 1, 3, 7, 17, 41, 103, 259, …`, the constant terms of the paper's returning skew series, continued by enumeration as `661, 1701`.[^14]
- **The automaton.** Its states are pairs (height, type of last step), and the variable `u` marks the final height. The kernel method of [[kernel-method](pages/kernel-method.md)] removes the unknown boundary values by cancelling the factor `u − r_2`.[^5]
- **References of the paper.** Its reference list includes Blecher and Knopfmacher, *Prefixes of bargraph paths* (2024), the bargraph version of the problem; Prodinger, *Motzkin paths of bounded height with two forbidden contiguous subwords of length two* (2023) and *Peakless Motzkin paths of bounded height* (2025), the bounded-height cases; Prodinger, *A walk in my lattice path garden* (2023), a survey of the automaton method; and Roitner's 2020 thesis on parameters of lattice paths.[^15]

## Relation to Other Wiki Pages

Every series the paper prints agrees with enumeration. The paper adds a closed form for Motzkin meanders with both corners counted, which at weights `(1, 0)` and `(0, 0)` gives the tower words and the castles whose columns drop by at most 1 ([[motzkin-castles](pages/motzkin-castles.md)]), and an explicit generating function for the skew Motzkin paths, a second family counted by `A082582`. The tower words of height at most `k` on a base of length `L` number `(k+1)^L` ([[castle-counting-formula](pages/castle-counting-formula.md)]), a count by width; the paper counts by length.
