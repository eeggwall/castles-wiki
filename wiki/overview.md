# Project Euler 502 — Overview

> Evolving synthesis of everything in the wiki. Updated by wiki-ingest when sources shift the understanding.

## Mission

This wiki does more than record the PE 502 solution. Its purpose is to take **every thread that runs through the known solution methods — and the failed ones — and follow each into the broader polyomino and combinatorics literature**, to find the profound connections PE 502 has to other branches of mathematics, surface research topics, and give seminars and external collaborators concrete threads to pull. Each concept page is written as a thread to follow, not merely a solved step; each ingested paper is an entry point into a neighboring domain.

## Current Understanding

The wiki studies **castles**: configurations of stacked integer-length, unit-height *blocks* on a *w*×*h* grid, obeying a fixed set of placement rules (no overhangs or floating blocks, grid-snapped, ≥1 unit gap between same-row neighbors, a full-width bottom row, and a maximum height of exactly *h*). See [[castle-polyomino](pages/castle-polyomino.md)].

The general object of interest is the castle at **any** block count, counted by `A(w,h)` (all castles of exact height *h*, either parity). [[project-euler-502](pages/project-euler-502.md)] studies a special case: its counting function [[castle-counting-function](pages/castle-counting-function.md)] `F(w,h)` is `A(w,h)` restricted to an **even** number of blocks, and asks for `(F(10^12,100) + F(10000,10000) + F(100,10^12)) mod 1,000,000,007`. This wiki treats the even-block parity restriction as a special case of the broader, more interesting problem of counting all castles regardless of parity. (The odd count `A − F` is left unnamed for now — `G` is reserved for generating functions.)

**The counting problem has a solved closed form.** Castles admit three exact encodings — collected on [[castle-representations](pages/castle-representations.md)] (binary strings, integer tuples, and U/R/D step strings). The U/R/D encoding ([[urd-step-strings](pages/urd-step-strings.md)]) recasts the castle rules as what this wiki originally called a [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)] — a term we coined ourselves to reach for the concept of "Dyck-like, but with a horizontal `R` step allowed" — before we had learned that this exact generalization is the well-established construction of **Motzkin paths** (`U/R/D` = `+1/0/−1`; the formal-language reading is on [[tower-word-language](pages/tower-word-language.md)]). The two names denote the same object; Motzkin is the term outside readers will recognize, and the self-coined "Dyck grammar" is kept only because it records how we arrived at the idea. From this grammar the [[castle-counting-formula](pages/castle-counting-formula.md)] follows: an unsigned tower count `T(k,L) = (k+1)^L`, a signed count `P` that encodes the even-block rule as a −1 weight per block, and `F(w,h) = [h^w − (h−1)^w − P(h−1,w) + P(h−2,w)]/2`. This reproduces all three integer checkpoints (verified by executing the source's Python). The enumeration side is organized around the [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] class (counted by `C(2H+W−3, W−1)`) and its variations.

**A cycle-factorization reading unifies the machinery.** The integer-tuple / column-height encoding is the workhorse of a second reading built on the analogy [[permutation-cycle-castle-analogy](pages/permutation-cycle-castle-analogy.md)] (permutation cycles ↔ castle peaks). It supplies a product-form proof of `T(k,L)=(k+1)^L` (independent column heights), a [[castle-sign](pages/castle-sign.md)] `s(C)=(−1)^blocks` that makes `P` a genuine sign homomorphism (the `(T±P)/2` even/odd projector), a [[castle-foata-transform](pages/castle-foata-transform.md)] (peaks ↔ positive runs ↔ records), and a [[monotone-streak-factorization](pages/monotone-streak-factorization.md)] — the `O(L)` canonical form over which the fast algorithms (Kitamasa, [[berlekamp-massey](pages/berlekamp-massey.md)]) evaluate the trillion-scale cases. The seminar-shaped synthesis of this reading is [[castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md)]: PE 502 as the elementary `(n−1)!` cycle-count toolkit (quotient-by-rotation / canonical-form / cycle-follow) upgraded step-by-step into the sign / Foata / streak triad above, with `F(4,2)=10` hand-checked twice.

**The crux and what the parity clause costs.** The single insight that made the problem tractable — and "took years to see" — is that sibling towers in the same row never interact; this column independence (captured on [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)], and structurally a consequence of the horizontal `R` step that separates a plain Dyck word from the Dyck *grammar*) is what yields the product form. The even-block clause is "almost the entire difficulty": without it the count collapses to the unsigned baseline `h^w − (h−1)^w`, and the entire signed apparatus (`P`, the sign, Berlekamp–Massey) exists to enforce that one parity constraint.

**How the targets are actually computed.** The full solution ([[project-euler-502-solution](pages/project-euler-502-solution.md)]) proves `T(k,L)=(k+1)^L` by induction via the [[binary-string-bijection](pages/binary-string-bijection.md)] and routes each large target through the [[castle-count-algorithms](pages/castle-count-algorithms.md)]: a rational-function path (`h ≤ 15000`) with a direct or [[kitamasa](pages/kitamasa.md)] extractor, and a *k*-direction [[berlekamp-massey](pages/berlekamp-massey.md)] path (`h > 15000`). It also records what *did not* work (direct enumeration; binary strings without independence; convex-castle variation enumeration; L-direction transfer matrix as primary). The [[project-euler-502-implementation-notes](pages/project-euler-502-implementation-notes.md)] ground this in `Problem502.java` and settle a standing question: the code runs **only the recurrences** — it builds no transfer matrix, computes no `T(k,L)` directly, and enumerates none of the encodings. The representations, bijection, and grammar are the *scaffolding* that derived the recurrences, not what executes.

**Threads into the wider literature.** Three reference papers are now ingested as entry points: [[counting-horizontally-convex-polyominoes](pages/counting-horizontally-convex-polyominoes.md)] (a neighboring convexity class whose count collapses to a short recurrence via restricted-shape auxiliaries), [[column-convex-polygon-enumeration](pages/column-convex-polygon-enumeration.md)] (the add-a-column / Temperley method - the closest external framework to the castle's column structure, with the classical Ferrers/stack/parallelogram families), and [[steep-polyominoes-q-motzkin-bessel](pages/steep-polyominoes-q-motzkin-bessel.md)] (steep Dyck words and q-Motzkin/q-Bessel generating functions - the Dyck-and-GF thread into q-analogs). A fourth, [[klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)], anchors the area ladder on [[convex-polyomino-by-area](pages/convex-polyomino-by-area.md)]: every convex polyomino is a stack (a convex castle) glued to each end of a parallelogram, so convex and parallelogram polyominoes share the growth constant `2.309138...`, which Klarner and Rivest squeezed between the roots of two families of finite-kernel polynomials. A fifth, [[algebraic-languages-and-polyominoes-enumeration](pages/algebraic-languages-and-polyominoes-enumeration.md)] (Delest-Viennot 1984), counts convex polyominoes by perimeter by cutting each into a parallelogram and two stacks and coding the pieces as words of context-free languages. It connects to the castle in two places. A [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] *is* a stack polyomino, its U/R/D word is their Fibonacci word relabeled, and the convex-castle binomial summed along anti-diagonals is `F_{2n}`. Their bijection [[parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection](pages/parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection.md)] turns area into the sum of Dyck peak heights and width into the number of peaks (Narayana). The paper ends by posing the area q-analog as open, which is the Q Department's program. A second pass found that a castle's semi-perimeter is `w + #blocks`, so the PE 502 parity clause is a perimeter parity and the minimum-block theorem is the convexity-perimeter fact. Castles by semi-perimeter are the bargraphs A082582, growing like `τ²` (tribonacci squared), while the signed count grows only like `τ` ([[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]). The tower-Narayana `k` is refined by descents ([[narayana-numbers](pages/narayana-numbers.md)]). A sixth, [[bender-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/bender-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)], reads the same three-piece decomposition asymptotically: the stacks contribute no exponential growth and the parallelogram supplies all of the `2.30914^n`.

**Castle classification.** [[castle-classification](pages/castle-classification.md)] is the hub for castle typing, split into two kinds of technique. **Geometric types** ([[castle-classification-shape](pages/castle-classification-shape.md)]) are the 42 skyline predicates hydrated from `charlesreid1.com/wiki/Project_Euler/502/Castle_Types` - 7 base types from the polyomino literature (column-convex, unimodal, Ferrers, staircase, parallelogram, directed, m-disparate) and 35 proposed - organized into seven structural axes: convexity/modality (unimodal, k-modal, valley), rate-of-change (m-smooth, m-disparate, plateau-free), path-like (Dyck-path and Motzkin-path, counted at fixed height by the height-refined triangles A080936 / A097862 rather than by Catalan / Motzkin numbers), symmetry (palindromic, centrally symmetric, self-conjugate), extremum (single-summit, twin-peak, rainbow, hook), parity/area (even-area, even-peak), and value patterns (two-level, crenellated, linear). The rainbow type (heights a permutation of `{1,…,h}`) connects immediately to the [[castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md)] triad. **Non-geometric types** ([[castle-classification](pages/castle-classification.md)]) classify by something not read off the shape. **Axis 8** types a *class* by the growth constant of its count: a class is a "`<metal>` `<axis>` growth castle" (`<metal>` tracking the [[metallic-means](pages/metallic-means.md)] family, `<axis>` ∈ {width, vertical, area, block} always stated explicitly) iff its count sequence graded by that axis grows at rate `δ_a`. The width axis rests on the **castle strip** ([[castle-strip](pages/castle-strip.md)]) - a castle read left-to-right under a neighbor rule, whose transfer matrix's Perron root is the growth constant. Known members: the anchored 1-smooth height-3 strip of [[pell-castle-strip](pages/pell-castle-strip.md)] (Pell-number counts) and the tower word are both **silver width growth castles**; the height-2 tree castles are a **golden width growth castle**; the plateau-free-except-ceiling rule realizes bronze, copper, nickel, and every higher rung. The non-metallic slot names the constant directly: the **n-nacci area growth family** (Fibonacci at `h = 2`, tribonacci at `h = 3`, tetranacci at `h = 4`; [[bounded-height-castles-nacci](pages/bounded-height-castles-nacci.md)]) and the **cubic-Pisot** constants of tree castles by area (**supergolden**, **plastic-squared** `ψ²`; [[tree-castle-by-area](pages/tree-castle-by-area.md)], [[plastic-number](pages/plastic-number.md)]), with the bare plastic number `ψ` still an open slot. **Axis 9** types an individual castle by the spectrum of its polyomino graph, the concept [[castle-graph](pages/castle-graph.md)] (cycle rank = number of 2×2 filled blocks, bipartite, planar, bounded pathwidth): the **tree castle** (counts hitting Fibonacci at `h = 2`, Jacobsthal at `h = 3`), the golden- and silver-spectrum castles of [[castle-graph-spectral-radius](pages/castle-graph-spectral-radius.md)] (`φ` for the six 4-cell paths and, by Smith's theorem, for no other castle; `1 + √2` for the `3×2` rectangle and three non-rectangular castles; the signed transfer matrix's own eigenvalues are twice algebraic units and never metallic; bronze absent among 4.87 million castles), the isospectral pairs of [[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)] (smallest non-isomorphic pairs at 10 cells for the adjacency spectrum, 11 for the Laplacian, 16 for both), and the **Ramanujan castle**, defined through the spectral radius of the castle graph's universal covering tree because castle graphs beyond four cells are never regular, with no census yet. The methods-side companion is [[spectral-analysis](pages/spectral-analysis.md)], the hub for the five spectra (transfer-matrix, LGV kernel, skyline DFT, combinatorial Laplacian, Ihara zeta) and the construction-↔-spectrum crosswalk. The Hardin word identity behind the plastic component is proved on [[hardin-word-identity](pages/hardin-word-identity.md)] by an explicit change of basis between the even-sector transfer matrix and a `2m+1`-state word automaton. [[castle-snippets](pages/castle-snippets.md)] provides a living reference of short, tested Python one-liners - enumeration primitives, one predicate per geometric type, castle-graph primitives, and growth-constant / OEIS-lookup probes - as computational building blocks for exploring classifications.

**Empirical backstop and OEIS mining.** A Python reference ([[project-euler-502-brute-force](pages/project-euler-502-brute-force.md)]) confirms the whole apparatus by direct enumeration — `A = F + F_odd`, the closed form, and a third `O(k²L)` `p_signed` DP for `P` — and gives a clean column-height block-count formula `#blocks = c_1 + ∑ max(0, c_i − c_{i−1})`. It also names the wiki's most concrete research method: **OEIS mining** — sweep the brute enumerator over a `(w,h)` rectangle (even, odd, unimodal, block-count distributions) and look the sequences up in the OEIS to reveal further structure. The unimodal counts are exactly the [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] (column-convex ∧ row-convex), the class the direct-enumeration attempt failed on — a natural first target.

**OEIS mining — the outward research wing.** A first systematic pass ([[oeis-mining-pe502](pages/oeis-mining-pe502.md)]) connects the castle counts to the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences, organized in *veins*, the script-generated directory of every A-number cited across the wiki (grouped by role, with citing pages and mention counts) is [[oeis-index](pages/oeis-index.md)], and the hand-curated catalogue of castle sequences with novelty status and submission priority is [[castle-sequence-catalogue](pages/castle-sequence-catalogue.md)]. [[tree-castle-by-area](pages/tree-castle-by-area.md)] then adds four new castle interpretations at once: **A000930** Narayana's cows (`h = 2`), **A006498** (`h = 3`, golden growth via factorization), **A000570** tournaments (`h = 4`, whose match is Tetali's 1998 classification of unique tournaments in disguise - the paper is now ingested at [[tetali-1998-unique-tournaments](pages/tetali-1998-unique-tournaments.md)]), and a second reading of **A005251** for unlimited height matching the plastic-squared growth already seen in the Hardin identity. The headline results (all verified with offsets, re-checked by enumeration during ingest):
- **Height-2 castles are the order-4 [[hyperbolic-sequence-family](pages/hyperbolic-sequence-family.md)]**: `F(w,2) = A038505(w+1)`, `odd(w,2) = A038503(w+1)−1` — a new geometric reading of two isolated sequences ([[oeis-height2-hyperbolic-castles](pages/oeis-height2-hyperbolic-castles.md)]); the highest-value interlink.
- **The Catalan/Narayana thread lives in the [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)], not the convex count**: the tower block-count GF is a [[narayana-numbers](pages/narayana-numbers.md)] polynomial over `(1−x)^w`, hitting A005408/A005891/A063490/A160747 ([[tower-narayana-polynomial](pages/tower-narayana-polynomial.md)]).
- **Convex castles are binomial, not Catalan**: `C(2h+w−3, w−1)` by an independence + Vandermonde argument ([[convex-castle-binomial-identity](pages/convex-castle-binomial-identity.md)]); convex ⟺ unimodal ⟺ exactly `h` blocks.
- **Every castle has a unique convex core**, `min(prefix max, suffix max)`, and the castles over a core are a product of tower counts per plateau, `prod T(m-1, free)` unsigned and `(-1)^h prod P(m-1, free)` signed, so `F(w,h)` is a sum over convex castles of products of `P` ([[convex-core](pages/convex-core.md)]); the maximal convex minorant is not unique.
- **By area**, convex ↔ `A001523` ([[weakly-unimodal-composition](pages/weakly-unimodal-composition.md)]), valley ↔ `A332578`, non-convex ↔ `A115981` ([[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]).
- **The signed count** `P(1,L) = A146559(L+1) = Re((1+i)^{L+1})` — correcting a real/imaginary-part confusion; `P(k,·)` is a C-finite family ([[signed-tower-count](pages/signed-tower-count.md)]).
- **New sequences** (generation candidates): `F(w,3)` and its siblings ([[new-sequence-fw3](pages/new-sequence-fw3.md)]), parity-refined area sequences, `strict_valley`, tower rows `w≥6`. `F(w,3)` was re-searched against the OEIS on 2026-09-19 with no match, so it is a confirmed novel-candidate. Submission drafts are kept in `raw/oeis-pe502/` per the human-authorship rule ([[oeis-cross-referencing](pages/oeis-cross-referencing.md)]).
- **The convergents crosswalk and the k-direction collapse** ([[castle-eigenvalue-oeis-crosswalk](pages/castle-eigenvalue-oeis-crosswalk.md)]): the convergent sequences of every [[metallic-means](pages/metallic-means.md)] rung are matched to OEIS; the k-direction characteristic polynomial of `P(·,L)` is `(x+1)^L (x−1)^{L−2}` - no irrational eigenvalues at all, `P(k,L)` is a quasi-polynomial in `k`, `|P(k,4)|` = A352116; the L-direction `k = 6` eigenvalue is `2ψ²` for the plastic number `ψ`, with `P(6,L) = 2^L·A005251(L+3) + (order-4 remainder)` and a periodic Jacobi–Perron expansion; and the continued-fraction ↔ mod-p twin of [[eigenvalue-continued-fractions](pages/eigenvalue-continued-fractions.md)] is made quantitative (norm `−1` ⟺ `δ^{p+1} = −1` at inert primes). [[tower-parity-sectors](pages/tower-parity-sectors.md)] then explains the even-`k` factorization of `char_k` as a symmetry of the signed transfer matrix (split by the parity of the last column height), gives both factors in closed form (`H_d(μ) = Σ (−1)^i C(⌊(d+i)/2⌋, i) μ^{d−i}` and its Lucas companion, proved by Pascal's rule), derives `ρ_6 = 2ψ²` from `H_3` being the minimal polynomial of `ψ²` ([[plastic-number](pages/plastic-number.md)]), and identifies the even sector for every `k ≡ 2 (mod 4)` with `2^L` times Hardin's "every nonzero letter ≤ a neighbor" word counts (A005251, A202882, A203094, A203184).

**The Catalan / Motzkin / q-analog neighborhood.** The classical sequence and lattice pages are now ingested, placing the castle in its combinatorial neighborhood: [[polyominoes](pages/polyominoes.md)] (the taxonomy — Ferrers/staircase/bar-chart/column-convex/directed — which states outright that a castle *is* a column-convex polyomino), [[catalan-numbers](pages/catalan-numbers.md)] (refined by Narayana, q-deformed by q-Catalan; the reference point for "the castle is binomial, not Catalan"), [[motzkin-numbers](pages/motzkin-numbers.md)] (three-step lattice paths; its q-analog is the steep-Dyck-word count), and [[q-catalan-numbers](pages/q-catalan-numbers.md)] (whose Polya/Gessel family counts parallelogram polyominoes by area). Together these anchor the **q-graded castle count** ("find the q-equivalent") — tracked as the lead thread in `TODO.md`.

**The Dyck root and the binomial/Catalan divide.** [[dyck-words](pages/dyck-words.md)] is the conceptual root of the castle's grammar: the castle U/R/D grammar is the Dyck **first-return grammar generalized** with a third letter, and the **steep** Dyck sub-family (`S → xx̄ | xSx̄ | xSx̄S`) is counted by the Motzkin numbers (steep length-2n = (n−1)th Motzkin, verified) — the origin of the steep-polyomino/q-Motzkin thread. [[lattice-paths](pages/lattice-paths.md)] (PE 15) is where the U/R/D encoding itself comes from, and its stars-and-bars `C(W+H,H)` count is the same binomial mechanism as the [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] count. Together they frame the wiki's recurring **binomial-vs-Catalan** divide: unconstrained lattice paths are binomial, ballot-constrained Dyck paths are Catalan, and the castle sits on the binomial side — except in the [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)] block-count, where Narayana appears.

**Inversions and the q-factorial.** [[aocp-combinatorics](pages/aocp-combinatorics.md)] (Knuth's TAOCP Vol. 3 notes) supplies the last foundational piece for the q-analog thread: the **inversion** statistic and its generating function `G_n(z) = ∏(1−z^k)/(1−z)^n`, the **q-factorial** (verified). Inversions are exactly the statistic that q-grades Dyck words into q-Catalan (Carlitz) and steep Dyck words into q-Motzkin, so [[permutation-inversions](pages/permutation-inversions.md)] is the permutation-level prototype of the inversion-graded generating functions the **q-equivalent** thread would build on — and its bounded-integer-tuple "inversion table" mirrors the castle's own independent-coordinate encoding.

**The generating-function method.** Three source treatments now sit beneath the concept page [[generating-functions](pages/generating-functions.md)]: [[analytic-combinatorics-part-a](pages/analytic-combinatorics-part-a.md)] (Flajolet & Sedgewick, Cambridge 2009 — the definitive treatment, Chapter I ingested), [[aocp-generating-functions](pages/aocp-generating-functions.md)] (Knuth Vol. 1 — the Fibonacci method, linear-recurrence ⇒ rational GF, the negative binomial), and [[generating-functions-topic](pages/generating-functions-topic.md)] (Sedgewick–Flajolet/Trotter — the intuition-and-examples one, with an explicit "Application: PE 502"). The first supplies the general framing — the [[symbolic-method](pages/symbolic-method.md)]: six admissible constructions (`+`, `×`, `SEQ`, `MSET`, `PSET`, `CYC`) with a mechanical specification-to-OGF dictionary (Theorem I.1) — of which the recurrence-⇒-rational-GF story is the SEQ / regular-language case (Prop. I.2). The castle's [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)] is a recursive specification in exactly this style. The other two show the castle's `P_k = num_k/den_k` rationality and its `Re((1+i)^{L+1})` closed form are textbook operations: the imaginary-roots example `1/(1+z²) → ½(iⁿ+(−i)ⁿ)` is the very mechanism, the EGF `(e^x+e^{−x})/2` parity projector is the classical form of the castle's `(A±P)/2` even/odd trick, and residue-filtered "every 4th term" (`1/(1−x^4)`) is why height-2 castle counts "sum every 4th binomial." A direct polyomino tie-in from AC Ch. I: [[stack-polyomino-gf](pages/stack-polyomino-gf.md)] (Ex I.8, OEIS A001523) — the unimodal-skyline castle-tower sub-family with an OGF built directly by the symbolic method, no recurrence. And the seminar-shaped [[pell-castle-strip](pages/pell-castle-strip.md)] runs from an AC end-of-chapter exercise on `D(x) = 1/(1−2x−x²)` — coefficient matching to extract the recurrence, no special "base case" argument needed — through its two-atom tiling reading, to the castle strip with exactly this generating function: 1-smooth skylines of height ≤ 3 anchored at the base, counted by [[pell-numbers](pages/pell-numbers.md)] (OEIS A000129) with silver-ratio `1 + √2 = [2;2,2,…]` growth. Its pedagogical shape — a textbook exercise spiraling into an entire seminar on polyomino theory — is the wiki's target energy.

**Generation algorithms.** [[aocp-generating-permutations-tuples](pages/aocp-generating-permutations-tuples.md)] (Knuth Vol. 4) shows the castle's brute-force enumerator *is* Algorithm M (mixed-radix add-one) with all radices `= h` — the space `{1..h}^w`. This opens a self-contained seminar/research thread: **translating Knuth's generation algorithms into castle space** — e.g. a "castle Gray code" changing one column by one per step (bounded block-count/sign deltas → a loopless enumerator), with the twist of restricting to valid (exact-height, even-block) castles.

**Status.** All seven PE 502 subpages are ingested, plus three reference papers, the OEIS mining workspace, and the classical sequence/lattice/taxonomy/combinatorics pages (Polyominoes, Catalan, Motzkin, q-Catalan, Dyck Words, Lattice Paths, the general Generating Functions reference, and the Knuth AOCP notes on Combinatorics, Permutations, Generating Functions, Generating Permutations & Tuples, Binomial Coefficients, Multisets, and Multinomial Coefficients — the AOCP web the castle references is now fully ingested). Two of the latest close specific loops: [[aocp-binomial-coefficients](pages/aocp-binomial-coefficients.md)] is the home of **Vandermonde's convolution** — the identity that closes the convex-castle count — and [[aocp-multisets](pages/aocp-multisets.md)] supplies the **two-line-array cycle factorization and Foata intercalation** that ground the castle's permutation-cycle analogy at the source level. ## Open Questions

- How does the count of *all* castles (`A`, both parities) relate to the even-only count `F`? The [[castle-counting-formula](pages/castle-counting-formula.md)] already exposes both halves — the unsigned `T` and the signed `P` — so this is a matter of reading off the general problem rather than an open derivation.
- **Which castles are column-convex or horizontally convex?** *(Largely answered by the mining pass:* every castle is column-convex by construction; the convex castle is the additional row-convex/unimodal condition, counted by the binomial `C(2h+w−3,w−1)`, and by area it is `A001523`.*)* Open still: whether the castle recurrences relate to `A001169` (horizontally convex polyominoes) or the add-a-column functional equations of [[column-convex-polygon-enumeration](pages/column-convex-polygon-enumeration.md)].
- **Is there a q-graded (area-tracking) castle count, and does it land on q-Motzkin / q-Bessel objects** like the steep-polyomino generating functions? Now sharper: `castle-by-area` shows area is the natural second grading, and the [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)] block-count is already a Narayana polynomial — a q-analog is the natural next step. On the polyomino side the answer is known: once the bottoms stop being constant (parallelogram, directed convex, convex), the area generating functions are quotients over one q-Bessel series `J_0` ([[bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes](pages/bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes.md)], solved as a [[q-differential-system](pages/q-differential-system.md)]), while the castle's own rungs (bar graphs, stacks) stay below that jump.
- **The convex ⟺ valley bijection.** Convex and valley castles are equinumerous in every `(w,h)` cell (both `C(2h+w−3,w−1)`, different sets) under a peak↔valley mirror — a candidate bijection to write up ([[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]).
- **Viennot heap theory.** The [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)] is a heap of pieces; its block-count Narayana structure invites the commutation-monoid / transfer-matrix machinery — an untraced thread.
- **OEIS submissions (human action):** the verified interlinks (A038505/A038503/A146559 first, then the tower/Narayana rows) and new sequences (`F(w,3)`, parity-refined area sequences, `strict_valley`, tower rows `w≥6`) are drafted in `raw/oeis-pe502/`, awaiting human rewording/signing per [[oeis-cross-referencing](pages/oeis-cross-referencing.md)].
- *(Resolved by [[project-euler-502-implementation-notes](pages/project-euler-502-implementation-notes.md)]:* the code runs only the recurrences — no transfer matrix, no direct `T(k,L)`, no enumeration; the encodings and grammar are derivation scaffolding.*)*
