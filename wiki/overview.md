# Project Euler 502 — Overview

> Evolving synthesis of everything in the wiki. Updated by wiki-ingest when sources shift the understanding.

## Mission

This wiki does more than record the PE 502 solution. It follows **every thread that runs through the known solution methods — and the failed ones — into the broader polyomino and combinatorics literature**, to find connections between PE 502 and other branches of mathematics, surface research topics, and give seminars and external collaborators concrete problems. Each ingested paper is an entry point into a neighboring domain.

## Current Understanding

The wiki studies **castles**: configurations of stacked integer-length, unit-height *blocks* on a *w*×*h* grid, obeying a fixed set of placement rules (no overhangs or floating blocks, grid-snapped, ≥1 unit gap between same-row neighbors, a full-width bottom row, and a maximum height of exactly *h*). See [[castle-polyomino](pages/castle-polyomino.md)].

The general object of interest is the castle at **any** block count, counted by `A(w,h)` (all castles of exact height *h*, either parity). [[project-euler-502](pages/project-euler-502.md)] studies a special case: its counting function [[castle-counting-function](pages/castle-counting-function.md)] `F(w,h)` is `A(w,h)` restricted to an **even** number of blocks, and asks for `(F(10^12,100) + F(10000,10000) + F(100,10^12)) mod 1,000,000,007`. (The odd count `A − F` is left unnamed for now — `G` is reserved for generating functions.)

**The counting problem has a solved closed form.** Castles admit three exact encodings — collected on [[castle-representations](pages/castle-representations.md)] (binary strings, integer tuples, and U/R/D step strings). The U/R/D encoding ([[urd-step-strings](pages/urd-step-strings.md)]) recasts the castle rules as a [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)]: a Dyck first-return grammar with a horizontal `R` step. Its tower words are **Motzkin paths** (`U/R/D` = `+1/0/−1`) with no `UD` and no `DU` factor, the cornerless Motzkin paths of the bargraph literature; the formal-language reading is on [[tower-word-language](pages/tower-word-language.md)]. From this grammar the [[castle-counting-formula](pages/castle-counting-formula.md)] follows: an unsigned tower count `T(k,L) = (k+1)^L`, a signed count `P` that encodes the even-block rule as a −1 weight per block, and `F(w,h) = [h^w − (h−1)^w − P(h−1,w) + P(h−2,w)]/2`. This reproduces all three integer checkpoints (verified by executing the source's Python). The enumeration side is organized around the [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] class (counted by `C(2H+W−3, W−1)`) and its variations.

**A cycle-factorization reading.** The integer-tuple / column-height encoding is the workhorse of a second reading built on the analogy [[permutation-cycle-castle-analogy](pages/permutation-cycle-castle-analogy.md)] (permutation cycles ↔ castle peaks). It supplies a product-form proof of `T(k,L)=(k+1)^L` (independent column heights), a [[castle-sign](pages/castle-sign.md)] `s(C)=(−1)^blocks` whose sum over towers is `P` (so `(T±P)/2` is the even/odd projector), a [[castle-foata-transform](pages/castle-foata-transform.md)] (peaks ↔ positive runs ↔ records), and a [[monotone-streak-factorization](pages/monotone-streak-factorization.md)] — the `O(L)` canonical form over which the fast algorithms (Kitamasa, [[berlekamp-massey](pages/berlekamp-massey.md)]) evaluate the trillion-scale cases. The seminar-shaped synthesis of this reading is [[castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md)]: PE 502 as the elementary `(n−1)!` cycle-count toolkit (quotient-by-rotation / canonical-form / cycle-follow) upgraded step-by-step into the sign / Foata / streak triad above, with `F(4,2)=10` hand-checked twice.

**The crux and what the parity clause costs.** The single insight that made the problem tractable — and "took years to see" — is that sibling towers in the same row never interact; this column independence (captured on [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)], and structurally a consequence of the horizontal `R` step that separates a plain Dyck word from the Dyck *grammar*) is what yields the product form. The even-block clause is "almost the entire difficulty": without it the count collapses to the unsigned baseline `h^w − (h−1)^w`, and the entire signed apparatus (`P`, the sign, Berlekamp–Massey) exists to enforce that one parity constraint.

**How the targets are actually computed.** The full solution ([[project-euler-502-solution](pages/project-euler-502-solution.md)]) proves `T(k,L)=(k+1)^L` by induction via the [[binary-string-bijection](pages/binary-string-bijection.md)] and routes each large target through the [[castle-count-algorithms](pages/castle-count-algorithms.md)]: a rational-function path (`h ≤ 15000`) with a direct or [[kitamasa](pages/kitamasa.md)] extractor, and a *k*-direction [[berlekamp-massey](pages/berlekamp-massey.md)] path (`h > 15000`). It also records what *did not* work (direct enumeration; binary strings without independence; convex-castle variation enumeration; L-direction transfer matrix as primary). The [[project-euler-502-implementation-notes](pages/project-euler-502-implementation-notes.md)] ground this in `Problem502.java` and settle a standing question: the code runs **only the recurrences** — it builds no transfer matrix, computes no `T(k,L)` directly, and enumerates none of the encodings. The representations, bijection, and grammar are the *scaffolding* that derived the recurrences, not what executes.

**Threads into the wider literature.** Reference papers serve as entry points: [[counting-horizontally-convex-polyominoes](pages/counting-horizontally-convex-polyominoes.md)] (a neighboring convexity class whose count collapses to a short recurrence via restricted-shape auxiliaries), [[column-convex-polygon-enumeration](pages/column-convex-polygon-enumeration.md)] (the add-a-column / Temperley method - the closest external framework to the castle's column structure, with the classical Ferrers/stack/parallelogram families), and [[steep-polyominoes-q-motzkin-bessel](pages/steep-polyominoes-q-motzkin-bessel.md)] (steep Dyck words and q-Motzkin/q-Bessel generating functions - the Dyck-and-GF thread into q-analogs). Adding a column to a castle gives a Bousquet-Mélou functional equation with no derivative term, solved by her Lemma 2.3 and matching the q-Bessel closed form below; against `A001169` (horizontally convex polyominoes) the castle keeps 1 of Temperley's `k+l−1` placements ([[castle-add-a-column-equation](pages/castle-add-a-column-equation.md)]). [[klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/klarner-rivest-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)] anchors the area ladder on [[convex-polyomino-by-area](pages/convex-polyomino-by-area.md)]: every convex polyomino is a stack (a convex castle) glued to each end of a parallelogram, so convex and parallelogram polyominoes share the growth constant `2.309138...`, which Klarner and Rivest bounded between the roots of two families of finite-kernel polynomials. [[algebraic-languages-and-polyominoes-enumeration](pages/algebraic-languages-and-polyominoes-enumeration.md)] (Delest-Viennot 1984), counts convex polyominoes by perimeter by cutting each into a parallelogram and two stacks and coding the pieces as words of context-free languages. It connects to the castle in two places. A [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] *is* a stack polyomino, its U/R/D word is their Fibonacci word relabeled, and the convex-castle binomial summed along anti-diagonals is `F_{2n}`. Their bijection [[parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection](pages/parallelogram-polyomino-dyck-bijection.md)] turns area into the sum of Dyck peak heights and width into the number of peaks (Narayana). The paper ends by posing the area q-analog as open, which is the program of [[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]. A castle's semi-perimeter is `w + #blocks`, so the PE 502 parity clause is a perimeter parity and the minimum-block theorem is the convexity-perimeter fact. Castles by semi-perimeter are the bargraphs A082582, growing like `τ²` (tribonacci squared), while the signed count grows only like `τ` ([[castle-perimeter](pages/castle-perimeter.md)]). The tower-Narayana `k` is refined by descents ([[narayana-numbers](pages/narayana-numbers.md)]). [[bender-1974-convex-n-ominoes](pages/bender-1974-convex-n-ominoes.md)] reads the same three-piece decomposition asymptotically: the stacks contribute no exponential growth and the parallelogram supplies all of the `2.30914^n`.

**Castle classification.** [[castle-classification](pages/castle-classification.md)] is the hub for castle typing, split into two kinds of technique. **Geometric types** ([[castle-classification-shape](pages/castle-classification-shape.md)]) are the 42 skyline predicates hydrated from `charlesreid1.com/wiki/Project_Euler/502/Castle_Types` - 7 base types from the polyomino literature (column-convex, unimodal, Ferrers, staircase, parallelogram, directed, m-disparate) and 35 proposed - organized into seven structural axes: convexity/modality (unimodal, k-modal, valley), rate-of-change (m-smooth, m-disparate, plateau-free), path-like (Dyck-path and Motzkin-path, counted at fixed height by the height-refined triangles A080936 / A097862 rather than by Catalan / Motzkin numbers), symmetry (palindromic, centrally symmetric, self-conjugate), extremum (single-summit, twin-peak, rainbow, hook), parity/area (even-area, even-peak), and value patterns (two-level, crenellated, linear). The rainbow type (heights a permutation of `{1,…,h}`) connects to the [[castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md)] triad. **Non-geometric types** ([[castle-classification](pages/castle-classification.md)]) classify by something not read off the shape. **Axis 8** types a *class* by the growth constant of its count: a class is a "`<metal>` `<axis>` growth castle" (`<metal>` tracking the [[metallic-means](pages/metallic-means.md)] family, `<axis>` ∈ {width, vertical, area, block} always stated explicitly) iff its count sequence graded by that axis grows at rate `δ_a`. The width axis rests on the **castle strip** ([[castle-strip](pages/castle-strip.md)]) - a castle read left-to-right under a neighbor rule, whose transfer matrix's Perron root is the growth constant. Known members: the anchored 1-smooth height-3 strip of [[pell-castle-strip](pages/pell-castle-strip.md)] (Pell-number counts) is a **silver width growth castle**, and the tower word grows at the silver ratio by word length; the height-2 tree castles are a **golden width growth castle**; the **ridge castles** (adjacent columns differ in height unless both reach the ceiling `h`; transfer matrix `R_h = J − D`) realize every rung, and are the only known realization of bronze, copper, nickel, and higher. The non-metallic slot names the constant directly: the **n-nacci area growth family** (Fibonacci at `h = 2`, tribonacci at `h = 3`, tetranacci at `h = 4`; [[bounded-height-castles-nacci](pages/bounded-height-castles-nacci.md)]) and the **cubic-Pisot** constants of tree castles by area (**supergolden**, **plastic-squared** `ψ²`; [[tree-castle-by-area](pages/tree-castle-by-area.md)], [[plastic-number](pages/plastic-number.md)]), and the bare plastic number `ψ` is the smallest area growth constant of any height-2 strip rule ([[area-growth-census](pages/area-growth-census.md)]). **Axis 9** types an individual castle by the spectrum of its polyomino graph, the concept [[castle-graph](pages/castle-graph.md)] (cycle rank = number of 2×2 filled blocks, bipartite, planar, bounded pathwidth): the **tree castle** (counts hitting Fibonacci at `h = 2`, Jacobsthal at `h = 3`), the golden- and silver-spectrum castles of [[castle-graph-spectral-radius](pages/castle-graph-spectral-radius.md)] (`φ` for the six 4-cell paths and, by Smith's theorem, for no other castle; `1 + √2` for 36 mirror classes in a scan to width 10, both orientations of the `3×2` rectangle among them; the signed transfer matrix's own eigenvalues are twice the roots of an integer polynomial with leading coefficient 2, so never metallic; bronze absent among 4.87 million castles), the isospectral pairs of [[isospectral-castles](pages/isospectral-castles.md)] (smallest non-isomorphic pairs at 10 cells for the adjacency spectrum, 11 for the Laplacian, 16 for both; non-spectral sandpile invariants separate them, see below), and the **Ramanujan castle**, defined through the spectral radius of the castle graph's universal covering tree because castle graphs beyond four cells are never regular; every castle with at most 14 cells is Ramanujan, the smallest failures have 15 cells, and the `2 × 14` rectangle is the smallest failing rectangle ([[ramanujan-castles](pages/ramanujan-castles.md)]). The methods-side companion is [[spectral-analysis](pages/spectral-analysis.md)], the hub for the five spectra (transfer-matrix, LGV kernel, skyline DFT, combinatorial Laplacian, Ihara zeta) and the construction-↔-spectrum crosswalk. The Hardin word identity behind the plastic component is proved on [[hardin-word-identity](pages/hardin-word-identity.md)] by an explicit change of basis between the even-sector transfer matrix and a `2m+1`-state word automaton. [[castle-snippets](pages/castle-snippets.md)] collects short, tested Python snippets - enumeration primitives, one predicate per geometric type, castle-graph primitives, and growth-constant / OEIS-lookup probes - as computational building blocks for exploring classifications.

**Empirical backstop and OEIS mining.** A Python reference ([[project-euler-502-brute-force](pages/project-euler-502-brute-force.md)]) confirms the whole apparatus by direct enumeration — `A = F + F_odd`, the closed form, and a third `O(k²L)` `p_signed` DP for `P` — and gives the column-height block-count formula `#blocks = c_1 + ∑ max(0, c_i − c_{i−1})`. It also proposes **OEIS mining**: sweep the brute enumerator over a `(w,h)` rectangle (even, odd, unimodal, block-count distributions) and look the sequences up in the OEIS to reveal further structure. The unimodal counts are the [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] counts (column-convex ∧ row-convex).

**OEIS mining.** A systematic pass ([[oeis-mining-pe502](pages/oeis-mining-pe502.md)]) connects the castle counts to the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences, organized in *veins*, the script-generated directory of every A-number cited across the wiki (grouped by role, with citing pages and mention counts) is [[oeis-index](pages/oeis-index.md)], and the hand-curated catalogue of castle sequences with novelty status and submission priority is [[castle-sequence-catalogue](pages/castle-sequence-catalogue.md)]. [[tree-castle-by-area](pages/tree-castle-by-area.md)] then adds new castle interpretations at every height tested, all as compositions of `A + 1` into parts `{1, 3, …, h + 1}`: **A000930** Narayana's cows (`h = 2`), **A006498** (`h = 3`, golden growth via factorization), **A079816**, **A189593** and **A189600** (`h = 5, 6, 7`, strongly restricted permutations), **A000570** tournaments (`h = 4`, whose match is Tetali's 1998 classification of unique tournaments, [[tetali-1998-unique-tournaments](pages/tetali-1998-unique-tournaments.md)]), and a second reading of **A005251** for unlimited height matching the plastic-squared growth already seen in the Hardin identity. The main results (offsets verified by enumeration):
- **Height-2 castles are the order-4 [[hyperbolic-sequence-family](pages/hyperbolic-sequence-family.md)]**: `F(w,2) = A038505(w+1)`, `odd(w,2) = A038503(w+1)−1` — a new geometric reading of two isolated sequences ([[oeis-height2-hyperbolic-castles](pages/oeis-height2-hyperbolic-castles.md)]).
- **The Catalan/Narayana thread lives in the [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)], not the convex count**: the tower block-count GF is a [[narayana-numbers](pages/narayana-numbers.md)] polynomial over `(1−x)^w`, hitting A005408/A005891/A063490/A160747 ([[tower-narayana-polynomial](pages/tower-narayana-polynomial.md)]).
- **Convex castles are binomial, not Catalan**: `C(2h+w−3, w−1)` by an independence + Vandermonde argument ([[convex-castle-binomial-identity](pages/convex-castle-binomial-identity.md)]); convex ⟺ unimodal ⟺ exactly `h` blocks.
- **Every castle has a unique convex core**, `min(prefix max, suffix max)`, and the castles over a core are a product of tower counts per plateau, `prod T(m-1, free)` unsigned and `(-1)^h prod P(m-1, free)` signed, so `F(w,h)` is a sum over convex castles of products of `P` ([[convex-core](pages/convex-core.md)]); the maximal convex minorant is not unique.
- **By area**, convex ↔ `A001523` ([[weakly-unimodal-composition](pages/weakly-unimodal-composition.md)]), valley ↔ `A332578`, non-convex ↔ `A115981` ([[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]).
- **The signed count** `P(1,L) = A146559(L+1) = Re((1+i)^{L+1})`; `P(k,·)` is a C-finite family ([[signed-tower-count](pages/signed-tower-count.md)]).
- **New sequences** (generation candidates): `F(w,3)` and its siblings ([[new-sequence-fw3](pages/new-sequence-fw3.md)]), parity-refined area sequences, `strict_valley`, tower rows `w≥6`. `F(w,3)` has no OEIS match (searched 2026-09-19). Submission drafts are kept in `raw/oeis-pe502/` per the human-authorship rule ([[oeis-cross-referencing](pages/oeis-cross-referencing.md)]).
- **The convergents crosswalk and the k-direction collapse** ([[castle-eigenvalue-oeis-crosswalk](pages/castle-eigenvalue-oeis-crosswalk.md)]): the convergent sequences of every [[metallic-means](pages/metallic-means.md)] rung are matched to OEIS; the k-direction characteristic polynomial of `P(·,L)` is `(x+1)^L (x−1)^{L−2}` - no irrational eigenvalues at all, `P(k,L)` is a quasi-polynomial in `k`, `|P(k,4)|` = A352116; the L-direction `k = 6` eigenvalue is `2ψ²` for the plastic number `ψ`, with `P(6,L) = 2^L·A005251(L+3) + (order-4 remainder)` and a periodic Jacobi–Perron expansion; and the continued-fraction ↔ mod-p twin of [[eigenvalue-continued-fractions](pages/eigenvalue-continued-fractions.md)] is made quantitative (norm `−1` ⟺ `δ^{p+1} = −1` at inert primes). [[tower-parity-sectors](pages/tower-parity-sectors.md)] then explains the even-`k` factorization of `char_k` as a symmetry of the signed transfer matrix (split by the parity of the last column height), gives both factors in closed form (`H_d(μ) = Σ (−1)^i C(⌊(d+i)/2⌋, i) μ^{d−i}` and its Lucas companion, proved by Pascal's rule), derives `ρ_6 = 2ψ²` from `H_3` being the minimal polynomial of `ψ²` ([[plastic-number](pages/plastic-number.md)]), and identifies the even sector for every `k ≡ 2 (mod 4)` with `2^L` times Hardin's "every nonzero letter ≤ a neighbor" word counts (A005251, A202882, A203094, A203184).

**The Catalan / Motzkin / q-analog neighborhood.** The classical sequence and lattice pages place the castle in its combinatorial neighborhood: [[polyominoes](pages/polyominoes.md)] (the taxonomy — Ferrers/staircase/bar-chart/column-convex/directed — which states outright that a castle *is* a column-convex polyomino), [[catalan-numbers](pages/catalan-numbers.md)] (refined by Narayana, q-deformed by q-Catalan; the reference point for "the castle is binomial, not Catalan"), [[motzkin-numbers](pages/motzkin-numbers.md)] (three-step lattice paths; its q-analog is the steep-Dyck-word count), and [[q-catalan-numbers](pages/q-catalan-numbers.md)] (whose Polya/Gessel family counts parallelogram polyominoes by area). Together these anchor the **q-graded castle count**. On the polyomino side, once the bottoms stop being constant (parallelogram, directed convex, convex), the area generating functions are quotients over one q-Bessel series `J_0` ([[bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes](pages/bousquet-melou-fedou-1995-convex-polyominoes.md)], solved as a [[q-differential-system](pages/q-differential-system.md)]), while castles by area alone (bar graphs, stacks) stay below that jump. With a weight `x` per block they reach it: grading the tower grammar by area gives castles as `Π/(1 − x − Π)`, where `Π = yJ_1/J_0` is the Bousquet-Mélou-Fédou parallelogram-polyomino generating function ([[castle-q-bessel-closed-form](pages/castle-q-bessel-closed-form.md)]). The Motzkin connection is exact ([[motzkin-castles](pages/motzkin-castles.md)]): every castle *is* a cornerless Motzkin path, which is Deutsch and Elizalde's bargraph bijection ([[deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin](pages/deutsch-elizalde-2016-bargraphs-cornerless-motzkin.md)]), and their first-return decomposition is the tower grammar without its height bound, so the castle GF by width and blocks is their quadratic `xB² − (1 − x − y − xy)B + xy = 0` and the PE 502 sign is `y → −y`. The 1-smooth Motzkin-path castles split under the parity clause into odd and even Motzkin paths (A343386 / A107587), and the sign moves their growth constant from `3` to the Gaussian integers `1 ± 2i`. Prodinger's kernel-method follow-up ([[prodinger-2025-cornerless-motzkin-bargraphs](pages/prodinger-2025-cornerless-motzkin-bargraphs.md)]) weights the two corners `UD` and `DU` that the tower word forbids, which are exactly the castle rules (no zero-width block, no touching blocks), so it interpolates from castles to all Motzkin paths; its skew-path GF is a second object counted by A082582, and a sequence it reports missing from OEIS turns out to count the castles whose columns never drop by more than one. The Gaussian `i` behind the sign comes from the transfer matrix ([[castle-sign-kms-matrix](pages/castle-sign-kms-matrix.md)]): with a weight `t` per block the castle transfer matrix is diagonally similar to the Kac-Murdock-Szegő matrix `ρ^|a−b|` with `ρ = √t`, so the unsigned count is the rank-one point `ρ = 1` and the signed count `P(k, L)` is `ρ = i`, whose tridiagonal inverse explains the `char_k` recurrence and the parity sectors.

**The Dyck root and the binomial/Catalan divide.** [[dyck-words](pages/dyck-words.md)] is the conceptual root of the castle's grammar: the castle U/R/D grammar is the Dyck **first-return grammar generalized** with a third letter, and the **steep** Dyck sub-family (`S → xx̄ | xSx̄ | xSx̄S`) is counted by the Motzkin numbers (steep length-2n = (n−1)th Motzkin, verified) — the origin of the steep-polyomino/q-Motzkin thread. [[lattice-paths](pages/lattice-paths.md)] (PE 15) is where the U/R/D encoding itself comes from, and its stars-and-bars `C(W+H,H)` count is the same binomial mechanism as the [[convex-castle](pages/convex-castle.md)] count. Together they frame the wiki's recurring **binomial-vs-Catalan** divide: unconstrained lattice paths are binomial, ballot-constrained Dyck paths are Catalan, and the castle sits on the binomial side — except in the [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)] block-count, where Narayana appears.

**Inversions and the q-factorial.** [[aocp-combinatorics](pages/aocp-combinatorics.md)] (Knuth's TAOCP Vol. 3 notes) supplies the **inversion** statistic and its generating function `G_n(z) = ∏(1−z^k)/(1−z)^n`, the **q-factorial** (verified). Inversions are the statistic that q-grades Dyck words into q-Catalan (Carlitz) and steep Dyck words into q-Motzkin, so [[permutation-inversions](pages/permutation-inversions.md)] is the permutation-level prototype of the inversion-graded generating functions the **q-equivalent** thread would build on — and its bounded-integer-tuple "inversion table" mirrors the castle's own independent-coordinate encoding.

**The generating-function method.** Three source treatments sit beneath the concept page [[generating-functions](pages/generating-functions.md)]: [[analytic-combinatorics-ch1-ogfs](pages/analytic-combinatorics-ch1-ogfs.md)] (Flajolet & Sedgewick, Cambridge 2009, Chapter I), [[aocp-generating-functions](pages/aocp-generating-functions.md)] (Knuth Vol. 1 — the Fibonacci method, linear-recurrence ⇒ rational GF, the negative binomial), and [[generating-functions-topic](pages/generating-functions-topic.md)] (Sedgewick–Flajolet/Trotter — the intuition-and-examples one, with an explicit "Application: PE 502"). The first supplies the general framing — the [[symbolic-method](pages/symbolic-method.md)]: six admissible constructions (`+`, `×`, `SEQ`, `MSET`, `PSET`, `CYC`) with a mechanical specification-to-OGF dictionary (Theorem I.1) — of which the recurrence-⇒-rational-GF story is the SEQ / regular-language case (Prop. I.2). The castle's [[generalized-dyck-grammar](pages/generalized-dyck-grammar.md)] is a recursive specification in exactly this style. The other two show the castle's `P_k = num_k/den_k` rationality and its `Re((1+i)^{L+1})` closed form are textbook operations: the imaginary-roots example `1/(1+z²) → ½(iⁿ+(−i)ⁿ)` is the mechanism, the EGF `(e^x+e^{−x})/2` parity projector is the classical form of the castle's `(A±P)/2` even/odd trick, and residue-filtered "every 4th term" (`1/(1−x^4)`) is why height-2 castle counts "sum every 4th binomial." A direct polyomino tie-in from AC Ch. I: [[stack-polyomino-gf](pages/stack-polyomino-gf.md)] (Ex I.8, OEIS A001523) — the unimodal-skyline castle-tower sub-family with an OGF built directly by the symbolic method, no recurrence. And the seminar-shaped [[pell-castle-strip](pages/pell-castle-strip.md)] runs from an AC end-of-chapter exercise on `D(x) = 1/(1−2x−x²)` — coefficient matching to extract the recurrence, no special "base case" argument needed — through its two-atom tiling reading, to the castle strip with exactly this generating function: 1-smooth skylines of height ≤ 3 anchored at the base, counted by [[pell-numbers](pages/pell-numbers.md)] (OEIS A000129) with silver-ratio `1 + √2 = [2;2,2,…]` growth.

**Generation algorithms.** [[aocp-generating-permutations-tuples](pages/aocp-generating-permutations-tuples.md)] (Knuth Vol. 4) shows the castle's brute-force enumerator *is* Algorithm M (mixed-radix add-one) with all radices `= h` — the space `{1..h}^w`. Translating Knuth's generation algorithms into castle space gives the [[castle-gray-code](pages/castle-gray-code.md)]: a reflected Gray code on `{1..h}^w` that changes one column by one per step, so `#blocks` moves by at most 1 and the sign updates in O(1), a loopless enumerator over the cube with the castle filters applied at emit. Whether a Gray tour exists on the valid castles alone is open ([[castle-native-gray-tour](pages/castle-native-gray-tour.md)]). The random counterpart is [[castle-samplers](pages/castle-samplers.md)]: ten samplers, from rejection and an exact transfer-matrix sampler (`O(wh)` per castle) to coupling from the past, Wilson's spanning-tree algorithm and Wang-Landau, each checked by execution. The valid set falls into 191 pieces under single-column `±1` moves at `(8, 4)`, so the Markov-chain samplers run on the whole cube and filter at emit, as the Gray walk does.

**The algebra of `char_k`.** A ring-theory exercise book ([[calugareanu-hamburg-exercises-basic-ring-theory](pages/calugareanu-hamburg-exercises-basic-ring-theory.md)], Chapters 17, 14, 13, 12, 5 and 4) supplies textbook proofs for the castle ring `F_p[x]/(char_k)`. The [[chinese-remainder-theorem](pages/chinese-remainder-theorem.md)] for comaximal ideals is what splits that ring along the factors of `char_k mod p`, and [[idempotent-decomposition](pages/idempotent-decomposition.md)] makes the split explicit: there are `2^r` idempotents for `r` distinct factors, and at `k = 2` the one for the dominant root is `(x² − x + 2)/4`. Eisenstein's criterion applied to `char_k` gives [[char-k-eisenstein-at-two](pages/char-k-eisenstein-at-two.md)]: after rescaling `x = 2y`, `char_k` is Eisenstein at 2 exactly when `k + 1` is a power of 2, so `char_{2^m−1}` is irreducible over `Q`. Chapter 14 adds the over-`Q` view: for even `k`, `Q[x]/(char_k)` is a product of two number fields whose sector factors have resultant `2^{k(k+2)/4}` (checked `k ≤ 30`), so the split already holds over `Z[1/2]`; mod 2 every `char_k` collapses to `x^{k+1}` or `x^k(x+1)`. Chapter 13 adds the geometric picture, [[castle-ring-spectrum](pages/castle-ring-spectrum.md)]: `Spec Z[x]/(char_k)` over `Spec Z`, whose fibers are the mod-`p` factorizations, whose even-`k` components (the sectors) meet only at `(2, x)`, and whose nilradicals at discriminant primes carry exactly the extra `p` in the periods. Chapter 12 adds that a fiber is semisimple exactly when it is not fat, and that `Z[x]/(char_k)` itself has no idempotents besides `0, 1`: the mod-`p` splittings do not lift to the integers, and the sector idempotent needs a denominator of exactly `2^{v_2(k!) + 1}` (checked `k ≤ 40`). Chapter 5 adds Frobenius: on each fiber `a ↦ a^p` is linear, with full rank exactly on non-fat fibers and a fixed space whose dimension is the number of points, so a fiber's shape can be read without factoring. Chapter 4 locates the obstructions between `Z[x]/(char_k)` and its fibers: the inverse of `x` and the parity-sector idempotent need exactly `1/2`, the other fiber idempotents do not lift even over `Z[1/2]`, and nilpotents appear at the discriminant primes. It also asks which ring a quadratic root generates: `char_4`'s minor sector gives the non-maximal `Z[√−3]`, and copper `φ³` generates `Z[√5]`, index 2 in `Z[φ]`. The seminar walk-through is [[castle-fibers-char-2-walkthrough](pages/castle-fibers-char-2-walkthrough.md)].

**Sandcastles.** The abelian sandpile on a castle ([[sandpile-group](pages/sandpile-group.md)]; the ingested sources are the 1988 paper of Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld ([[bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md)]), Dhar's 1990 theory of the abelian model ([[dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile](pages/dhar-1990-self-organized-critical-sandpile.md)]), [[rossin-2000-group-of-a-sandpile](pages/rossin-2000-group-of-a-sandpile.md)], Rossin's 2000 Algorithms Seminar talk, and [[dhar-ruelle-sen-verma-1995-algebraic-aspects](pages/dhar-ruelle-sen-verma-1995-algebraic-aspects.md)], whose open-boundary square grids are the duals of the square castles and whose groups match the wiki's census) comes in two models, treated separately: the sink model, with one sink cell, and the tide model, with the whole bottom row as the sink. Each gives a finite group whose order is a spanning-tree count and which is read off the castle's `2 × 2` blocks: with `4` on the block matrix's diagonal in the sink model, and `3` for a block sitting on the ground under the tide, whose cycle closes through the ground as a triangle. Tree castles have the trivial group in both. In Dhar's terms a castle sandpile is his abelian model with toppling matrix `Δ = L̃`, the reduced Laplacian of either model, so the recurrent piles are equally likely and number `det L̃` (entropy `ln det L̃`, approaching his square-lattice value `1.1662` per cell on large rectangles), the expected topplings are `L̃⁻¹`, and a grain's order is a least common denominator of `L̃⁻¹`. Chau and Cheng's *completely deterministic* sandpiles, where every grain acts the same ([[chau-cheng-1991-deterministic-soc-sandpile](pages/chau-cheng-1991-deterministic-soc-sandpile.md)]), are on castles exactly the trivial groups in both models, since every cell next to the sink has a single sink edge; Chau 1993 ([[chau-1993-abelian-sandpile-model](pages/chau-1993-abelian-sandpile-model.md)]) is recorded for its framing and references. The sink group depends only on how the blocks touch (the 2-wide ladders give `Z/4, Z/15, Z/56, …`, A001353, either way up), and the census of all 33,150 castles to 16 cells ([[sandpile-census](pages/sandpile-census.md)]) finds that it separates none of the 122 cospectral groups, because in the census cospectral castles always share their graph of blocks. The tide group also depends on how the blocks stand (ladders lying down give the even Fibonacci numbers, standing up A001835) and depends only on the castle's runs of raised columns. Dynamic invariants separate more: in the sink model the clock spectrum ([[sandcastle-clock](pages/sandcastle-clock.md)]) separates 62 of 105 adjacency- and 5 of 17 Laplacian-cospectral groups and the sink avalanche profile ([[sandpile-identity](pages/sandpile-identity.md)]) separates all of them, while under the tide the clock has no sink to choose and loses its mirror dependence except through ties. The mean avalanche is exact by Dhar's theorem in both models; under the tide it depends only on height for rectangles and battlements and the blocks set only the tail, while with one sink cell the blocks lower the mean ([[castle-avalanches](pages/castle-avalanches.md)]).

## Open Questions

- **Is `char_k` irreducible over `Q` for every odd `k`?** SymPy says yes through `k = 31`; Eisenstein at 2 proves it only for `k = 2^m − 1` ([[char-k-eisenstein-at-two](pages/char-k-eisenstein-at-two.md)]). `k = 5` is the smallest open case.
- **The convex ⟺ valley bijection.** Convex and valley castles are equinumerous in every `(w,h)` cell (both `C(2h+w−3,w−1)`, different sets), but no explicit bijection is known; complementing the heights does not keep the maximum at `h` ([[castle-by-area](pages/castle-by-area.md)]).
- **Is the sink avalanche profile a complete invariant of castle graphs?** It separates every cospectral group to 16 cells, and no two non-isomorphic castle graphs share one up to 13 cells ([[sandpile-identity](pages/sandpile-identity.md)]). Related: do two cospectral castles with different block graphs exist ([[sandpile-census](pages/sandpile-census.md)])?
- **A heap-theoretic proof of the Narayana numerator.** The [[tower-heap](pages/tower-heap.md)] is a Viennot heap of pieces ([[viennot-heap-tower](pages/viennot-heap-tower.md)]): Cartier-Foata inversion recovers `1/(1−x)^w` for cell count and the transfer matrix gives `Narayana_w(x)/(1−x)^w` for block count, but the naive block-piece basis does not reproduce the tower count.
- **OEIS submissions (human action):** the height-2 comments on A038505 and A038503 are OEIS draft edits (entered 2026-09-18, proposed 2026-09-26) awaiting editor approval, and A146559 carries the approved identity `a(n) = A038503(n) − A038505(n)`; the remaining interlinks (A146559's `a(n) = P(1, n−1)`, then the tower/Narayana rows) and new sequences (`F(w,3)`, parity-refined area sequences, `strict_valley`, tower rows `w≥6`) are drafted in `raw/oeis-pe502/` for human rewording and signing per [[oeis-cross-referencing](pages/oeis-cross-referencing.md)].
