Entries name the F page they came out of and say what is verified versus conjectured. Scripts are the `scratch-fmemo-*.py` files at the repo root (untracked) and the page scripts described in each page's execution footnote. Plain hyphens. Repo-root file like `TODO.md`, not a wiki page.

Legend: `[v]` verified by execution, `[c]` conjecture or reading, `[q]` open question, `[x]` tested and refuted.

## Compression

- `[v]` **The fractional difference is a prediction residual, and a lossless one.** For `0 < alpha < 1` every Grunwald-Letnikov weight past the first is negative and they sum to `-1`, so `Delta^alpha c_i = c_i - (power-law weighted mean of the columns to the left)`. In generating functions the residual sequence is `(1 - x)^alpha C(x)`, and `(1 - x)^{-alpha}` inverts it exactly (roundtrip error `10^{-15}`). The residual sequence is a re-encoding of the skyline; only the positive part in `B_alpha` loses information.
- `[v]` **The L1 residual argmin recovers the integration order.** `sum_i |Delta^alpha c_i|` is the L1 cost after ARFIMA fractional differencing by `(1 - B)^alpha`. On centred width-64 skylines the argmin is `0.00` for i.i.d. heights, `1.00` for a 1-smooth random walk, `2.00` for a ramp, `0.48` for fractionally integrated noise with `d = 0.3` (biased up, as an L1 argmin on 64 samples is).
- `[x]` **On real audio the continuous rung never wins.** Fixed fractional predictor (32 taps of `-w_k(alpha)`, Q12, Rice residual, 4096-sample blocks) on the song-as-castle file `Funk.aiff`, both channels: `alpha = 0`: 30.7%, `0.5`: 23.5%, `1`: 16.9%, `1.5`: 15.2%, `1.9`: 13.6%, **`2.0`: 11.6%**, `2.1`: 14.0%. The minimum is sharp and at the integer. Per-block choice of `alpha` from a grid `0..3` step `0.1` gives 10.0% and the chosen orders are `{0: 16, 1: 17, 2: 13, 3: 6}` blocks: no block ever picks a non-integer order. Reference points from the same coder: least-squares LPC orders 1/2/4/8 at 18.4/13.2/12.7/12.8%, FLAC fixed predictors orders 0-3 at 30.7/16.9/11.6/12.1%. (The song page's 9.5% for LPC-8 came from a different coder; the comparison here is internal.) The reason: integer orders have integer taps with finite support, so no quantization noise and no power-law tail dragging distant samples into the prediction.
- `[v]` **Where the fractional rung does pay: long-memory sources.** On synthetic ARFIMA skylines of `2^16` samples the best order tracks `d` (`d = 0.3 -> 0.3`, `0.5 -> 0.6`, `0.7 -> 0.7`) and beats the best integer order by 1.6%, 1.4%, 0.5%. At `d = 0` and `d >= 1` the integer order wins outright. The fractional predictor is a codec for `1/f`-type sources, not for music with local polynomial structure. The Hurst item of the F Division should look at image rows and textures, not waveforms.
- `[c]` **`log_2 F(w, h)` at fractional width is the bit cost per half column.** castle-entropy has `log_2 F ~ w log_2 h - 1`; the real interpolant makes `log_2 F(w, h)` smooth in `w` with derivative the marginal cost of a column, `log_2 h` in the limit. The cosine in `F(w, 2)` makes the marginal cost at height 2 wobble with period 8 around one bit. Untested against an arithmetic coder.

## Encoding

- `[v]` **Two irrational orders are an injective encoding of a castle within a cell.** The pair `(B_alpha, B_beta)` at `(alpha, beta) = (sqrt 2 - 1, pi - 3)` takes distinct values on every castle of every cell tested: `(6,3)` 665, `(7,3)` 2059, `(8,3)` 6305, `(5,4)` 781, `(6,4)` 3367, `(7,4)` 14197, `(5,5)` 2101, `(6,5)` 11529 (to nine decimals). A single irrational order misses a few percent (tail-blind pairs); the full function on 49 orders has no collisions either. So a castle is a point in the plane, recoverable in principle from two real numbers; no proof, and the decoding is by table.
- `[v]` **`B_{1/2}` is exactly representable in binary, but is not injective and costs more bits than the cell.** Weights `1, -1/2, -1/8, -1/16, -5/128, ...` (numerators A002596, denominators A046161), so `B_{1/2}` of a width-`w` castle is an integer multiple of `2^{-(2w-2)}`. As an integer code it needs 14 bits for `(6,4)` against `log_2 3367 = 11.7`, and only 1017 of 3367 values are distinct. Exactness, not compactness, is what half order buys.
- `[v]` **The residual collisions are tail-blind pairs.** For `alpha` near 1 the statistic cannot see how a castle descends after its last positive-residual column (`(1,1,1,4,1)` and `(1,1,1,4,2)` agree on all of `[0.5, 1)`), because the block count is the total ascent and never saw the descent either. Small `alpha` or a second order lifts it.
- `[v]` **Position-aware, unlike area and blocks.** Area and block count are invariant under reversing the skyline; `B_alpha` is not (in `(5,3)` only the 19 palindromes match their reversal). First single-number castle statistic on the wiki that knows which end is which.
- `[v]` **The half-column problem has three OEIS answers, and the nonnegative one is posets.** Strip rules that are `R^2` for a 0/1 rule `R`: 8, 88, 3245 at heights 2, 3, 4 = A226321 (0/1 matrices that are squares of 0/1 matrices). Rules with a real principal square root (all eigenvalues real and nonnegative, exact): 13, 261, 15418 = A086510. Rules with all eigenvalues real and positive: 3, 25, 543 = A003024, the DAG count, so these are exactly `I + N` with `N` nilpotent. Rules whose half-column has nonnegative weights: 3, 19, 219 = A001035, labeled posets, and the sets coincide with the reflexive-transitive-antisymmetric rules exactly at heights 2, 3, 4. Mechanism: `sqrt(I + N) = sum_k C(1/2, k) N^k`, and the `k = 2` coefficient `-1/8` lands on every 2-chain `a < b < c` whose direct edge `a < c` is missing; transitivity is what keeps the root nonnegative. A weighted half-column rule exists iff the rule is a partial order on the heights (proved for `h <= 4`, conjectured in general).
- `[v]` **The half-column of the nondecreasing rule is the half-sum operator.** The chain poset (`c_{i+1} >= c_i`) is the upper-triangular all-ones matrix `U = (I - E)^{-1}`, and `U^{1/2} = (I - E)^{-1/2}` is Toeplitz with entries `C(2k, k) / 4^k` for a jump of `k` heights: `1, 1/2, 3/8, 5/16, 35/128, ...`, the coefficients of `(1 - x)^{-1/2}`. Half a column of the nondecreasing castle is the fractional sum of order `1/2`. The same coefficients give `B_{1/2}(box) / h = sum_{i<w} C(2i,i)/4^i = 2w C(2w,w)/4^w` (numerators A001803), so the box's half-order block count and the chain rule's half-column are the same numbers.
- `[c]` **A half-column rule is a rate-2 recoding of a strip.** If `R^2 = M` with `R` 0/1, every width-`w` strip under `M` is a width-`2w` strip under `R` read at even columns. The metallic strips (1-smooth, ceiling exception) admit no real square root at all (simple negative eigenvalue), so metallic-growth castle languages are already at their finest column rate.

## Encryption

- `[v]` **Area parity is a trivial clause; block parity is not.** `sum_C (-1)^{area} = (-1)^{w+h+1}` in every cell, so an even-area PE 502 would have answer `(A +- 1)/2`. The one-bit parity check that makes PE 502 hard, and that castle-steganography channel B and the in-band-signaling item exploit, is hard only because it sits at `alpha = 1`; at `alpha = 0` the same construction leaks nothing.
- `[x]` **The keyed phase `e^{i pi B_alpha}` is broken with three castles.** With `alpha` as the secret and the phase mod `2 pi` as the public check value, `B_alpha(c)` is piecewise polynomial in `alpha`, so each known `(castle, phase)` pair leaves finitely many candidate `alpha` (13, 15, 21 candidates on width-12 castles), and the intersection of three candidate sets is the secret to nine decimals (`0.618033989` recovered). A functional that is polynomial in the key is not a keyed hash; the phase is at best an integrity check with a public `alpha`.
- `[v]` **The phase sum is not a simple dephasing.** `|P_alpha|` stays near zero to `alpha = 0.25`, then grows and exceeds `|P_1|` inside `(0.5, 1)` (`(6,4)`: 157 at `alpha = 0.9` against `|P_1| = 91`). For an integrity check with a public order the flat region `alpha <= 0.25` is the one with the least bias.
- `[x]` **A fractional warden does not see channel B.** Simulation: 4000 image-like row castles (`w = 512`, `h = 256`, smooth walk with jumps), block parity set to a random bit by a one-cell edit at a random strict local extremum (2013 rows edited, parity matches the bit on all 4000). Marginal distribution of `B_alpha` at `alpha = 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1`: KS p-value `1.000` at every order; the edit moves `B_alpha` by at most `1.0` per row against a natural standard deviation of 180 to 24000 (shift/std between `3 * 10^{-5}` and `5 * 10^{-3}`). The residual at the edited column changes by exactly `w_0 = 1`, against a typical residual magnitude of `9.1`. A warden counting "barely extreme" columns (exactly one above or below both neighbours) sees 76.4 per row in cover against 76.8 in stego, KS p = 0.97. First-order and this second-order statistic both fail; channel B is safe against a warden armed with `B_alpha`. What is left for the block-counting-warden item is a warden that knows the cover, or a higher-order joint statistic.
- `[v]` **Square roots in the castle ring: the half-step law at height 2.** In `F_p[x] / (x^2 - 2x + 2)` the element `x` (the `1 + i` eigenvalue) is a square, so `x^w` has a square root for every `w` and the fractional Kitamasa step `M^{1/2}` exists for all widths, iff `p = 7 (mod 8)` when `p` is inert (`p = 3 mod 4`), or when `p` splits (`p = 1 mod 4`) and both `1 +- i` are quadratic residues (`p = 41, 113, 137, 257, ...`, about 14% of split primes below 300). Verified for every odd prime below 300. The inert law is the norm criterion: `N(1 + i) = 2` and `2` is a residue iff `p = +-1 (mod 8)`. Otherwise `x^w` has a root iff `w` is even, so half of all widths have no half-step. For `k = 2, 3, 4` the pattern over `p < 24` is mixed (tabulated in the script) and awaits the same norm analysis factor by factor.
- `[c]` **Reading for the ring item.** Over `R` the half-step exists iff no negative eigenvalue; over `F_p` iff `x` is a residue in every factor field. The real obstruction (`-1`) and the finite one (non-residue) are the same obstruction in two completions; the F Division ring page should be organized around that.
