- [x] **[Castle BDD / ZDD](wiki/pages/castle-bdd-zdd.md)** - valid-castle set `V(w, h)` as a BDD or zero-suppressed BDD of size `O(h · w · log h)`, built by lifting the [castle-strip](wiki/pages/castle-strip.md) transfer matrix to a DFA on state `(last-column height, blocks-mod-2, is-h-reached)`. ### E Department (enumeration - counting, recurrences, generation)

- [ ] **Every sign is one character theorem** - the `(T±P)/2` projector on [castle-sign](wiki/pages/castle-sign.md), the m-th-root generalization on [parity-via-roots-of-unity](wiki/pages/parity-via-roots-of-unity.md), the skyline DFT on [spectral-analysis](wiki/pages/spectral-analysis.md), and the mod-`p` period on [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md) are four uses of one theorem: characters of a finite abelian group are orthogonal and the group is its own dual; write it down once with Parseval and Poisson and re-derive the four uses as instances; the mirror `c_i -> c_{w+1-i}` does not commute with cyclic shift, so any "mod cyclic shift" invariant or "mirror-deduped" census lives under a dihedral group whose characters are not all one-dimensional.
- [ ] **Fourier spectrum of `(-1)^blocks` on `Z_h^w`** - the sign `s(C) = (-1)^blocks` on the mixed-radix space of [aocp-generating-permutations-tuples](wiki/pages/aocp-generating-permutations-tuples.md); compute its Fourier transform over `Z_h^w` for small `(w,h)`, its largest coefficient and spectral concentration, and the Gowers `U^2` linearity score; find the largest subgroup `H < Z_h^w` on whose cosets block parity is constant (the in-band-signaling edits are moves inside such a coset); ask whether the `alpha`-family `P_alpha` of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) gets closer to a character at any order (at `alpha = 0` it is `(-1)^{w+h+1}`, a character; at `alpha = 1` it is hardest).
- [ ] **General closed form for `P(k,L)`** - for all `k` and all `L`. In `k`, `P(k,L) = (-1)^k A_L(k) + B_L(k)` with explicit `A_L, B_L` for `L<=12` on [castle-eigenvalue-oeis-crosswalk](wiki/pages/castle-eigenvalue-oeis-crosswalk.md); what remains is a uniform formula and a proof of the `(x+1)^L (x-1)^{L-2}` factorization.
- [ ] **Minimality of empirical recurrence orders** - prove the conjectured orders are minimal (`k+1` in `L`, exactly `2L-2` in `k`); the k-direction half reduces to `deg A_L = L-1` and `deg B_L = L-3`.
- [ ] **Gray codes for polyominoes (Ruskey/Weston Theorem 4.4)** - Ruskey's paper on Gray codes for polyominoes: castles are column-convex polyominoes ([castle-polyomino](wiki/pages/castle-polyomino.md)), so the paper's polyomino classification and Gray-code construction map directly to [castle-native-gray-tour](wiki/pages/castle-native-gray-tour.md). Which polyomino sub-family in Theorem 4.4 corresponds to `V(w, h)` once the even-block filter is added, and does its Gray code descend to that filtered subset? Flagged on Ruskey's UVic Fall 2015 Knuth 4A class projects page (`webhome.cs.uvic.ca/~ruskey/classes/Knuth4A/projectKnuth4A_2015.html`).
- [ ] **Bent Hamilton cycles on `d`-dimensional grid graphs (Ruskey/Sawada)** - the castle cube `{1..h}^w` is a `d`-dimensional grid graph, and Ruskey/Sawada construct Hamilton cycles on `Q'(d_1, …, d_n)` with a "bent" property (bounded direction changes per axis); a symmetric cycle for `Q'(3, 3, 3)` is one specific realization. Does that construction restrict to `V(w, h)` under the proper and even-block filters, and does the bend count give a handle on the parity-flip that trips M1 at `(3, 2)` on [castle-native-gray-tour](wiki/pages/castle-native-gray-tour.md)? Same Ruskey Fall 2015 project list.
- [ ] **Antimatroid Gray codes (Pruesse/Ruskey, "Gray codes from antimatroids")** - antimatroids are a general framework for restricted-family Gray codes with a loopless ideal generator. Does `V(w, h)` form (or embed in) an antimatroid? If so, Pruesse/Ruskey transports a loopless castle-native tour into Arc 10 without the hand-picked move-set search on [castle-native-gray-tour](wiki/pages/castle-native-gray-tour.md). Same source.

- [x] **[Viennot heaps of pieces on the tower](wiki/pages/viennot-heap-tower.md)** - trace-monoid setup on `N^w` with diagonal dependency; Cartier-Foata inversion gives `1/(1-x)^w` for the cell-count GF as the formal-power-series identity `1/(1-x)^w * (1-x)^w = 1`; block count is not piece-additive under this basis, and the interval-piece coarsening (dependency = shared column) has trivial-heap count `C(w+k, 2k)` but its Cartier-Foata reciprocal `1/(1-3x+x^2) = 1 + 3x + 8x^2 + ...` (A001906) misses the tower row `1, 3, 5, 7, ...` (A005408) starting at `x^2`, so the naive block-piece basis fails; the working reading of `Narayana_w(x)/(1-x)^w` is the finite-state transfer matrix `M[a,b] = x^{max(0, b-a)}` on state space `N`, with a heap-theoretic proof of the numerator (a path-graph-dependency refinement, or a commutant of `M`) left as the open half of the thread.
- [x] **[Castle Gray code](wiki/pages/castle-gray-code.md)** - Knuth's reflected Gray code on `{1..h}^w` walked pedagogically at `(3,2)` for `h=2` and via Ives' Algorithm H (TAOCP §7.2.1.1) at `(2,3)` for general `h`; three-way split of the cube into improper ("too short"), proper-odd, and proper-even; `Δ blocks ∈ {-1, 0, +1}` lemma proved from the column-height formula; O(1) incremental sign and `P` update, giving a loopless enumerator on the cube with the two castle filters as O(1) predicates at emit. Hub for Arc 10 (Knuth's algorithms in castle space).
- [x] **[Castle-native Gray tour](wiki/pages/castle-native-gray-tour.md)** - Hardy-styled working note on the Ruskey-methodology question of whether a Gray tour exists on `V(w, h)` directly (not an algorithm; Ruskey is a methodology, not a procedure). Small cases: `V_proper(3, 2)` alone admits a Hamilton path under M1; `V(3, 2)` needs `M1 ∪ M6` (`A → B → D → E → F → C`); `V(3, 3)` is three castles isolated under every local move set tried. Different failure modes at `h = 2` (pendants) and `h = 3` (sparseness). No pattern visible at these sizes; the page pauses at the wall.

### F Department (fractional calculus - castles at non-integer order)

Fractional calculus defines the `alpha`-th derivative and integral for real `alpha`, and its three classical definitions each land on a castle object the wiki already owns. Grunwald-Letnikov is a limit of binomially weighted finite differences: on a skyline it is the column difference `c_i - c_{i-1}` (whose negative part counts blocks) with the binomial weights `(-1)^k C(alpha, k)` run out to every earlier column. Riemann-Liouville is convolution with a power-law kernel then differentiation: on a generating function it is multiplication by `(1 - x)^{-alpha}`, the fractional partial sum. Caputo differentiates first and keeps classical initial conditions: on a C-finite recurrence it is the version that respects the initial terms Kitamasa starts from. Every fractional operator is nonlocal - the value at one column or one index depends on all the others with power-law weights - and that nonlocality is the whole content of the Department: power-law memory along the width, heavy-tailed jumps on the castle graph, and dense operators where the integer-order castle has sparse ones. Fourteen items, foundational rather than incremental; each is meant to open a line of work, not close one.

- [ ] **Half-sum castles: fractional partial sums and the `sqrt(pi)` door** - the `alpha`-th fractional partial sum of a count sequence has GF `A(x) / (1 - x)^alpha` (Cesaro means of order `alpha`); `alpha = 1` is the cumulative count by width, `alpha = 1/2` the half-cumulative. For rational `A` and `alpha = 1/2` the GF is algebraic of degree 2, the class of the tower word A004149 and of Catalan and Motzkin: half a sum moves a regular language's count into the context-free class, and the question is whether the half-summed castle count is itself the count of a natural context-free castle object. Asymptotically the transfer theorem gives `n^{alpha - 1} / Gamma(alpha)` at the `x = 1` singularity, and `Gamma(1/2) = sqrt(pi)`, so `pi` enters the coefficients of an exactly specified castle sequence, in the constant rather than only in a limit. The half-sum of the constant sequence is `C(2n, n) / 4^n`, so Wallis is the `alpha = 1/2` case; this is a new route through the [algebraic-transcendental-wall](wiki/pages/algebraic-transcendental-wall.md) for the T Department. The nondecreasing rule's half-column is already the order-`1/2` partial sum operator (Toeplitz in `C(2k,k)/4^k`) and `B_{1/2}(box)/h` is its row sum (A001803), so the half-summed castle object may already be sitting in the half-column embedding. Program: tabulate the half-sums of `F(w,h)` for `h = 2..5`, check OEIS, and find the castle object they count.
- [ ] **Mittag-Leffler generating functions: from OGF to EGF in one parameter** - the Mittag-Leffler function `E_alpha(z) = sum z^n / Gamma(alpha n + 1)` is the fractional exponential; the transform `sum a_n x^n / Gamma(alpha n + 1)` is the ordinary GF at `alpha = 0` and the exponential GF at `alpha = 1`. Castles are unlabelled and live on OGFs; permutations and `(n-1)!` cycles are labelled and live on EGFs; [castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](wiki/pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md) bridges them by three discrete upgrades. This gives the bridge a continuous parameter. Program: apply the Mittag-Leffler transform to `F(w,h)`, to `P(k,L)`, and to the Foata-form permutation counts, and ask at which `alpha` the castle GF and the permutation GF are closest; separately, the fractional Poisson process (Mittag-Leffler waiting times) as an eleventh sampler technique for the T Department.
- [ ] **The castle is a comb: subdiffusion at order `1/2`** - a tree castle, and any castle with a height-1 backbone, is a finite comb: a backbone with teeth of heights `c_i - 1`. Diffusion on a comb is the classical example of subdiffusion: a walker on the backbone is trapped in teeth, mean-square displacement grows like `t^{1/2}`, and the continuum limit is a time-fractional diffusion equation of order `1/2` (Arkhincheev-Baskin 1991, Weiss-Havlin). So the polyomino graph of [castle-graph](wiki/pages/castle-graph.md) is a fractional-diffusion medium and the order of the equation is a shape invariant. Program: measure the MSD exponent by simulation on castles across the shape axes (tree, convex, valley, battlement) and across the height-distribution of teeth; confirm `1/2` for uniform teeth and find how it moves for power-law tooth heights (the Hurst / ARFIMA item below supplies those) and for castles with 2x2 blocks (where the graph is no longer a tree); ask whether the exponent sees the parity clause at all. This is the [castle-graph](wiki/pages/castle-graph.md) counterpart of the pi-via-random-walks item in the T Department.
- [ ] **The Hurst exponent of a skyline: fractionally integrated castles** - a skyline is a time series, and the fractional integration order `d` of ARFIMA, `(1 - B)^d c = white noise`, is a discrete fractional derivative of the column heights. `d = 0` is i.i.d. columns (the uniform castle of [castle-entropy](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md)); `d = 1` is a random-walk skyline (the 1-smooth Motzkin strips of [pell-castle-strip](wiki/pages/pell-castle-strip.md)); `0 < d < 1` is long memory, `d = 1/2` is `1/f` noise. The fractional residual `(1 - x)^d C(x)` is lossless (inverted exactly by `(1 - x)^{-d}`), so a fractional predictor is a codec. Measured so far: on the song's waveform a fixed fractional predictor (32 taps, Q12, Rice residual, 4096-sample blocks) has a sharp minimum at the integer order 2 (11.6%, against 13.6% at 1.9 and 14.0% at 2.1), and per-block order choice on a `0..3` grid at step `0.1` never picks a non-integer order, because integer taps have finite support and no quantization noise; on synthetic ARFIMA skylines of `2^16` samples the best order tracks `d` and beats the best integer order by 0.5 to 1.6% for `0 < d < 1`, and loses at `d = 0` and `d >= 1`. So the fractional predictor is a codec for `1/f`-type sources, not for waveforms with local polynomial structure. Program: estimate `d` on the cameraman rows of [image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md) and on textures, where long memory is plausible, and Rice-code the rounded fractional residuals there; the DTMF castles as the `d = 0` control; and use `d` as the compressibility knob, a candidate statistic for the rule-generated-castle detector in the R Department.
- [ ] **Power-law memory rules: the non-Markov castle strip** - every strip rule on [castle-strip](wiki/pages/castle-strip.md) is Markov: whether height `b` may follow height `a` depends on `a` alone. A fractional strip rule weights every earlier column, the column `k` back by `C(alpha, k)`, the Grunwald-Letnikov kernel; the constraint has power-law memory and the exact transfer matrix is infinite. This is the fractional-numerics problem of the FMATH literature in castle form: truncating memory to `K` columns gives an `h^K`-state transfer matrix, dense where the Markov one was sparse, and the growth constant `rho(alpha, K)` converges in `K` at a power-law rate set by `alpha`. Program: build the truncated matrices for `h = 2, 3` and small `K`, measure the convergence, and look for a fast-convolution evaluation of the count using the binomial Toeplitz structure of the kernel (the NTT over `F_65537` on [song-as-castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md) is the right tool); then ask whether the `K -> infinity` growth constant is a new kind of number for the N Department or lands in the reachable-field census after all.
- [ ] **Fractional powers in the castle ring** - Kitamasa evaluates `x^w mod char(x)` in `F_p[x] / (char)`, the ring of [castle-cryptography-ring](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-ring.md). A fractional power `x^{w/2}` is a square root in that ring, and `x^{w/n}` an `n`-th root; existence and count are governed by the factorization of `char` and the gcd of `n` with the orders of the factor fields, so fractional exponentiation mod `p` is root extraction, the cousin of the discrete-log trapdoor. Program: characterize which `(char, p, n)` admit a unique `n`-th root of `x^w` (the fractional Kitamasa is then well defined and cheap), which admit many (a `1/n`-th column that is genuinely ambiguous), and which none; connect to the mod-`p` periods of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md), where a unique `M^{1/2}` exists whenever the period is odd and only half the elements have one when it is even; and add the half-step as an oracle in the castle Diffie-Hellman red-team of [castle-cryptography-round-two](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-round-two.md). Height 2 is settled for every odd prime below 300: in `F_p[x] / (x^2 - 2x + 2)` the element `x` is a square for every width iff `p = 7 (mod 8)` when `p` is inert, or `p` splits and both `1 +- i` are quadratic residues (`p = 41, 113, 137, 257, ...`, about 14% of split primes); otherwise `x^w` has a root iff `w` is even, so half of all widths have no half-step. The inert law is the norm criterion, `N(1 + i) = 2` a residue iff `p = +-1 (mod 8)`. Over `R` the half-step exists iff no negative eigenvalue, over `F_p` iff `x` is a residue in every factor field: the same obstruction in two completions, which is how the ring page should be organized. `k = 2, 3, 4` await the norm analysis factor by factor.
- [ ] **Two orders as coordinates** - the pair `(B_alpha, B_beta)` at `(alpha, beta) = (sqrt 2 - 1, pi - 3)` takes distinct values on every castle of every cell tested, `(6,3)` through `(6,5)` and `(8,3)`, to nine decimals; a single irrational order misses a few percent (the tail-blind pairs), and two lift it. So a castle is a point in the plane, recoverable from two real numbers, though only by table. Against it, `B_{1/2}` is exactly representable (an integer multiple of `2^{-(2w-2)}`) but as an integer code needs 14 bits for `(6,4)` against `log_2 3367 = 11.7` and takes only 1017 of 3367 distinct values: exactness, not compactness, is what half order buys. Program: prove the two-order injectivity or find the first collision, and a decoder that is not a lookup.
- [ ] **The poset theorem for the half-column** - a rule has a half-column with nonnegative weights iff it is a partial order on the heights, proved for `h <= 4` (the sets coincide with the reflexive-transitive-antisymmetric rules exactly, A001035); the mechanism is `sqrt(I + N) = sum_k C(1/2, k) N^k`, whose `k = 2` coefficient `-1/8` lands on every 2-chain `a < b < c` missing its direct edge, so transitivity is what keeps the root nonnegative. Program: all heights, and the explicit entries of `sqrt(I + N)` as signed chain counts. Alongside it the rate-2 reading: if `R^2 = M` with `R` a 0/1 rule, every width-`w` strip under `M` is a width-`2w` strip under `R` read at even columns, so a 0/1 half-column is a finer column rate for the same language, and the metallic strips (simple negative eigenvalue, no real root) are already at their finest rate; ask which castle languages are not.
- [ ] **Fractional width as bit cost** - [castle-entropy](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md) has `log_2 F ~ w log_2 h - 1`; the real interpolant of [fractional-width-and-height](wiki/pages/fractional-width-and-height.md) makes `log_2 F(w,h)` smooth in `w`, with derivative the marginal cost of a column, `log_2 h` in the limit, and the cosine in `F(w,2)` makes the marginal cost at height 2 wobble with period 8 around one bit. Program: test the fractional bit cost against an arithmetic coder driven by the transfer matrix, and read the wobble as a coding gain or loss per column.
- [ ] **`Z(q, alpha)` as a cell fingerprint** - at an irrational order the partition function of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) is a Dirichlet polynomial with almost all exponents distinct; ask whether it answers the isospectral-style question "which cell is this" from a sample of castles, and whether two cells can share it.
- [ ] **Boundary defect of `Delta^alpha Delta^beta = Delta^{alpha+beta}` at the wall** - the semigroup law fails on a width-`w` skyline when the Grunwald-Letnikov sum is truncated at the left wall; measure the defect on the 5460 castles of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) as a function of `w`, `alpha`, `beta`; sort that page's facts into those that need the semigroup law (the ARFIMA argmin) and those that do not (`B_0 = area`, `B_1 = blocks`); in the height direction, where [fractional-width-and-height](wiki/pages/fractional-width-and-height.md) found the parity clause is a branch cut, name the group actually acting (`(C, +)` on the principal branch, or the covering group of the cut).

- [x] **[Fractional block count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md)** - `B_alpha = sum_i max(0, Delta^alpha c_i)` equals area at `alpha = 0` and block count at `alpha = 1` exactly (5460 castles); `B_{1/2}` is dyadic and separates all ten `(4,2)` even castles; not monotone in `alpha` (829 of 5460), bounded by area, can undershoot blocks; collisions are tail-blind pairs inherited from the ascent count; position-aware; `P_0 = (-1)^{w+h+1}` so area parity is trivial while `|P_alpha|` exceeds `|P_1|` inside `(0.5, 1)`; the L1 residual argmin recovers a skyline's ARFIMA order; for nondecreasing castles `B_alpha = [x^w](1-x)^{alpha-1} C(x)` and is monotone, and `B_{1/2}(box) = h 2w C(2w,w)/4^w ~ 2h sqrt(w/pi)`.
- [x] **[Fractional width and fractional height](wiki/pages/fractional-width-and-height.md)** - `char_k` has no negative real root for `k <= 8`, so `F(w,h)` is real at real `w` on the principal branch for `h <= 9`, with `F(w,2) = (2^{w+1} - 2^{(w+3)/2} cos(pi(w+1)/4))/4`; at half-integer height `F` is complex with imaginary part `(-1)^m (A_w(h-1) + A_w(h-2))/2`, the alternating part of the parity clause; the half-column counts are OEIS sequences (squares of 0/1 rules A226321, nonnegative spectrum A086510, positive spectrum = DAGs A003024, nonnegative half-column = labeled posets A001035, verified `h <= 4`), the nondecreasing rule's half-column is the `(1-x)^{-1/2}` Toeplitz matrix, `J^{1/2} = J/sqrt h`, and every metallic strip has a simple negative eigenvalue and no real half-column.
- [x] **[Fractional recurrences: the rung between rungs](wiki/pages/fractional-recurrences.md)** - the fractional-Fibonacci `nabla^alpha a_n = a_{n-1}` has characteristic `(1-r)^alpha = r` and growth `g(alpha) = 1/r*(alpha)`, a continuous strictly increasing bijection `[0, infty) -> [1, infty)`; rational `alpha = p/q` gives algebraic growth of degree `<= max(p,q)` (small orders reproduce the census cubics: supergolden at `1/3`, plastic-squared at `2/3`, plastic-cubed at `3/2`), and irrational `alpha` gives transcendental growth by Gelfond-Schneider on `alpha = log r / log(1-r)`. The metallic ladder crosses the curve at one rational point only: **golden at exactly `alpha = 1/2`**, because `delta_1 - 1 = 1/delta_1` is a unit of `Z[phi]` while for `a >= 2` we have `N(delta_a - 1) = -a`, and the norm relation `a^m = pm 1` has no nontrivial integer solution. So silver, bronze, copper, nickel meet the curve at transcendental orders `alpha_a ≈ 1.65, 3.31, 5.36, 7.70`. The Fibonacci-Pell interpolation `nabla^alpha a_n = a_{n-1} + a_{n-2}` picks up silver as a second rational-`alpha` point (`alpha = 1`). Fractional recurrences at irrational `alpha` are the transcendental complement of the reachable-field census.
- [x] **[Levy flights on the castle graph](wiki/pages/levy-flights.md)** - the Riascos-Mateos space-fractional Laplacian `L^alpha = U diag(lambda_i^alpha) U^T` generates a long-jump walk (dense generator matrix); its heat trace `Tr exp(-t L^alpha)` is spectral, so the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair of [isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md) shares it at every `(alpha, t)`. Local return probability `[exp(-t L^alpha)]_{vv}` is eigenvector-weighted and does separate the pair: the height-3 tower tip in `B` has strictly higher return probability than the height-2 spike tips in `A` at every `alpha in {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2}` and every `t in {0.5, 1, 2, 5}` tested, and the sorted 11-value multiset differs at every `(alpha, t)`. The separator is **monotone increasing in `alpha`**: long jumps *shrink* the A/B gap rather than widen it, so Levy flights homogenize local traps - the opposite of the comb-teeth regime.

### X Department (ciphers - keystreams, steganography, commitments)

Ciphers put the castle machinery under an adversary. The castle-as-characteristic-polynomial reading of [signed-tower-count](wiki/pages/signed-tower-count.md) turns the counting recurrence into a ring `F_p[x] / (Q)`, and the fast-index trick of [kitamasa](wiki/pages/kitamasa.md) into a modular exponentiation - the trapdoor of Diffie-Hellman. The eigenvalue-order structure of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md) becomes the discrete-log target, and the two round-one breaks (Pohlig-Hellman via char-poly factorization, Berlekamp-Massey via the count stream) are one-line applications of tools already on the wiki. On a separate axis, every skyline ([image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md), [song-as-castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md)) is a channel that can carry a hidden payload by LSB or block parity ([castle-steganography](wiki/pages/castle-steganography.md)), and the even-block clause reads as a natural commitment scheme; every isospectral pair ([isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md)) reads as an adversary's ambiguity. The Department houses the three-seminar crypto arc (build, round two, blue team) and the steganography line, and it is where new adversarial questions - keystream complexity, distinguishers by rule, hardness of the isospectrality census, warden statistics - land.

- [ ] **What survives an FM channel** - send the row castles of [castle-steganography](wiki/pages/castle-steganography.md) as a frequency-modulated tone through a band-limited voice channel and measure, per bit, which castle statistics survive demodulation (LSB of height, block parity, histogram bins, low DFT modes); code channel A against the measured error profile.
- [ ] **Integrity check with a public order** - the keyed phase `e^{i pi B_alpha}` with `alpha` secret is broken with three known castles: `B_alpha(c)` is piecewise polynomial in `alpha`, so each known `(castle, phase)` pair leaves finitely many candidate orders (13, 15, 21 on width-12 castles) and three intersections recover the key to nine decimals. A functional polynomial in the key is not a keyed hash. What survives is an integrity check with a public `alpha`: `|P_alpha|` stays near zero to `alpha = 0.25`, then grows and exceeds `|P_1|` inside `(0.5, 1)` (`(6,4)`: 157 at `0.9` against 91), so the flat region is the least-biased choice. Program: measure how many bits of integrity the public-order phase carries against a one-cell edit, and whether any order beats the parity bit.
- [ ] **A warden that counts blocks** - channel B of [castle-steganography](wiki/pages/castle-steganography.md) leaves the per-row block parity a coin flip, and the fractional block count does not see it either: on 4000 image-like row castles (`w = 512`, `h = 256`) with parity set by a one-cell edit at a strict local extremum, the marginal of `B_alpha` at `alpha = 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1` has KS p-value `1.000` at every order (the edit moves `B_alpha` by at most `1.0` against a natural standard deviation of 180 to 24000), and a count of barely-extreme columns is 76.4 per row in cover against 76.8 in stego, KS p = 0.97. What is left is a warden that knows the cover, or a higher-order joint statistic (adjacent-row parity correlation, block-count distribution across rows).
