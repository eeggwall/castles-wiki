## Prize Problems

Not checkboxes: each prize problem bridges Divisions and moves a limit every castle researcher has hit. Tiers are scales: kilo is objects about `10^3` wide, tall, or in cells; mega `10^6`; giga `10^9`; tera `10^12`, where the PE 502 targets live. Divisions bridged are in parentheses.

### Kilo

- **The kilo-drum** (S, Z, Telephone Company) - prove that almost all tree castles have an isospectral mate as the cell count grows, the castle analog of Schwenk's "almost all trees are cospectral," or prove castles are the family you can hear; exhibit a 1000-cell isospectral family by construction.
- **The thousandth polynomial** (N, S, E) - factor `char_k` completely at `k = 1000`, with the Galois group of every factor, and prove for all `k` that the two parity sectors are the only splitting.
- **The kilo sandcastle** (S, Z, T) - the sandpile identity element of the 1000 x 1000 castle in both models - the tide model (the base row as the sink) and the sink model (one corner sink cell) - and a proof of its scaling limit in each; the square-grid identity is a famous fractal, and the question is what the tide, or a single corner sink, does to it.

### Mega

- **The castle's own random-matrix law** (S, T, N) - the limiting eigenvalue distribution of the normalized signed transfer matrix as `k -> infinity`, checked at `k = 10^6`, and the pi it carries; Arc 9 with a spectrum the castle owns rather than borrows.
- **The shape of a million blocks** (Q, T, Z) - the limit shape of a uniformly random convex castle of area `10^6`, as random partitions have one, and whether the parity clause leaves any trace on it.

### Giga

- **The address of the Ninth** (R, E, Telephone Company) - the exact rank of the Furtwängler Ninth in `V(391,608,000, 65536)`, a roughly 6-gigabit integer, then unrank it back to the recording bit for bit: the sound-to-castle bijection as one address.
- **The period at P** (N, X) - the exact period of `F(., h) mod 10^9+7` for every `h <= 100`, which needs the cyclotomic factorizations of `P^j - 1`: the full castle ring at Project Euler's own prime, and the true security of castle Diffie-Hellman there.

### Tera

- **The tera square** (E, N) - `F(10^12, 10^12) mod 10^9+7`. Kitamasa in the width needs a `10^12`-state matrix and the height direction a quasi-polynomial of degree `2 * 10^12`; PE 502 stopped its square target at `10^4` for this reason, and the prize is an algorithm sublinear in both directions.
- **The tera Gray tour** (E, R, Z) - a loopless castle-native Gray code, moving a bounded number of cells per step, run through more than `10^12` castles at O(1) per step; settles for all sizes the tour that bumps, swaps, double bumps and far swaps together cannot give at width 3 and any odd height, where a step must move two columns at once.

### Capstone

- **The end of the census** (N, Q, F, S) - a decision rule that says, for every algebraic integer, whether it is a castle growth constant by width, area, or perimeter, at what minimum height, and whether any castle carries it as an adjacency spectral radius; the quadratic min-height law, the missing Salem numbers, and the bronze hunt fall out as one theorem.

### Arc 14. Sandcastles
- Thesis: the abelian sandpile is literally sand on a castle, in two models treated separately - the sink model (one sink cell) and the tide model (the base row is the tide, the sink) - and the two see different cycles: trees wash away in both (trivial group) and only the 2x2 blocks hold sand, but under the tide a block on the ground closes through the ground as a triangle, so the tide also hears how high the blocks stand; so the sandpile group, its identity element, the clock period of a grain dropped on the apex, and the avalanche statistics of Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld are castle invariants in each model, finer than the Laplacian spectrum, or provably not.
- Status: delivered, both models on every page. Seminar page: [sandcastle-seminar](wiki/pages/sandcastle-seminar.md), following the 16-cell silver-spectrum castle `(3,2,1,2,2,1,2,3)` side by side in the sink and tide models.

### Arc 17. Every castle is a Motzkin path
- Thesis: a castle is a cornerless Motzkin path (Deutsch-Elizalde), so the Motzkin numbers are not a resemblance but the castle's own family; relaxing the two castle rules walks from tower words to all Motzkin paths (Prodinger's corner weights), the parity clause splits the Motzkin numbers into odd and even paths and turns the growth constant `3` into the Gaussian integers `1 +- 2i`, bounded height climbs the ladder `2, 1 + sqrt 2, phi^2, 1 + sqrt 3, ... -> 3`, and one castle family (never drop by two) turns out to be a sequence Prodinger reported missing from OEIS.

- [ ] **Poincaré castles (new family)** - named for Poincaré's roulette problem: a spun wheel lands in each sector about equally often whatever the spin, and the wheel here is the Project Euler 502 parity. **The roulette test:** for a primitive rule (some power of its transfer matrix has every entry positive), the even-block share of its strips tends to 1/2 unless the parity of each allowed step's rise, `max(0, b - a) mod 2`, splits as `phi(a) + phi(b)` or `phi(a) + phi(b) + 1` for a function `phi` from heights to `{0, 1}`; this is Wielandt's equality case for the signed transfer matrix against the unsigned one ([castle-sign](wiki/pages/castle-sign.md)). Checked against exact eigenvalues on every primitive rule: 30 of 139 keep a parity bias at height 3, and 796 of 25,575 at height 4. The **Poincaré castles** are the case `phi = 0`: castles in which every rise between neighboring columns is even. Such a castle has `blocks ≡ c_1 (mod 2)`, so its first column alone decides the parity (checked for every member with `h <= 5`, `w <= 7`); for example `(2, 2, 1, 3, 3, 1)` has 4 blocks. They number `1, 4, 12, 32, 80, 193, 457` at height 3, of which `0, 0, 1, 5, 16, 43, 107` are even-block, growing at `psi^3 = psi + 1 = 2.3247...` by width, plastic cubed, with minimal polynomial `x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x - 1` ([plastic-number](wiki/pages/plastic-number.md)); at height 4 they number `1, 5, 18, 58, 178, 532, 1563`, growing at `2.7693...`, the root of `x^3 - 3x^2 + x - 1`; at height 2 they are the `w` castles `(2, ..., 2, 1, ..., 1)`, all with 2 blocks. Their mirror images, the castles in which every drop is even, are the case `phi(a) = a mod 2`, with `blocks ≡ c_w (mod 2)`. Gets its own page, `poincare-castles`.

- [ ] **Littlewood castles (new family)** - named for Littlewood's 1914 theorem that `pi(x) - li(x)` changes sign infinitely often, though no change had been observed; his student Skewes bounded the first change. The **parity lead** of a family at width `w` is the sign of its even-block count minus its odd-block count. The Littlewood castles are the castles of height 4 whose neighboring columns follow the rule `1 -> {2, 3}`, `2 -> {1, 3, 4}`, `3 -> {2, 4}`, `4 -> {2, 4}` ([castle-strip](wiki/pages/castle-strip.md)), for example `(1, 2, 4, 4, 2, 3)`. They number `1, 4, 13, 35, 90, 221, 533, 1264, 2973, 6943` at widths 1 to 10, with even-block minus odd-block counts `1, 4, 9, 11, 14, 31, 49, 50, 85, 179`. The even-block castles lead at every width up to 161, the odd-block castles lead at width 162, and the lead changes 82 times in all by width 300, and infinitely often after: the largest eigenvalues of the signed transfer matrix are a complex pair of modulus 1.5392, just above a real eigenvalue 1.5107. The **Skewes width** of a family is the first width at which its parity lead changes. The Littlewood castles have Skewes width 162, as do their mirror images (the transposed rule), the largest among all 65,536 rules of height 4 in an exact search to width 300; at height 3 the largest over all 512 rules is 19, for the rule `1 -> {1, 2}`, `2 -> {1, 3}`, `3 -> {1, 2, 3}` (exact search to width 1000). Gets its own page, `littlewood-castles`, which also carries the parity lead of all castles.

A Markov castle has height 2 and is built from pairs of columns, `(1, 1)` for the letter `a` and `(2, 2)` for the letter `b`, laid out as a lower Christoffel word: for coprime `p, q >= 1` and `n = p + q`, letter `i` is `b` exactly when `floor(ip/n) - floor((i-1)p/n) = 1`; the castle `(2, 2)`, the word `b`, is one too. Its Markov number is one third of the trace of the product of the matrices `[[c_i, 1], [1, 0]]` over its columns. Examples: `ab` gives `(1, 1, 2, 2)` and 5, `aab` gives `(1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2)` and 13, `abb` gives `(1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 2)` and 29. Every Christoffel castle up to width 78 gives a Markov number, and each of the 56 Markov numbers up to `10^7` comes from exactly one castle (1 comes from the word `a`, the flat `(1, 1)` of height 1). A Markov castle with `p` letters `b` and `q` letters `a` has `1 + min(p, q)` blocks, so it is an even-block castle exactly when `min(p, q)` is odd. Gets its own page, `markov-castles`.

- [ ] **Gauss castles (new family)** - named for Gauss's continued-fraction map `x -> {1/x}`. The Gauss castles of continuant `q` are the castles with `K(c) = q`, finitely many for each `q`; for example `(1, 4, 1)` has continuant 6, since `[0; 1, 4, 1] = 5/6`. ### Z Division (zoo - specimens, taxonomy, rule variations)

- [ ] **Demon castles (new family)** - named for Laplace's demon, which knows the future from the present. In a demon castle equal heights are always followed by equal heights, so the skyline is the orbit of one map on the heights `{1, ..., h}`: a run of distinct heights, then a cycle that repeats. For example, in `(1, 3, 2, 4, 2, 4, 2)` every 2 is followed by 4 and every 4 by 2. A demon castle with `m` distinct heights, `h` among them, has `m (h-1)!/(h-m)!` orders for its distinct heights and, when `w > m`, `m` choices for where its cycle starts, so the demon castles of width `w` and height `h` number `D(w, h) = sum_{m < w, m <= h} m^2 (h-1)!/(h-m)!`, plus `w (h-1)!/(h-w)!` when `w <= h` (checked for `h <= 5`, `w <= 8`). From width `h + 1` on the count is constant, `E(h) = (h-1)! sum_{m=1}^{h} m^2/(h-m)!`, which is `1, 5, 27, 163, 1109, 8481, 72415, 685007` for `h = 1, ..., 8`, with no match in the OEIS (searched 2026-10-28). Gets its own page, `demon-castles`.
  - [ ] **Demon castles and the parity clause** - along the repeating cycle the block count grows by the cycle's total rise once per period; give the even-block demon castles of each `(w, h)` cell and the growth of `E(h)` with `h`.
- [ ] **Cauchy castles (new family)** - named for the Cauchy-Frobenius lemma (Burnside's lemma). Two maps send the castles of each `(w, h)` cell to castles of the same cell: the mirror `c_i -> c_{w+1-i}` and the band reflection `c_i -> max(c) + min(c) - c_i`; together they generate a group of order 4. The band reflection sends the vee castles onto the convex castles, the bijection asked for in the Q Division item *Convex to vee bijection*. The Cauchy castles are the castles fixed by the two maps together, `c_i + c_{w+1-i} = max(c) + min(c)`, for example `(2, 3, 3, 4)`. They number `h^k + (h-1)^k` at width `2k`, and at width `2k + 1` they number `h^k` for odd `h` and `(h-1)^k` for even `h`. The centrally symmetric castles, open on [castle-classification-shape](wiki/pages/castle-classification-shape.md), are the Cauchy castles with `min(c) = 1`: `h^k - (h-2)^k` at width `2k`, and at width `2k + 1` the same for odd `h` and none for even `h`. The lemma counts the castles up to the group as `(A + P + 1 + C)/4`, with `A = h^w - (h-1)^w`, `P = h^{ceil(w/2)} - (h-1)^{ceil(w/2)}` the palindromic castles, 1 for the box `(h, ..., h)`, the only castle the band reflection fixes, and `C` the Cauchy castles: at height 2, `1, 2, 3, 6, 10, 20, 36, 72` for `w = 1, ..., 8`. The mirror keeps the block count and the band reflection adds `h + min(c) - c_1 - c_w` to it, so the even-block castles are closed under the mirror only. All checked for `h <= 5`, `w <= 8`, `h^w <= 4*10^5`. Gets its own page, `cauchy-castles`, which also answers the symmetry question of the item *Dihedral Burnside on the census*.

### Q Division (q-numbers - castles by area)

- [ ] **Lightfoot castles of capacity k (new family)** - named for N. M. H. Lightfoot's *The Solidification of Molten Steel* (Proc. London Math. Soc., 1930). Pour molten steel over a castle: column `i` fills to `min(L_i, R_i)`, the lower of the tallest columns on its left and on its right, and the steel freezes into the castle's convex core ([convex-core](wiki/pages/convex-core.md)). The **capacity** of a castle is the number of steel cells, and the Lightfoot castles of capacity `k` are the castles of width `w` and height `h` that hold `k` of them. Example: `(2, 1, 3, 1, 1, 2)` holds 3 cells and freezes into `(2, 2, 3, 2, 2, 2)`. The steel sits in the valleys ([valley-castles](wiki/pages/valley-castles.md)): the capacity is the sum over the valleys of depth times span, while the block count is `h` plus the sum of the depths alone, so the capacity and the Project Euler 502 parity are two weightings of the same valleys. Capacity 0 is the convex castles, which have no valley, and capacity 1 is the valley castles of depth 1 and span 1. Counts: capacity 0, `C(w + 2h - 3, 2h - 2)`; capacity 1, `(2h - 3) C(w + 2h - 4, 2h - 1)` for `h >= 2`; all capacities, `h^w - (h - 1)^w`. With `[m]_q = 1 + q + ... + q^{m-1}`, the plateau fibers of the convex core give `sum_w sum_c x^w q^cap(c) = x (1 - q[h-1]_q x)/(1 - [h]_q x) * prod_{m=1}^{h-1} ((1 - q[m-1]_q x)/(1 - [m]_q x))^2`, which telescopes at `q = 1` to `x/((1 - hx)(1 - (h-1)x))` and is `x/(1 - x)^{2h-1}` at `q = 0`; so at fixed `k` the count is a polynomial in `w` of degree `2h - 2 + k`. Checked against enumeration at `q = 2, 3, 5` for `h <= 6`, `w <= 8`, `h^w <= 3*10^5`, and the two valley identities for `h <= 5`, `w <= 7`. Gets its own page, `lightfoot-castles`.

- [ ] **Hardy castles of rank k (new family)** - named for the `e^{C sqrt n}` growth of Hardy and Ramanujan's partition asymptotics. A Hardy castle of rank `k` has at most `k` blocks in every row. Rank 1 is the convex castles, whose count by area grows like `e^{2 pi sqrt(n/3)}` (A001523, [weakly-unimodal-composition](wiki/pages/weakly-unimodal-composition.md)). Rank 2 by area: `1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 127, 249, 481, 915, 1713, 3158, 5735, 10269, 18138` at areas 1 to 16, computed to area 70 by a dynamic program on the run lengths of each row and checked against brute force to area 16; the first castle excluded is `(2, 1, 2, 1, 2)`, area 8, with three blocks in row 2. Rank 3 first departs from all castles at area 11, with `(2, 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 2)`. ### S Division (spectral analysis - castles as 2D polyominoes)

Five spectra sit naturally on a castle: transfer-matrix, LGV kernel, skyline DFT, combinatorial Laplacian, Ihara zeta. Seminar targets below, roughly increasing effort.

- [ ] **Laplace castles (new family)** - named for the Laplacian. A Laplace castle is a castle whose castle graph ([castle-graph](wiki/pages/castle-graph.md)) has a Laplacian with only integer eigenvalues. Among all castles with at most 20 cells exactly 12 are Laplace castles: the paths of 1 to 3 cells, `(1)`, `(1, 1)`, `(2)`, `(1, 1, 1)`, `(1, 2)`, `(2, 1)` and `(3)`; the star `(1, 2, 1)`; the square `(2, 2)`; the `2 x 3` boxes `(2, 2, 2)` and `(3, 3)`; and the `3 x 3` box `(3, 3, 3)`. None has more than 9 cells. A box of `a` columns and `b` rows is a Laplace castle exactly when `a, b <= 3`, since its Laplacian eigenvalues are the sums of those of two paths, and the path of `n` cells has only integer Laplacian eigenvalues exactly when `n <= 3` (the path of 4 cells has `2 - sqrt 2`). Gets its own page, `laplace-castles`.
  - [ ] **The list of Laplace castles is complete** - prove that no castle with more than 9 cells has an integral Laplacian spectrum, so that the 12 castles above are all of them.
- [ ] **Open-stroke castles at even widths** - the open-stroke castles of [konigsberg-castles](wiki/pages/konigsberg-castles.md), with exactly two odd vertices once a ground vertex is joined to the bottom row, number `h² + 3h − 2` at odd widths by a classification into five families, and for `h ≤ 12` they number `(2h⁴ + 11h³ − 5h² − 2h + 3)/3` in all (`3, 33, 137, 377, 831, …`); prove the even-width total `(2h⁴ + 11h³ − 8h² − 11h + 9)/3` by classifying how two columns of defect 1 (dips, spikes, steps of 2), or one of defect 2, sit between up to three runs, together with the counts `m(m + 1)` at the even widths `2m ≤ 2h − 4`, `3(h − 1)²` at width `2h − 2` and `(5h − 4)(h − 1)` at width `2h`, and the widest castle, three double staircases sharing their end columns, of width `6h − 2`.
- [ ] **Bronze-spectrum castle hunt** - is `(3+sqrt(13))/2 = 3.303` the adjacency spectral radius of any castle? None in twelve exhaustive boxes (about `7.1 x 10^11` skylines, [bronze-castle-hunt](wiki/pages/bronze-castle-hunt.md)), and rectangles and every split into five or fewer evenly connected groups are proved impossible; what remains is a reason `x^2 - 3x - 1` never divides a castle's characteristic polynomial, or a construction with six or more groups that forces it.
- [ ] **Skyline DFT - individual-castle signatures** - the DFT of a column-height sequence is a complete individual invariant modulo cyclic shift; classify castles by spectral concentration (low-pass / high-pass / sparse-spectrum). Hooks into compressed sensing and turnpike reconstruction.
- [ ] **LGV kernel spectrum - determinantal universality** - eigenvalues of the non-crossing-path kernel `N(w; i, j)` in `[0,1]`; a sine-kernel bulk limit would put castles in the same universality class as random Young tableaux and GUE.
- [ ] **Hear the sand (Ihara zeta at u=1)** - Hashimoto's `1/zeta(u) = (1-u^2)^(r-1) det(I - Au + Q u^2)` on castle graphs where `r` is the 2x2-block count and `Q = D - I`; Northshield's theorem (JCTB 74, 1998; read via its statement in Kim-Kwon-Lee, arXiv 0705.2284) gives `f'(1) = 2(r-1) kappa` for `f(u) = det(I - Au + Q u^2)`, with `kappa` the spanning-tree count = `|K_sink|`, so `1/zeta` vanishes to order `r` at `u = 1` with leading coefficient `2^r (1-r) kappa` (checked on castles; both are 0 for a single block, `r = 1`) and the order of the sink-model sandpile group is a coefficient of the zeta - a single function that says Ramanujan through its poles ([[ramanujan-castles]]) and sand-content through its vanishing at 1; tabulate over the census and ask which higher zeta coefficients see the invariant factors of `K_sink`, and do the same for the tide model with the zeta of the castle whose bottom row is merged into one vertex (its tree count is `|K_tide|`).
- [ ] **Cyclic sandcastles (both models)** - the original conjecture ("`K` is cyclic iff every maximal cluster of 2x2 blocks is a 2-wide ladder") is refuted on [sandpile-census](wiki/pages/sandpile-census.md): its own example `(2,2,1,2,2)` has two single-block clusters and `K = Z/4 x Z/4`, and with "ladder" read as a horizontal row it fails on 9,547 castles to 16 cells, starting with `(3,3)`. Proved there: a path-shaped cluster (straight or bent) always gives a cyclic group, so a castle whose clusters are all paths is cyclic iff the cluster orders are pairwise coprime. Open: which non-path clusters give cyclic groups - sink model, 921 castles to 16 cells do, the smallest `(2,3,3,3)` (`Z/712`), while the `2 x 2` square of blocks `(3,3,3)` gives `Z/8 x Z/24`; tide model, 1,660 do, the smallest `(3,3,3)` itself (`Z/95`), while `(4,4,4)` splits (`Z/13 x Z/91`, cyclic `Z/2415` in the sink model).

### T Division (transcendentals - pi and siblings)

Umbrella for approximating transcendental constants (pi first, then Catalan's `G`, `zeta(3)`, `gamma`, ...) via castle-native machinery. Ten sampler techniques (rejection, transfer-matrix, Sattolo-Foata, Gray-code, rank/unrank, MCMC, coupling-from-the-past, Wilson-on-LGV, Wang-Landau, random-rule ensembles) are the substrate.

- [ ] **The height-law scope** - classify the families with finitely many members at each width by the limit law of `height / sqrt(w)`, as the growth types classify families by growth constant ([castle-classification](wiki/pages/castle-classification.md)); the Kolmogorov castles have Kolmogorov's law ([kolmogorov-castles](wiki/pages/kolmogorov-castles.md)), and the next three to compute are the Dyck-path and Motzkin-path castles ([castle-classification-shape](wiki/pages/castle-classification-shape.md), Axis 3) and the 1-smooth castles with first column at height 1 ([1-smooth-castles](wiki/pages/1-smooth-castles.md)).
- [x] **[Kolmogorov castles](wiki/pages/kolmogorov-castles.md) (new family)** - the castles of the two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test are the Dyck-path castles of width `2n + 1` with each peak marked by the sample ahead on it, `C(2n, n)` in all; by the reflection count of Gnedenko and Korolyuk those of height `h` number `sum_k (-1)^k [C(2n, n + kh) - C(2n, n + k(h - 1))]` (`16, 38, 14, 2` at width 9), every member has `n + 1` blocks, and by the limit theorems of Kolmogorov (1933) and Smirnov the share of height at most `1 + x sqrt(w - 1)` tends to Kolmogorov's `K(x)`, 95% at `x = 1.3581`, so the test's 5% critical value is a castle height.
- [x] **[Castle samplers](wiki/pages/castle-samplers.md) (prerequisite)** - the ten sampler techniques on one page, each implemented and checked by execution. Transfer-matrix sampler exact in rational arithmetic, `O(wh)` per draw; `V(w, h)` has 191 components under single-column +-1 moves at `(8, 4)`, so MCMC and CFTP run on the cube and filter at emit; heat-bath is monotone for `t <= 1` and antimonotone for `t > 1` (CFTP after flipping odd columns); Wilson trees match `det L~` (sink and tide) with edge marginals = effective resistance; LGV sequential sampler exact on parallelogram polyominoes; Wang-Landau resolves a 4e12 dynamic range at `(20, 6)` with percent-level saturation; Bernoulli random rules follow the circular and semicircle laws. Calibration: permutation cycle-count variance - ln n -> gamma - pi^2/6, while castle peaks grow linearly in `w`.
- [ ] **pi via CLT-style castle statistics** - pick a castle statistic `X(C)` (area, block count, peak count, tallest-peak position, ...), establish a CLT `(X - mu)/sigma -> N(0,1)`, and read pi off the mode density `1/(sigma sqrt(2 pi))`. Tier-1: area CLT via transfer-matrix sampler.
- [ ] **pi via castle spectral limit densities** - every RMT limit density carries pi (sine-kernel spacings, Wigner semicircle, Tracy-Widom, Airy edge, Marchenko-Pastur, Kesten-McKay); each is a direct hook into a [spectral-analysis](wiki/pages/spectral-analysis.md) method.
- [ ] **pi via castle geometric probability** - Buffon needle across skyline, Buffon-Laplace disk fit inside castle, random chord in bounding box, Barbier for uniform-rotation silhouettes; plus Ehrhart / lattice-point routes on the castle polytope. Tier-1: Buffon needle.
- [ ] **pi via classical asymptotic identities on castles** - Stirling on `(n-1)!` Foata forms, Basel from column-height autocorrelation, Wallis-like ratios from `(T±P)/2`; speculative since not every castle asymptotic yields a factorial or a `1/n^2` decay.
- [ ] **pi via random walks on castle graphs** - simple random walk return / cover / hitting times on the polyomino graph; loop-erased random walk with conformally invariant limit and pi-laden observables.
- [ ] **Other transcendentals** - Catalan's `G`, Apery's `zeta(3)`, Euler-Mascheroni `gamma` via the same machinery; placeholder, no concrete castle route inventoried yet.

### F Division (fractional calculus - castles at non-integer order)

Fractional calculus defines the `alpha`-th derivative and integral for real `alpha`, and its three classical definitions each land on a castle object the wiki already owns. Grunwald-Letnikov is a limit of binomially weighted finite differences: on a skyline it is the column difference `c_i - c_{i-1}` (whose negative part counts blocks) with the binomial weights `(-1)^k C(alpha, k)` run out to every earlier column. Riemann-Liouville is convolution with a power-law kernel then differentiation: on a generating function it is multiplication by `(1 - x)^{-alpha}`, the fractional partial sum. Caputo differentiates first and keeps classical initial conditions: on a C-finite recurrence it is the version that respects the initial terms Kitamasa starts from. Every fractional operator is nonlocal - the value at one column or one index depends on all the others with power-law weights - and that nonlocality is the whole content of the Division: power-law memory along the width, heavy-tailed jumps on the castle graph, and dense operators where the integer-order castle has sparse ones. Seventeen items, foundational rather than incremental; each is meant to open a line of work, not close one.

- [ ] **Mittag-Leffler generating functions: from OGF to EGF in one parameter** - the Mittag-Leffler function `E_alpha(z) = sum z^n / Gamma(alpha n + 1)` is the fractional exponential; the transform `sum a_n x^n / Gamma(alpha n + 1)` is the ordinary GF at `alpha = 0` and the exponential GF at `alpha = 1`. Castles are unlabeled and live on OGFs; permutations and `(n-1)!` cycles are labeled and live on EGFs; [castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](wiki/pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md) bridges them by three discrete upgrades. This gives the bridge a continuous parameter. Program: apply the Mittag-Leffler transform to `F(w,h)`, to `P(k,L)`, and to the Foata-form permutation counts, and ask at which `alpha` the castle GF and the permutation GF are closest; separately, the fractional Poisson process (Mittag-Leffler waiting times) as an eleventh sampler technique for the T Division.
- [ ] **The Hurst exponent of a skyline: fractionally integrated castles** - a skyline is a time series, and the fractional integration order `d` of ARFIMA, `(1 - B)^d c = white noise`, is a discrete fractional derivative of the column heights. `d = 0` is i.i.d. columns (the uniform castle of [castle-entropy](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md)); `d = 1` is a random-walk skyline (the 1-smooth Motzkin strips of [pell-castle-strip](wiki/pages/pell-castle-strip.md)); `0 < d < 1` is long memory, `d = 1/2` is `1/f` noise. The fractional residual `(1 - x)^d C(x)` is lossless (inverted exactly by `(1 - x)^{-d}`), so a fractional predictor is a codec. Measured so far: on the song's waveform a fixed fractional predictor (32 taps, Q12, Rice residual, 4096-sample blocks) has a sharp minimum at the integer order 2 (11.6%, against 13.6% at 1.9 and 14.0% at 2.1), and per-block order choice on a `0..3` grid at step `0.1` never picks a non-integer order, because integer taps have finite support and no quantization noise; on synthetic ARFIMA skylines of `2^16` samples the best order tracks `d` and beats the best integer order by 0.5 to 1.6% for `0 < d < 1`, and loses at `d = 0` and `d >= 1`. So the fractional predictor is a codec for `1/f`-type sources, not for waveforms with local polynomial structure. Program: estimate `d` on the cameraman rows of [image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md) and on textures, where long memory is plausible, and Rice-code the rounded fractional residuals there; the DTMF castles as the `d = 0` control; and use `d` as the compressibility knob, a candidate statistic for the rule-generated-castle detector in the R Division.
- [ ] **Fractional powers in the castle ring** - Kitamasa evaluates `x^w mod char(x)` in `F_p[x] / (char)`, the ring of [castle-cryptography-ring](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-ring.md). A fractional power `x^{w/2}` is a square root in that ring, and `x^{w/n}` an `n`-th root; existence and count are governed by the factorization of `char` and the gcd of `n` with the orders of the factor fields, so fractional exponentiation mod `p` is root extraction, the cousin of the discrete-log trapdoor. Program: characterize which `(char, p, n)` admit a unique `n`-th root of `x^w` (the fractional Kitamasa is then well defined and cheap), which admit many (a `1/n`-th column that is genuinely ambiguous), and which none; connect to the mod-`p` periods of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md), where a unique `M^{1/2}` exists whenever the period is odd and only half the elements have one when it is even; and add the half-step as an oracle in the castle Diffie-Hellman red-team of [castle-cryptography-round-two](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-round-two.md). Height 2 is settled for every odd prime below 300: in `F_p[x] / (x^2 - 2x + 2)` the element `x` is a square for every width iff `p = 7 (mod 8)` when `p` is inert, or `p` splits and both `1 +- i` are quadratic residues (`p = 41, 113, 137, 257, ...`, about 14% of split primes); otherwise `x^w` has a root iff `w` is even, so half of all widths have no half-step. The inert law is the norm criterion, `N(1 + i) = 2` a residue iff `p = +-1 (mod 8)`. Over `R` the half-step exists iff no negative eigenvalue, over `F_p` iff `x` is a residue in every factor field: the same obstruction in two completions, which is how the ring page should be organized. `k = 2, 3, 4` await the norm analysis factor by factor.
- [ ] **Two orders as coordinates** - the pair `(B_alpha, B_beta)` at `(alpha, beta) = (sqrt 2 - 1, pi - 3)` takes distinct values on every castle of every cell tested, `(6,3)` through `(6,5)` and `(8,3)`, to nine decimals; a single irrational order misses a few percent (the tail-blind pairs), and two lift it. So a castle is a point in the plane, recoverable from two real numbers, though only by table. Against it, `B_{1/2}` is exactly representable (an integer multiple of `2^{-(2w-2)}`) but as an integer code needs 14 bits for `(6,4)` against `log_2 3367 = 11.7` and takes only 1017 of 3367 distinct values: exactness, not compactness, is what half order buys. Program: prove the two-order injectivity or find the first collision, and a decoder that is not a lookup.
- [ ] **The poset theorem for the half-column** - among the rules with all eigenvalues positive, `I + N` with `N` acyclic, the principal half-column has nonnegative weights iff the rule is a partial order on the heights, checked for `h <= 5` (the sets coincide with the reflexive-transitive-antisymmetric rules exactly, A001035; the free strip `J`, outside the class, has the nonnegative half-column `J/sqrt(h)`); the mechanism is `sqrt(I + N) = sum_k C(1/2, k) N^k`, whose `k = 2` coefficient `-1/8` lands on every 2-chain `a < b < c` missing its direct edge, so transitivity is what keeps the root nonnegative. Program: all heights, and the explicit entries of `sqrt(I + N)` as signed chain counts. Alongside it the rate-2 reading: if `R^2 = M` with `R` a 0/1 rule, every width-`w` strip under `M` is a width-`2w - 1` strip under `R` read at every other column, so a 0/1 half-column is a finer column rate for the same language, and the metallic strips (simple negative eigenvalue, no real root) are already at their finest rate; ask which castle languages are not.
- [ ] **Fractional width as bit cost** - [castle-entropy](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md) has `log_2 F ~ w log_2 h - 1`; the real interpolant of [fractional-width-and-height](wiki/pages/fractional-width-and-height.md) makes `log_2 F(w,h)` smooth in `w`, with derivative the marginal cost of a column, `log_2 h` in the limit, and the cosine in `F(w,2)` makes the marginal cost at height 2 wobble with period 8 around one bit. Program: test the fractional bit cost against an arithmetic coder driven by the transfer matrix, and read the wobble as a coding gain or loss per column.
- [ ] **`Z(q, alpha)` as a cell fingerprint** - at an irrational order the partition function of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) is a Dirichlet polynomial with almost all exponents distinct; ask whether it answers the isospectral-style question "which cell is this" from a sample of castles, and whether two cells can share it.
- [ ] **Boundary defect of `Delta^alpha Delta^beta = Delta^{alpha+beta}` at the wall** - the semigroup law fails on a width-`w` skyline when the Grunwald-Letnikov sum is truncated at the left wall; measure the defect on the 5460 castles of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) as a function of `w`, `alpha`, `beta`; sort that page's facts into those that need the semigroup law (the ARFIMA argmin) and those that do not (`B_0 = area`, `B_1 = blocks`); in the height direction, where [fractional-width-and-height](wiki/pages/fractional-width-and-height.md) found the parity clause is a branch cut, name the group actually acting (`(C, +)` on the principal branch, or the covering group of the cut).
- [ ] **A castle object counted by `K_4`** - `K_4(w) = sum_k C(2k,k) F(w-k,4)` counts even-block height-4 castles followed by a height-4 skyline with as many 1s as 4s, cut at a marked column; find a castle family, maybe rule-changed, that it counts without the cut, or show the cut is forced.
- [ ] **A formula for `G_h(1)`** - the value at `x = 1` of the row generating function, `0, 1/2, 1/30, 1/20, 41/1740, 103/6090, 421/35490` for `h = 2..8`, is the `sqrt(pi)` coefficient of the half-sum and minus the negative-width castle count; find the formula, and say why only `h = 2` gives zero.
- [ ] **A fractional Dhar mean** - replace `L~^-1` on [castle-avalanches](wiki/pages/castle-avalanches.md) by `(L~^alpha)^-1`, the fractional Laplacian of [levy-flights](wiki/pages/levy-flights.md); does height alone still set the mean for rectangles and battlements, and do the `2 x 2` blocks still control only the tail?

- [x] **[Half-sum castles](wiki/pages/half-sum-castles.md)** - the half-sum of `F(w,h)` in the width tends to `sqrt(h/(h-1)) F(w,h)` because the pole at `1/h` beats the branch point, so `sqrt(pi)` survives only in the remainder `G_h(1)/sqrt(pi w)`, where `G_h(1)` is minus the negative-width castle count, while the half-sum shifted onto the pole gives `pi = lim w 16^w / K_4(w)^2` from integers; the integer half-sums count castle-times-balanced-tail products, and only the nondecreasing castles have an intrinsic reading (half a sum in the width is half a row in the height).
- [x] **[Power-law memory rules: the non-Markov castle strip](wiki/pages/power-law-memory-rules.md)** - the fractional 1-smooth rule `|sum_{k=0..K-1} (-1)^k C(alpha, k) c_{n-k}| <= 1` is a non-Markov strip constraint whose K-truncated transfer matrix is `h^{K-1} x h^{K-1}` and 0/1. At `h = 2, alpha in (0, 1)` the Perron root `rho(alpha, K)` climbs monotonically in `K` and **ratchets through the reachable-field census's named small Perron roots** - plastic (1.3247) at `(0.5, 6)`, supergolden (1.4656) at `(0.5, 7)` and `(0.75, 4)`, golden (1.6180) at `(0.5, 9)` and `(0.75, 5)`, plastic-squared (1.7549) at `(0.9, 6)`. Convergence to the K -> infinity limit is empirically geometric (ratios ~0.89, 0.84, 0.82 at `alpha = 0.5, 0.75, 0.9`), not the `K^{-alpha}` the tail-decay argument predicts within `K <= 16`. At `h = 3` for `alpha in (0, 1)` the growth constant equals the `h = 2` value at every `(alpha, K)` tested - the power-law-memory constraint binarizes the dominant component. - [x] **[Fractional block count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md)** - `B_alpha = sum_i max(0, Delta^alpha c_i)` equals area at `alpha = 0` and block count at `alpha = 1` exactly (5460 castles); `B_{1/2}` is dyadic and separates all ten `(4,2)` even castles; not monotone in `alpha` (829 of 5460), bounded by area, can undershoot blocks; collisions are tail-blind pairs inherited from the ascent count; position-aware; `S_0 = (-1)^{w+h+1}` so area parity is trivial while `|S_alpha|` exceeds `|S_1|` inside `(0.5, 1)`; the L1 residual argmin recovers a skyline's ARFIMA order; for nondecreasing castles `B_alpha = [x^w](1-x)^{alpha-1} C(x)` and is monotone, and `B_{1/2}(box) = h 2w C(2w,w)/4^w ~ 2h sqrt(w/pi)`.

- [x] **[Fractional recurrences: the rung between rungs](wiki/pages/fractional-recurrences.md)** - the fractional-Fibonacci `nabla^alpha a_n = a_{n-1}` has characteristic `(1-r)^alpha = r` and growth `g(alpha) = 1/r*(alpha)`, a continuous strictly increasing bijection `[0, infty) -> [1, infty)`; rational `alpha = p/q` gives algebraic growth of degree `<= max(p,q)` (small orders reproduce the census cubics: supergolden at `1/3`, plastic-squared at `2/3`, plastic-cubed at `3/2`), and irrational `alpha` gives transcendental growth by Gelfond-Schneider on `alpha = log r / log(1-r)`. The metallic ladder crosses the curve at one rational point only: **golden at exactly `alpha = 1/2`**, because `delta_1 - 1 = 1/delta_1` is a unit of `Z[phi]` while for `a >= 2` we have `N(delta_a - 1) = -a`, and the norm relation `a^m = pm 1` has no nontrivial integer solution. So silver, bronze, copper, nickel meet the curve at transcendental orders `alpha_a ≈ 1.65, 3.31, 5.36, 7.70`. The Fibonacci-Pell interpolation `nabla^alpha a_n = a_{n-1} + a_{n-2}` picks up silver as a second rational-`alpha` point (`alpha = 1`). Fractional recurrences at irrational `alpha` are the transcendental complement of the reachable-field census.
- [x] **[Levy flights on the castle graph](wiki/pages/levy-flights.md)** - the Riascos-Mateos space-fractional Laplacian `L^alpha = U diag(lambda_i^alpha) U^T` generates a long-jump walk (dense generator matrix); its heat trace `Tr exp(-t L^alpha)` is spectral, so the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair of [isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md) shares it at every `(alpha, t)`. Local return probability `[exp(-t L^alpha)]_{vv}` is eigenvector-weighted and does separate the pair: the height-3 tower tip in `B` has strictly higher return probability than the height-2 spike tips in `A` at every `alpha in {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2}` and every `t in {0.5, 1, 2, 5}` tested, and the sorted 11-value multiset differs at every `(alpha, t)`. The separator is **monotone increasing in `alpha`**: long jumps *shrink* the A/B gap rather than widen it, so Levy flights homogenize local traps - the opposite of the comb-teeth regime.
- [x] **[Diffusion on tree castles: the castle comb](wiki/pages/tree-castle-diffusion.md)** - a castle's graph is a comb (the bottom row with a tooth of `c_i - 1` cells on each column) exactly when the castle is a tree castle, so the castle comb is a tree castle and no new family. The horizontal moves of the simple random walk are the reflecting walk on the backbone, so `msd(t) = E hor(t)` until the walk can reach an end column, and a stationary step is horizontal with probability `(w - 1)/(n - 1) = (w - 1)/(w + b - 2)`, the coefficient Mendez et al. and Yuste et al. compute. On the battlement `(1, h, 1, h, ...)` the displacement is `(4 sqrt(2/pi) + o(1)) sqrt(t)` while the teeth look infinite and `2t/(h + 1)` at long times, meeting at `t = 8(h + 1)^2/pi`: the order `1/2` holds in a window that closes near `h^2`, and the shape sets the crossover and the long-time rate. Power-law tooth heights need unbounded teeth, so castles of fixed height are on the normal side (Yuste et al.); the rate is a skyline invariant, `1/2` to `4/5` on the six skylines of one graph; castles with `2 x 2` blocks are not combs and stay open.

### X Division (ciphers - keystreams, steganography, commitments)

Ciphers put the castle machinery under an adversary. The castle-as-characteristic-polynomial reading of [signed-tower-count](wiki/pages/signed-tower-count.md) turns the counting recurrence into a ring `F_p[x] / (Q)`, and the fast-index trick of [kitamasa](wiki/pages/kitamasa.md) into a modular exponentiation - the trapdoor of Diffie-Hellman. The eigenvalue-order structure of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md) becomes the discrete-log target, and the two round-one breaks (Pohlig-Hellman via char-poly factorization, Berlekamp-Massey via the count stream) are one-line applications of tools already on the wiki. On a separate axis, every skyline ([image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md), [song-as-castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md)) is a channel that can carry a hidden payload by LSB or block parity ([castle-steganography](wiki/pages/castle-steganography.md)), and the even-block clause reads as a natural commitment scheme; every isospectral pair ([isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md)) reads as an adversary's ambiguity. The Division houses the crypto series (build, red team and blue team, run three times) and the steganography line, and it is where new adversarial questions - keystream complexity, distinguishers by rule, hardness of the isospectrality census, warden statistics - land.

- [ ] **Shrinking-generator castle** - an irregularly clocked castle as the nonlinear variant that linearization does not cover.
- [ ] **Integrity check with a public order** - the keyed phase `e^{i pi B_alpha}` with `alpha` secret is broken with three known castles: `B_alpha(c)` is piecewise polynomial in `alpha`, so each known `(castle, phase)` pair leaves finitely many candidate orders (13, 15, 21 on width-12 castles) and three intersections recover the key to nine decimals. A functional polynomial in the key is not a keyed hash. What survives is an integrity check with a public `alpha`: `|S_alpha|` stays near zero to `alpha = 0.25`, then grows and exceeds `|S_1|` inside `(0.5, 1)` (`(6,4)`: 157 at `0.9` against 91), so the flat region is the least-biased choice. Program: measure how many bits of integrity the public-order phase carries against a one-cell edit, and whether any order beats the parity bit.
- [ ] **A warden that counts blocks** - channel B of [castle-steganography](wiki/pages/castle-steganography.md) leaves the per-row block parity a coin flip, and the fractional block count does not see it either: on 4000 image-like row castles (`w = 512`, `h = 256`) with parity set by a one-cell edit at a strict local extremum, the marginal of `B_alpha` at `alpha = 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1` has KS p-value `1.000` at every order (the edit moves `B_alpha` by at most `1.0` against a natural standard deviation of 180 to 24000), and a count of barely-extreme columns is 76.4 per row in cover against 76.8 in stego, KS p = 0.97. What is left is a warden that knows the cover, or a higher-order joint statistic (adjacent-row parity correlation, block-count distribution across rows).
- [ ] **In-band signaling as a castle exploit** - the PE 502 parity clause is control computed from the data skyline; characterize the single-column and multi-column edits that change the skyline without flipping block parity (measured 0.438 flip rate at `(33,16)`), and the reverse.
- [ ] **Avalanche-profile hash** - the sink avalanche profile on [sandpile-identity](wiki/pages/sandpile-identity.md) (topplings from one grain on every cell, starting at every sink cell's identity) behaves like a unique fingerprint of the castle graph: no two castles that color refinement proves different share one up to 13 cells. Read it as a hash of castle graphs: how short a digest of it stays collision-free, whether a castle can be recovered from its profile (one-wayness), how fast collisions appear if it is truncated, and whether a keyed variant (secret sink cell, secret drop order) is a usable MAC; the tide profile is no candidate, since it depends only on the runs of raised columns. By design it is isomorphism-invariant, so a castle and its mirror image always collide.
- [ ] **Sandpile discrete log** - a castle's sandpile group used as a discrete-log group is weak by design, because `L~^-1` turns the logarithm into linear algebra; write the break as a red-team contrast to the torus of [castle-cryptography-round-three](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-round-three.md), measured on the same largest-invariant-factor yardstick (own reasoning; no sandpile-cryptography literature read).
- [ ] **Lock-and-key castles** - a pin-tumbler key is a castle: one column per pin position, height = cut depth. The lock's pin stacks are the matching castle, and the key opens the lock exactly when the heights agree column by column. The locksmith's maximum-adjacent-cut rule (neighboring cuts may not differ by too much) is a bounded-step rule like the smooth strips on [castle-strip](wiki/pages/castle-strip.md), so the keyspace of a blank is a transfer-matrix count. Master-key systems are sets of castles sharing a master, and the known privilege-escalation weakness of master-keyed locks (probing one pin position at a time) is a one-column search. Also: generating castles with distinct fingerprints as unique keys.

### The Telephone Company

Phones, audio, coding-theory-adjacent applied mathematics with an AT&T Bell Labs vibe. Signal processing, waveform codecs, keyed voice channels, DTMF and MF tone catalogs, expander-graph and Ihara-zeta reads of the castle graph, information-theoretic entropy of skylines, and the "hear the shape of a castle" isospectral seminar all live here.
- [x] **[Mersenne castles](wiki/pages/mersenne-castles.md)** - neighboring columns, read as strings whose lengths are their heights (Mersenne's law), sound a unison, an octave, a fifth or a fourth: all castles at height 2, the 1-smooth castles at height 3 (silver by width), `3.1701` by width at height 4, the root of `x^3 - 4x^2 + 2x + 2`. A consonance keeps the part of a height prime to 6, so the count at height `h` is the count at its largest divisor `2^a 3^b`, and only the flat castles are left at heights prime to 6. On the heights `2^i 3^j` the rule is the triangular lattice with a loop at each point, since a fourth and a fifth make an octave; its largest eigenvalue `rho(h)` stays below 7 and tends to 7, with `7 - rho(h) = O(1/log h)`, and the castles of height `h` grow at `rho` of that divisor.
  - [ ] **The approach to 7** - find the rate at which `rho(h)`, the largest eigenvalue of the consonance rule on the heights `2^i 3^j <= h`, tends to 7; [mersenne-castles](wiki/pages/mersenne-castles.md) proves `7 - rho(h) = O(1/log h)` from the average number of consonant heights, and `(7 - rho(h))(ln h)^2` rises from `23.5` at `h = 32` to `59.7` at `h = 2^40`. The triangle of heights `2^i 3^j <= h` has about `(ln h)^2/(2 ln 2 ln 3)` points.

- [x] **[Tone-castle catalog](wiki/pages/tone-castle-catalog.md)** - every DTMF and MF digit at 8 kHz as an exactly periodic castle (period `8000/gcd(8000,f)`), with DFT support and Goertzel response; which tone pairs are exactly periodic and which only approximately. Done: all 15 MF codes have w = 80 (2.7+ periods per burst); DTMF w = 8000 except keys 5 (4000) and 8 (2000), longer than a 45-55 ms burst; supports fill whole divisor classes (30 of 35 castles all bins); rise(c) odd for every signal, so burst parity alternates with the period count; the DTMF Goertzel block (N = 136) picks up leakage from the castle's area.
- [ ] **Lossless song codec on castles** - close the gap between the 9.5% LPC-8 + Rice estimate and the 26.6% `afconvert` FLAC on the same file, and state the castle codec (skyline -> Tier-1 rule + residual) as a spec. One internal coder (Q12 taps, Rice residual, 4096-sample blocks, both channels) gives FLAC fixed predictors of orders 0 to 3 at 30.7 / 16.9 / 11.6 / 12.1% and least-squares LPC of orders 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 at 18.4 / 13.2 / 12.7 / 12.8%; the fractional rung between them never wins (F Division, Hurst item), so the spec is integer-order.
- [ ] **The Woodward gap as a block-count meter** - encode a skyline as a tone whose instantaneous frequency is the column height; Woodward's theorem says the tone's power spectrum approaches the height histogram as the modulation slows, and the block count is what makes it fast. Fit the spectrum-to-histogram distance as a function of block count over random castles at fixed histogram, and ask which pairs (same histogram, same block count) the audio spectrum still cannot separate - the audio-spectral analog of the isospectral census.
- [ ] **Ramanujan castles / Ihara zeta** - `zeta_C(u)` over prime closed walks on the castle graph; the Ramanujan castle as the spectrally most expander-like shape at given size; longer-horizon, hooks into arithmetic combinatorics.
- [ ] **1/f noise of a sandcastle** - drop grains at random under the tide and record the toppling rate per round (topplings in each parallel round) as a signal; compute its power spectrum and exponent `beta` and compare with `2 - alpha_w` from the weighted durations ([bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](wiki/pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md) found `beta ~ 1.57` in 2D, `0.95` with open edges); then play it: the rectangle's signal against the battlement's (one-dimensional spikes, expected near white noise), through the Telephone Company's tone and codec tooling, as what a sandcastle sounds like.

- [x] **[What survives a phone line](wiki/pages/castle-phone-line.md)** - row castles played as pitch-stepping tones (like a dial-up modem, not FM radio) through a simulated phone line: the odd/even block count stays at a coin flip until the line is nearly perfect, the height histogram and slow skyline trends always survive, and hiding each bit as height 0 or 3 modulo 6 instead of in the lowest bit brings the 56-character message back exact from one 2-second row.
- [x] **[Castle entropy and compression](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md)** - information-theoretic view: uniform entropy `~ w log_2 h - 1`, growth constants as topological entropies, and the compressibility axis on [castle-compression](wiki/pages/castle-compression.md).
- [x] **[A song as a castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md)** - three rungs on Rick Astley's "Never Gonna Give You Up" as the running example: the 66-bit YouTube id `dQw4w9WgXcQ` is a `(17,16)` or `(12,64)` castle by exact rank/unrank; a generic ID3-style label is `w~200` at `h=256` and a 128-bit acoustic fingerprint is a `(17,256)` castle (the URL is `w=9` at that height); a peak-normalized 16-bit waveform is an `h=65536` castle whose codec ladder (WAV / FLAC / MP3 / AAC / Opus) is the tier ladder, with Berlekamp-Massey and the NTT over `F_65537` as the finite-field layer; seminar seed on 2600 Hz, blue-box, and jump-the-5 castles; crown-jewel finale ties every tool to **Beethoven's Ninth**, the Furtwängler-1951 recording whose 74-minute length set the Red Book CD (`w = 391,608,000` at `h = 65536`; 78-min high-density variant is `w = 412,776,000`), including the pianissimo A-E opening as a two-atom Tier-0 tone castle and the "Ode to Joy" phrase as a diatonic `h=5` castle.

### Facilities

Applied, physical-world tooling: turning real objects into castles.

- [ ] **Key photographs to castles** - a converter from a photograph of a key to its castle: find the blade, read the bitting (cut depth at each pin position) against the key blank's standard spacing and depth increments, and output the castle of heights. Test on photographs of common house keys; report where the reading fails (wear, angle, lighting).
- [ ] **Standard key blanks as castle families** - collect manufacturer bitting specifications (number of pin positions, number of depths, maximum adjacent cut) for common blanks, and tabulate each blank as a castle family with its keyspace size; feeds the lock-and-key item in the X Division.
- [ ] **Cities as castles** - famous city skylines (building heights read left to right from a fixed viewpoint) as castles, with their block counts, parity and entropy; then use the skyline as a lead-in to fractals: zooming into a skyline changes how much detail each column carries, the way measuring a coastline with a shorter ruler makes it longer (the coastline paradox), so the skyline's length and block count as functions of resolution give a fractal dimension. Ties to [image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md) (an image read as skylines) and [castle-entropy](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md).
- [ ] **Mountains as castles** - mountain ranges and ridgelines as castles: national-park skylines and city mountainscapes (the Wasatch Front behind Salt Lake City, for example) sampled from elevation profiles into column heights; compare their block statistics and roughness with city skylines, and ask whether a ridgeline's castle has a stable fractal dimension as the sampling gets finer. BTW suggest that a landscape raised by slow tectonics reaches a self-similar critical state with slides on all scales ([bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](wiki/pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md)); test whether ridgeline castles show power-law statistics (drops, plateau lengths) that a random skyline does not.
- [ ] **Ballparks as castles** - start at home plate with the ground as the base row and read each ray out of home plate as a castle: the field is a long flat run, the outfield wall is a sudden column, and the stands behind it continue the skyline. A ray that stops at the wall is exactly the **hook** of [castle-classification-shape](wiki/pages/castle-classification-shape.md) (`c_1 = h`, every other column 1): it carries only two numbers, distance and wall height, like a ruler. The stands, tiers and porches behind the wall are what make each ray an interesting castle, and they need real 3D park geometry (outfield fence distances and heights, stand heights and tiers; for example the park data in baseball simulation databases such as OOTP's, or public park-dimension data, subject to their terms). A castle cannot overhang (every block rests on the one below), so an upper deck that hangs over the seats beneath is recorded only by its upper outline; how much each park loses to that rule is itself a statistic. One park is then a fan of castles, one per angle - the two foul lines, left-center, center, right-center, plus signature features (Fenway Park's Green Monster, the pool beyond the right-center wall at Chase Field, formerly Bank One Ballpark). Comparing the 30 MLB parks, two at a time or all at once, is an R Division representation question: how to encode a fan of castles as one object and put two parks side by side.
- [ ] **BTW's shoebox on a castle mold** - [bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality](wiki/pages/bak-tang-wiesenfeld-1988-self-organized-criticality.md) ends with a home experiment: wet sand piled steeply in the corner of a shoebox produces slides of all sizes as it dries; run it in a castle-shaped mold (a skyline template as the back wall, the floor as the tide), photograph the slides, and compare their size statistics with the simulated tide avalanches of [castle-avalanches](wiki/pages/castle-avalanches.md).
- [ ] **Elemental ladders: naming the rungs past bronze** - de Spinadel's metal names on the `q = 1` ladder stop at bronze (`delta_3`); her copper and nickel are `(p, q) = (1, 2)` and `(1, 3)`, off the ladder, and she writes them with element subscripts (`sigma_Ag`, `sigma_Br`, `sigma_Cu`, `sigma_Ni`) ([metallic-means](wiki/pages/metallic-means.md)). No primary source names `delta_4` or above, and a literature check found no alkali-metal or atomic-number naming of any constants. Decide whether the wiki ever names the higher rungs or stays with `delta_a`; any naming must encode a castle invariant (min height, field, norm), not decorate. The `(p1, p2)` triangle on [quadratic-min-height](wiki/pages/quadratic-min-height.md) (row `p1` holds `p1` pairs, all first at height `p1 + 1`, closed by the whole number `p1 + 1`) is the structure such a naming would have to respect.
- [ ] **Two degree axes for the metallic ladder** - the real roots of `x^3 = x^2 + 1` (`1.4656`) and `x^3 = 2x^2 + 1` (`2.2056`) belong to the axis `x^k = a x^{k-1} + 1`. The first is the wiki's "supergolden", Crilly's supergolden ratio ("A supergolden rectangle", Math. Gazette 78 (1994) 320-325, p. 322-323, where it is written `psi`); the second has no name in any primary source found. Gil and Worley's degree-`k` metallic means ([arXiv:1901.02619](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02619), Fibonacci Quart. 57 (2019) 45-50) are a different axis: the `m`-th mean of degree `k` is the positive root of `x^k - m x^{k-1} - x^{k-2} - ... - x - 1`, from invert transforms of the `k`-nacci numbers, so the cubic golden, silver and bronze means are tribonacci `1.8393`, `2.5468` and `3.383`. Both axes already touch castles: supergolden is the area growth of the q-Fibonacci castles ([q-fibonacci-castle](wiki/pages/q-fibonacci-castle.md)), the `x^3 = 2x^2 + 1` root is an unnamed `h = 3` Perron root (6 matrices) in [reachable-field-census](wiki/pages/reachable-field-census.md), and the degree-`k` golden mean is the `k`-nacci area growth of all castles of exact height `k` ([castles-of-height-h-by-area](wiki/pages/castles-of-height-h-by-area.md)). Gil and Worley count tilings by tiles of lengths `1..k` with the length-1 tile in `m` colors; for `k >= 3` these read as castles by area with column heights `1..k` and the height-1 columns in `m` colors. List the `x^3 = 2x^2 + 1` root in the census, find which degree-`k` rungs with `m >= 2` (`2.5468` first) are strip Perron roots and at what height, and decide whether the colored-column castles earn a page.
