## The Abelian Group (not a Division)

The Abelian Group is not a Division. It has no letter, no descriptor, no crossover row, and no pages of its own. It opens cases against the foundations the Divisions stand on and files each finding back to the Division under review. The premise: the wiki's load-bearing tricks - the parity sign and the `(T±P)/2` projector, the roots-of-unity generalization, the mod-`p` periods, the `JD` involution behind the sector factorization, the skyline DFT, the cycle rank of the castle graph, the metallic means as units, the fractional order knob - are each a statement about a finite or finitely generated abelian group (a character of `Z/m`, the unit group of a ring or a number field, the cokernel of a Laplacian, a one-parameter group of operators) that no page names. Naming the group does two things. It turns a trick into an instance of a theorem: character orthogonality, the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem, Dirichlet's unit theorem, cyclic sieving. And it locates exactly where commutativity fails and a Division's foundation is weaker than it reads: the mirror does not commute with the cyclic shift, the castle monoid is free, truncated fractional operators lose the semigroup law. Every case ends in one of three findings - the foundation is an abelian group and here is the theorem; it is not, and here is the obstruction; or it is a group nobody needed, and the page should say so. Ten founding cases, all feeding S14; each names the Divisions it reviews.

- [ ] **Case 1 - every sign is a character** - the `(T±P)/2` projector is Fourier inversion on `Z/2`, the `m`-th-root generalization on [parity-via-roots-of-unity](wiki/pages/parity-via-roots-of-unity.md) is Fourier inversion on `Z/m`, the block-count distribution read off the Narayana polynomial at roots of unity is the DFT of that distribution, the skyline DFT on [spectral-analysis](wiki/pages/spectral-analysis.md) is Fourier on `Z/w`, and the mod-`p` period on [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md) is the order of `x` in a cyclic group. Five pages, one theorem: the characters of a finite abelian group are orthogonal and the group is its own dual. Write the theorem down once, with Parseval and Poisson summation, and re-derive the five uses as instances. Then state the one thing the abelian theory cannot supply: the mirror `c_i -> c_{w+1-i}` does not commute with the cyclic shift, so the "modulo cyclic shift" DFT invariant and the "mirror-deduped" census live under a dihedral group whose characters are not all one-dimensional. Reviews: E, S, N. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 2 - the parity clause is not a character** - `{1..h}^w` is already a mixed-radix space on [aocp-generating-permutations-tuples](wiki/pages/aocp-generating-permutations-tuples.md); read modulo `h` it is the group `Z_h^w`, and the sign `s(C) = (-1)^blocks` is a function on it. If `s` were a character, `F(w,h)` would be a one-line sum. It is not: the block count is `sum_i max(0, c_i - c_{i-1})`, piecewise linear with a kink at every level column, so the case is how far from a character it is. Compute the Fourier transform of `s` over `Z_h^w` for small `(w,h)`, its largest coefficient and its spectral concentration, and the Gowers `U^2` norm as a linearity score; find the largest subgroup `H < Z_h^w` on whose cosets block parity is constant (the in-band-signaling edits of the Misc Division are exactly moves inside such a coset); and ask whether the `alpha`-family `P_alpha` of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) gets closer to a character at any order, which would make one `alpha` the most linear reading of the clause. Reviews: E, X, F. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 3 - the full commutant of `M_k`** - [tower-parity-sectors](wiki/pages/tower-parity-sectors.md) found one signed permutation `JD` commuting with the signed transfer matrix, with `(JD)^2 = (-1)^k I`: a `Z/2` action for even `k`, a `Z/4` action for odd `k`, whose characters are the fourth roots of unity, which is why odd `k` splits over `Q(i)` and not over `Q`. Nobody asked for the whole group. Enumerate every monomial matrix (signed permutation of the heights) commuting with `M_k` for `k <= 12`, determine whether the group is abelian, and decompose `Q^{k+1}` into its isotypic components: if the group is exactly `<JD>`, the two-sector factorization of `char_k` is complete and the irreducibility of each sector over `Q` has a group-theoretic reason; if larger, `char_k` factors further and the gallery has missed a sector. Then the strip matrices of [castle-strip](wiki/pages/castle-strip.md) and the minimum-gap matrices of [tower-spacing-castles](wiki/pages/tower-spacing-castles.md), where height reversal may or may not survive the rule change. Reviews: N, E, Z. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 4 - cyclic sieving at `q = -1` is the involution Hardin is missing** - the sign-reversing involution the Numbers Division wants for Hardin's `2^L` ([hardin-word-identity](wiki/pages/hardin-word-identity.md)) is a `Z/2` action on towers whose fixed points are the survivors, and `2^L` is the orbit count of a free `(Z/2)^L` action, one involution per column. The cyclic sieving phenomenon (Reiner-Stanton-White 2004) supplies such actions by theorem: a finite set with a `Z/n` action and a polynomial `X(q)` with `X(omega^d) = |Fix(c^d)|`. Its founding example is the Narayana polynomial under rotation of noncrossing partitions, and the block-count distribution of a tower is Narayana ([tower-narayana-polynomial](wiki/pages/tower-narayana-polynomial.md)), so `P(k,L)` at `q = -1` would be the fixed-point count of a half-turn. Program: make the rotation explicit on towers through the cycle-forest form, verify sieving numerically for `L <= 6`, check whether its half-turn is sign-reversing on towers with fixed points `2^L W_m(L+1)`, then run the same test on the area-graded polynomials of the Q Division ([castle-by-area](wiki/pages/castle-by-area.md), [tree-castle-by-area](wiki/pages/tree-castle-by-area.md)): a `q`-polynomial that sieves has a canonical cyclic symmetry the Division has not been using. Reviews: N, Q, E. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 5 - the castle monoid and its abelianization** - castles concatenate at height-1 columns, and the Klarner decomposition `A = P/(1 - P)` of the Q Division says the castle monoid is free on the prime castles: not abelian. But `blocks(C_1 C_2) = blocks(C_1) + blocks(C_2) - 1` (the base row is one block across the join), so `blocks - 1` is a monoid homomorphism to `(Z, +)` and the sign `(-1)^{blocks - 1}` is a one-dimensional character of a free monoid, which means it factors through the abelianization, the free commutative monoid of multisets of primes. Program: write the signed Klarner identity `A_s = P_s / (1 - P_s)` with `P_s` the signed prime GF and check it against the parity split on [castle-by-area](wiki/pages/castle-by-area.md); count castles per multiset of primes (a multinomial) and decide which other castle statistics are monoid characters (area is; test peaks, records, and the nonlocal `B_alpha`, which cannot be); and check whether the join survives the tower-spacing and no-touching rules of the Z Division, where a height-1 column may no longer separate. Reviews: Q, E, Z. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 6 - kernel and cokernel of the castle's boundary map** - "cycle rank = number of 2x2 filled blocks" on [castle-graph](wiki/pages/castle-graph.md) is the statement `H_1(G; Z) = Z^{#2x2}`, true because the filled polyomino is contractible (every column is filled from the base, so there are no holes) and filling each 2x2 square kills exactly one cycle. The same boundary matrix `∂: Z^E -> Z^V` has `H_1` as its kernel and the sandpile group `K(G)` as the torsion of the cokernel of `L = ∂ ∂^T`: a finite abelian group whose order is the number of spanning trees (Kirchhoff) and whose invariant factors are the Smith normal form of `L`. The Laplacian spectrum fixes the order but not the group. Program: exhibit the 2x2 squares as a `Z`-basis of the cycle lattice and check unimodularity against a spanning-tree basis; compute `K(G)` for every castle to 16 cells; tabulate which isospectral pairs on [isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md) it separates (the tree pairs at 11 and 16 cells have trivial `K` and are safe; the 10-cell adjacency pair and the 50 adjacency groups at 16 cells are the targets); find the smallest pair with equal Laplacian spectrum and non-isomorphic `K`, and the smallest with isomorphic `K` and different spectrum, which settles whether `K` is finer or merely different on castles. Reviews: S, R, Z. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 7 - the identity element of the castle sandpile** - the abelian sandpile model on the castle graph with the base row as sink: chips on cells, toppling at degree, recurrent configurations in bijection with spanning trees by Dhar's burning algorithm, and the recurrent configurations forming the group `K(G)` of Case 6 under addition-and-stabilization. Program: compute and draw the identity element for the golden and silver castles and the 10-cell isospectral pair, measure how many topplings a single chip at the apex triggers as a function of shape, and ask whether the identity element or the toppling count is a spectral invariant; a non-spectral one is a new separator for S4 and the second drawing its centerpiece needs. Reviews: S, Z. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 8 - the ladder lives in a unit group** - each metallic mean is a fundamental unit of `Q(sqrt(a^2 + 4))` ([metallic-means](wiki/pages/metallic-means.md)), whose unit group is `Z/2 x Z`, and `copper = delta_4 = phi^3` says the height-5 strip lands on the cube of the generator of `Z[phi]^*`; the plastic number is the fundamental unit of a cubic field ([plastic-number](wiki/pages/plastic-number.md)). Program: for every Perron root in [reachable-field-census](wiki/pages/reachable-field-census.md), decide whether it is a unit at all (the signed transfer eigenvalues are twice algebraic units, so they are not), and if so locate it in the unit group of its field: which power of a fundamental unit for the quadratics, which lattice point of `Z^r` for the cubics (Dirichlet: `r = 1` for one real root, `r = 2` for totally real). The ladder becomes a subset of a finitely generated abelian group, the min-height law for `(p1,p2)` becomes a question about which lattice points are realized, and the F Division's rungs between rungs are the points not in the group at all. The torsion `Z/2` is the sign, and the norm `+-1` is the character of the unit group that the continued-fraction period parity already reads. Reviews: N, F. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 9 - the castle ring's unit group, by invariant factors** - `F_p[x] / (char_k)` splits by CRT into a product of finite fields when `char_k` is squarefree mod `p`, so its unit group is a product of cyclic groups `Z/(p^{d_i} - 1)`; Pohlig-Hellman is the structure theorem for finite abelian groups applied to that product, the mod-`p` period is the order of `x` in it, Kitamasa is exponentiation in it, and the two `d=3` linear-complexity deficits are coincidences of root orders inside its factors. Program: write the invariant-factor decomposition for every `(char_k, p)` the pages of [castle-cryptography-ring](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-ring.md) and [castle-cryptography-round-two](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-round-two.md) use, read each round's security as the largest invariant factor and both breaks as one statement about it; then the discriminant-zero primes of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md), where `char_k` has a repeated factor `g^m`, the ring is not semisimple, and the unit group acquires a `p`-group `1 + (g)` whose order is the extra factor `p` the observatory measured in the period; and the F Division's fractional Kitamasa as root extraction in this group, unique exactly when `n` is coprime to every invariant factor. Reviews: X, N, F. (S14)
- [ ] **Case 10 - Burnside on the census, and the order knob at the wall** - two foundations that divide by a symmetry without naming its group. First, [isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md) is "mirror-deduped," the skyline DFT on [spectral-analysis](wiki/pages/spectral-analysis.md) is "a complete invariant modulo cyclic shift," and the Z Division counts shapes up to reflection: mirror is `Z/2`, cyclic shift is `Z/w`, and together they generate the dihedral `D_w`, which is not abelian, so the census's groups depend on which is meant. For mirror alone over all castles with `n <= 16` cells there are `65,535` compositions and `765` palindromic ones, so Burnside gives `33,150` mirror classes and `(2^{n-1} + 2^{floor(n/2)}) / 2` per `n`; state the group on each page and reconcile the stated counts. Second, every F Division claim rests on `Delta^alpha Delta^beta = Delta^{alpha + beta}`, the statement that `alpha -> Delta^alpha` is a homomorphism from `(R, +)` into the commutative algebra generated by the shift; on a width-`w` skyline with the Grunwald-Letnikov sum cut at the left wall it fails by a boundary term. Measure the defect on the 5460 castles of [fractional-block-count](wiki/pages/fractional-block-count.md) as a function of `w`, `alpha`, `beta`; sort that page's facts into those that need the group law (the ARFIMA argmin) and those that do not (`B_0 = area`, `B_1 = blocks`); and in the height direction, where [fractional-width-and-height](wiki/pages/fractional-width-and-height.md) found the parity clause is a branch cut, name the group actually acting: `(C, +)` on the principal branch, or the covering group of the cut. Reviews: Z, S, R, F. (S14)

### F Division (fractional calculus - castles at non-integer order)

Fractional calculus defines the `alpha`-th derivative and integral for real `alpha`, and its three classical definitions each land on a castle object the wiki already owns. Grunwald-Letnikov is a limit of binomially weighted finite differences: on a skyline it is the column difference `c_i - c_{i-1}` (whose negative part counts blocks) with the binomial weights `(-1)^k C(alpha, k)` run out to every earlier column. Riemann-Liouville is convolution with a power-law kernel then differentiation: on a generating function it is multiplication by `(1 - x)^{-alpha}`, the fractional partial sum. Caputo differentiates first and keeps classical initial conditions: on a C-finite recurrence it is the version that respects the initial terms Kitamasa starts from. Every fractional operator is nonlocal - the value at one column or one index depends on all the others with power-law weights - and that nonlocality is the whole content of the Division: power-law memory along the width, heavy-tailed jumps on the castle graph, and dense operators where the integer-order castle has sparse ones. Ten items, foundational rather than incremental; each is meant to open a line of work, not close one.

- [ ] **Half-sum castles: fractional partial sums and the `sqrt(pi)` door** - the `alpha`-th fractional partial sum of a count sequence has GF `A(x) / (1 - x)^alpha` (Cesaro means of order `alpha`); `alpha = 1` is the cumulative count by width, `alpha = 1/2` the half-cumulative. For rational `A` and `alpha = 1/2` the GF is algebraic of degree 2, the class of the tower word A004149 and of Catalan and Motzkin: half a sum moves a regular language's count into the context-free class, and the question is whether the half-summed castle count is itself the count of a natural context-free castle object. Asymptotically the transfer theorem gives `n^{alpha - 1} / Gamma(alpha)` at the `x = 1` singularity, and `Gamma(1/2) = sqrt(pi)`, so `pi` enters the coefficients of an exactly specified castle sequence, in the constant rather than only in a limit. The half-sum of the constant sequence is `C(2n, n) / 4^n`, so Wallis is the `alpha = 1/2` case; this is a new route through the [algebraic-transcendental-wall](wiki/pages/algebraic-transcendental-wall.md) for the T Division. Program: tabulate the half-sums of `F(w,h)` for `h = 2..5`, check OEIS, and find the castle object they count. (S9)
- [ ] **Mittag-Leffler generating functions: from OGF to EGF in one parameter** - the Mittag-Leffler function `E_alpha(z) = sum z^n / Gamma(alpha n + 1)` is the fractional exponential; the transform `sum a_n x^n / Gamma(alpha n + 1)` is the ordinary GF at `alpha = 0` and the exponential GF at `alpha = 1`. Castles are unlabelled and live on OGFs; permutations and `(n-1)!` cycles are labelled and live on EGFs; [castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count](wiki/pages/castles-as-upgraded-cycle-count.md) bridges them by three discrete upgrades. This gives the bridge a continuous parameter. Program: apply the Mittag-Leffler transform to `F(w,h)`, to `P(k,L)`, and to the Foata-form permutation counts, and ask at which `alpha` the castle GF and the permutation GF are closest; separately, the fractional Poisson process (Mittag-Leffler waiting times) as an eleventh sampler technique for the T Division. (S1)
- [ ] **Fractional recurrences: the rung between rungs** - the metallic ladder `delta_a = (a + sqrt(a^2 + 4)) / 2` is a continuous function of `a` but only integer `a` is realized by a 0/1 strip; the reachable-field census is a lattice inside a continuum. Discrete fractional difference equations (Atici-Eloe nabla calculus) give a second continuum: `Delta^alpha a_n = a_{n-1}` and its relatives, solved by discrete Mittag-Leffler functions, with order `alpha` sweeping between the order-1 recurrence (geometric) and the order-2 recurrence (Fibonacci, Pell). Program: compute the growth constant of the fractional Fibonacci / Pell family as a function of `alpha`, show whether it hits the metallic means anywhere off the integers, and settle whether the growth constants are algebraic at all - the characteristic equation carries `(1 - 1/z)^alpha`, so the expectation is that every non-integer `alpha` produces a transcendental growth constant, which would make fractional recurrences the complement of the reachable-field census: the constants a castle strip can never realize. (S2)
- [ ] **The castle is a comb: subdiffusion at order `1/2`** - a tree castle, and any castle with a height-1 backbone, is a finite comb: a backbone with teeth of heights `c_i - 1`. Diffusion on a comb is the classical example of subdiffusion: a walker on the backbone is trapped in teeth, mean-square displacement grows like `t^{1/2}`, and the continuum limit is a time-fractional diffusion equation of order `1/2` (Arkhincheev-Baskin 1991, Weiss-Havlin). So the polyomino graph of [castle-graph](wiki/pages/castle-graph.md) is a fractional-diffusion medium and the order of the equation is a shape invariant. Program: measure the MSD exponent by simulation on castles across the shape axes (tree, convex, valley, battlement) and across the height-distribution of teeth; confirm `1/2` for uniform teeth and find how it moves for power-law tooth heights (the Hurst / ARFIMA item below supplies those) and for castles with 2x2 blocks (where the graph is no longer a tree); ask whether the exponent sees the parity clause at all. This is the [castle-graph](wiki/pages/castle-graph.md) counterpart of the pi-via-random-walks item in the T Division. (S13)
- [ ] **Levy flights on the castle graph: the fractional Laplacian** - the space-fractional operator `L^alpha` (Riascos-Mateos 2014) on the polyomino graph is dense: every cell talks to every cell, with weights decaying in graph distance. The random walk it generates has long jumps, the opposite regime from the comb's trapping, and the pair (trapping in teeth, jumps across the moat) is the castle's version of the two anomalous-diffusion regimes. Program: the fractional heat trace `Tr exp(-t L^alpha)` as a family of spectral invariants; it is a function of the Laplacian spectrum, so the 11-cell Laplacian-isospectral tree pair of [isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md) shares every one of them - but the return probability at a named cell is not spectral, so the question is whether the fractional walk's return time at the apex separates the pair. Also the numerics: `L^alpha` on a `w x h` castle is a dense `wh x wh` matrix, exactly the storage problem of the fractional-PDE literature, and the castle's Toeplitz structure along each column is the fast-convolution handle. (S4)
- [ ] **The Hurst exponent of a skyline: fractionally integrated castles** - a skyline is a time series, and the fractional integration order `d` of ARFIMA, `(1 - B)^d c = white noise`, is a discrete fractional derivative of the column heights. `d = 0` is i.i.d. columns (the uniform castle of [castle-entropy](wiki/pages/castle-entropy.md)); `d = 1` is a random-walk skyline (the 1-smooth Motzkin strips of [pell-castle-strip](wiki/pages/pell-castle-strip.md)); `0 < d < 1` is long memory, `d = 1/2` is `1/f` noise. Program: estimate `d` on the wiki's real skylines - the cameraman rows of [image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md), the audio waveform of [song-as-castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md) (Voss-Clarke 1975 predicts `d` near `1/2` for music), the DTMF castles (`d = 0` up to the periodicity) - and use `d` as the compressibility knob: the LPC residual of [castle-compression](wiki/pages/castle-compression.md) is smallest when the predictor's order matches `d`, so `d` is a candidate statistic for the rule-generated-castle detector in the Enumeration Division. (S12)
- [ ] **Power-law memory rules: the non-Markov castle strip** - every strip rule on [castle-strip](wiki/pages/castle-strip.md) is Markov: whether height `b` may follow height `a` depends on `a` alone. A fractional strip rule weights every earlier column, the column `k` back by `C(alpha, k)`, the Grunwald-Letnikov kernel; the constraint has power-law memory and the exact transfer matrix is infinite. This is the fractional-numerics problem of the FMATH literature in castle form: truncating memory to `K` columns gives an `h^K`-state transfer matrix, dense where the Markov one was sparse, and the growth constant `rho(alpha, K)` converges in `K` at a power-law rate set by `alpha`. Program: build the truncated matrices for `h = 2, 3` and small `K`, measure the convergence, and look for a fast-convolution evaluation of the count using the binomial Toeplitz structure of the kernel (the NTT over `F_65537` on [song-as-castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md) is the right tool); then ask whether the `K -> infinity` growth constant is a new kind of number for the Numbers Division or lands in the reachable-field census after all. (S13)
- [ ] **Fractional powers in the castle ring** - Kitamasa evaluates `x^w mod char(x)` in `F_p[x] / (char)`, the ring of [castle-cryptography-ring](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-ring.md). A fractional power `x^{w/2}` is a square root in that ring, and `x^{w/n}` an `n`-th root; existence and count are governed by the factorization of `char` and the gcd of `n` with the orders of the factor fields, so fractional exponentiation mod `p` is root extraction, the cousin of the discrete-log trapdoor. Program: characterize which `(char, p, n)` admit a unique `n`-th root of `x^w` (the fractional Kitamasa is then well defined and cheap), which admit many (a `1/n`-th column that is genuinely ambiguous), and which none; connect to the mod-`p` periods of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md), where a unique `M^{1/2}` exists whenever the period is odd and only half the elements have one when it is even; and add the half-step as an oracle in the castle Diffie-Hellman red-team of [castle-cryptography-round-two](wiki/pages/castle-cryptography-round-two.md). (S5)

### X Division (ciphers - keystreams, steganography, commitments)

Ciphers put the castle machinery under an adversary. The castle-as-characteristic-polynomial reading of [signed-tower-count](wiki/pages/signed-tower-count.md) turns the counting recurrence into a ring `F_p[x] / (Q)`, and the fast-index trick of [kitamasa](wiki/pages/kitamasa.md) into a modular exponentiation - the trapdoor of Diffie-Hellman. The eigenvalue-order structure of [mod-p-observatory](wiki/pages/mod-p-observatory.md) becomes the discrete-log target, and the two round-one breaks (Pohlig-Hellman via char-poly factorization, Berlekamp-Massey via the count stream) are one-line applications of tools already on the wiki. On a separate axis, every skyline ([image-as-castle](wiki/pages/image-as-castle.md), [song-as-castle](wiki/pages/song-as-castle.md)) is a channel that can carry a hidden payload by LSB or block parity ([castle-steganography](wiki/pages/castle-steganography.md)), and the even-block clause reads as a natural commitment scheme; every isospectral pair ([isospectral-castles](wiki/pages/isospectral-castles.md)) reads as an adversary's ambiguity. The Division houses the three-seminar crypto arc (build, round two, blue team) and the steganography line, and it is where new adversarial questions - keystream complexity, distinguishers by rule, hardness of the isospectrality census, warden statistics - land.
