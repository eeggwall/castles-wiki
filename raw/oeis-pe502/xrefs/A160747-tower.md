## Identity and proof

Here N(n,k) = binomial(n,k)*binomial(n,k-1)/n is the Narayana number, A001263(n,k).

**Castles = 6-cores.** A castle of width 5 with column heights c(1), ..., c(5) >= 1 has
c(1) + Sum_{i=2..5} max(0, c(i) - c(i-1)) blocks. Cho, Huh, Nam, and Sohn, Theorem 3.3
((a) <-> (b)), map a 6-core λ with m corners to the sequence (n_0, ..., n_5), where n_i is
the number of beads in column i of its 6-abacus. Then n_0 = 0, and
Sum_{i=1..6} |n_i - n_{i-1}| = 2m with n_6 := 0. The theorem carries a bound n_i <= k
(hook length h(1,1) < 6k). It costs nothing here: every n_i <= m, so k = m covers all 6-cores
with m corners. A sequence that starts and ends at 0 rises as much as it falls. So the
condition is n_1 + Sum_{i=2..5} max(0, n_i - n_{i-1}) = m, which says the castle with column
heights c(i) = n_i + 1 has m + 1 blocks. Castles of width 5 with m + 1 blocks are therefore
in bijection with 6-cores with m corners. Their Proposition 3.4 counts the latter:

    cc(6, m) = Sum_{i=1..min(m,3)} N(m,i) * binomial(2m+6-2i, 2m),        m >= 1,

and as polynomials in m this equals 1 + 7m(m+1)(m^2+m+2)/4, Mathar's closed form on the entry
(checked symbolically). a(0) = 1 is the castle of height 1, the bottom row alone (the empty
partition). On p. 14 the paper
itself points to A160747 for t = 6 and to A063490 for t = 5.

**Narayana numerator.** The coefficient of x^n in x^(k-1)/(1-x)^5 is binomial(n+5-k, 4), and
the entry's g.f. numerator 1 + 10x + 20x^2 + 10x^3 + x^4 is (N(5,1), ..., N(5,5)). So the first
formula restates the g.f.

**Bargraphs.** A castle of width 5 is a bargraph of width 5, and its semiperimeter is the
width plus the number of blocks, 5 + c(1) + Sum_{i=2..5} max(0, c(i) - c(i-1)) = n + 6 for a
castle with n + 1 blocks. Hence a(n) = A271942(n+6, 5): A271942(s, w) counts the bargraphs of
semiperimeter s and width w.
