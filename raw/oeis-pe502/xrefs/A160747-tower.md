## The tower object (self-contained definition)

A **tower** of width w is a stack of unit-height blocks on a line of w cells: each block
is a horizontal segment of adjacent cells at some height, every block rests on the floor or
on the block directly below it (nothing floats or overhangs), and two blocks in the same
row are separated by at least one empty cell.  Equivalently a tower is a column-height
vector (c_1,...,c_w) of nonnegative integers; its number of blocks is the number of
maximal horizontal runs, c_1 + Sum_{i=2..w} max(0, c_i - c_{i-1}).

## Draft additions

### Comment (append)

> a(n) is also the number of towers of width 5 with n blocks, where a tower is a stack of
> unit-height blocks on a line of cells such that every block rests on the floor or on the
> block below, and two blocks in the same row are separated by at least one empty cell.
> Equivalently, it is the number of 5-tuples of nonnegative integers (c1,...,c5) with
> c1 + max(0,c2-c1) + max(0,c3-c2) + max(0,c4-c3) + max(0,c5-c4) = n.

### Formula (append)

> a(n) = Sum_{k=1..5} N(5,k) * C(n + 5 - k, 4), where (N(5,1),...,N(5,5)) =
> (1, 10, 20, 10, 1) are the Narayana numbers (A001263); the generating function is
> (1 + 10x + 20x^2 + 10x^3 + x^4)/(1-x)^5, i.e. the Narayana(5) polynomial over (1-x)^5.
