## The tower object (self-contained definition)

A **tower** of width w is a stack of unit-height blocks on a line of w cells: each block
is a horizontal segment of adjacent cells at some height, every block rests on the floor or
on the block directly below it (nothing floats or overhangs), and two blocks in the same
row are separated by at least one empty cell.  Equivalently a tower is a column-height
vector (c_1,...,c_w) of nonnegative integers; its number of blocks is the number of
maximal horizontal runs, c_1 + Sum_{i=2..w} max(0, c_i - c_{i-1}).

## What is already there (do NOT duplicate)

- Formula already present: g.f. `(1+3x+x^2)/(1-x)^3`, `a(n) = 5*A000217(n) + 1`, and
  — notably — "Narayana transform (A001263) of [1, 5, 0, 0, 0, ...]" (Adamson 2007).
- So the algebraic link is already on the books; what is **new** here is the concrete
  geometric/heap interpretation, and the observation that the g.f. numerator
  `1+3x+x^2` is exactly the Narayana(3) polynomial.

## Draft additions

### Comment (append)

> a(n) is also the number of towers of width 3 with n blocks, where a tower is a stack of
> unit-height blocks on a line of cells such that every block rests on the floor or on the
> block below, and two blocks in the same row are separated by at least one empty cell.
> Equivalently, it is the number of triples of nonnegative integers (c1, c2, c3) with
> c1 + max(0, c2-c1) + max(0, c3-c2) = n.

### Formula (append; makes the Narayana link explicit)

> a(n) = Sum_{k=1..3} N(3,k) * C(n + 3 - k, 2), where (N(3,1), N(3,2), N(3,3)) =
> (1, 3, 1) are the Narayana numbers (A001263); equivalently the generating function is
> (1 + 3x + x^2)/(1-x)^3.
